Here's a fantastic clone originally from  Sunbelle Exotics that can turn solid red during the fall (I think I acquired a division from Wes?).    Vigor-wise, I'd rate this clone as average: it's not particularly fast growing, but it's not slow either.  It does produce divisions readily, and can form some nice specimens once fully established.   This plant comes from one of the eastern most locations for S. leucophylla, although I'm not sure if they were introduced there, or if they occur naturally.  all I can say for sure is that it has a subtle unique look compared to plants from other populations.    
  
This plant is also found very far South for S. leucophylla, and is subjected to warmer conditions in the wild in comparison to plants in Washington Co, AL for example.  Is there some horticultural advantage to growing plants from southern locations versus northern locations?  I always thought plants from the north are more cold tolerant and produce better spring pitchers, but that has yet to be proven.  S. leucophylla Franklin Co, FL clone B was the second pitcher for me to open this year (helmut's delight opened first).   
  
Breeding-wise, this plant is phenominal-this clone was the father plant used to make this: [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/914/leucophylla-red-clone-select-franklin](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/914/leucophylla-red-clone-select-franklin)

Hi Mike,  
Looks like your Franklin County B is doing very well. As far as we know, talking with a lot of long time cp swampers, this may be the southeastern most stand for leucos. The plants are indeed unique, and there are no other Sarracenia species growing in the site.  Not even psittacina.  When we were there,  the colors ranged from intense red, like B, through to more traditional colors, and some clones are huge. Manny visited the site last year and said the pines are overtaking them, and very few pitchers were up because they are being shaded out.  
We have crossed different clones from the site that are in our collection to each other, and the seedlings are coming along. We made different combos with them this spring as well, so we'll learn a lot as they grow up over the next several years.

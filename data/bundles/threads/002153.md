One of the weirdest and coolest purpureas: this clone produces regular purple pitchers but has a yellowish flower!  Now, how did this happen?  I'm starting to believe this was either a sexual mutation or an asexual mutation that shows up in the offspring as well.  I don't think it's the result of hybridization, although it's quite possible, since flavas may have existed close-by many centuries ago, who knows!  
  
In any case, this isn't a pure yellow flower-you can see some red anthocyanins mixed in, so it really makes you wonder.

To clear up the confusion, the original plant died, but offspring were grown from seed. Out of these offspring, Christoph selected one to call "Phoenix". I am unsure, based on the description of the variety, whether or not any plant from this line, that displays the same characteristics, could be called "Phoenix", or if it must be a vegetative clone. However, Mikes plants are siblings of the "Phoenix" that was published, each one a separate seed grown individual. And the trait does breed true. I am slowly working on outbreeding to more vigorous plants from the same watershed, one of which is truly the largest purp I have ever seen. I would like a more vigorous plant with the same characteristics.

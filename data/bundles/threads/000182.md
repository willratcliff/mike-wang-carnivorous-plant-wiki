Below are pictures from my first attempt to grow Disas (a south african terrestrial orchid).  They did very well at first-they were grown in an unheated greenhouse in Sacramento, California.  However, as the summer approached with 116F temperatures, they definitely suffered.    
  
Fortunately, a year later, I moved back near the coast, so the summer months no longer kill them off.  However, we do get winter freezing, where the top layer of the medium is solid ice.  Some leaves get burned from the cold, which makes them produce smaller plants and flowers.  Fortunately, they survive and don't get killed from the cold.

#### **Mike Wang** (`@meizzwang`)
`post #8` · `id=3685` · `2013-09-18 19:44:36 UTC`

HI Wireman,  
  
Disas are like Cephalotus-make sure the soil temperature doesn't go past 80 or 85F during the hottest of days.  Bright morning light, and filtered afternoon light is ideal.  Cool nights (ie. 65F and below) allow you to grow these where the day temps. are 100F+.    
  
  They're also freeze-sensitive.  Go  30 F or below, and there will be permanent damage.  Interestingly enough, they are slightly frost tolerant.  I usually take them inside when I know it's going to freeze, and keep them in the garage for a few weeks until the cold passes.  Even when kept in very little light, they're barely growing at this stage, and it doesn't hurt them to be indoors, so long as it's unheated.

Eric: these are very much like carnivorous plants: they like nutrient poor soils, and can't be grown in regular soil. I have them growing  in pure sphagnum moss, and have to transplant them every year because the sphagnum breaks down, and they start to decline when in mushy, old soil.

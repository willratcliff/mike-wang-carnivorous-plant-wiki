I don't think the photos online do this subspecies justice, so here are some pics that do!   I took these photos years ago, lost them, and just found them!  Figured these would be worth adding to the photo-library.  
  
They're pretty easy to grow: I use 100% peat and feed them regularly during the grow season with maxsea 16-16-16, about 3/4 strength.  They are soil fertilized.  As soon as the flower bud is maybe a few days from opening, I stop fertilizing completely.  These probably can sit in water if you have taller pots, but I don't allow them to ever sit in water, as the roots rot easily for me.   Just like carnivorous plants, these need DI or reverse osmosis water for best results.   
  
I can't remember, but I think these flowers might be slightly fragrant!     Lilium pardalinum ssp. volmeri:

I grow these in 100% pure peat moss and don't let them sit in water.  they're in part shade, and should be in almost full shade if there is a heat wave, otherwise the leaves will turn purple and then burn.  These plants seem to prefer bright, indirect light.  feeding-wise, I give them maxsea 1X per month, and fertilize with about 1/5 strength.  These plants are slightly less sensitive to fertilizers than Sarracenia, so you can get away with a little bit of error here and there.  That's all I know for now, will keep you all posted once I learn more about these plants!

Keep them at first warm for 6-8 weeks. I usually do this in a plastic bag with life Sphagnum at about 20 C°. After 4 weeks they might start to germinate their micro bulb.   
When it is developed a bit keep it cool below 5 C° for at least 4 more weeks or leave them outside if it is winter.    
  
After it gets warm again it will sprout its first leaf.  
  
If you sow it in spring outside many won`t germinate or go dormant for extended periods. If you happen to get them in summer you can do it just outside. But they should never dry out.

I've tried many experiments on germinating these, and the only one that has worked so far is to leave them outside, cross your fingers, and hope for the best!  Once you have bulbs, they're pretty easy to grow and multiple vegetatively!    
  
  
Despite best efforts, this is what typically happens: a few seeds will sprout here and there.  Just be sure to not throw away your pots if nothing germinates, they can last a very long time if kept in the shade and kept damp.  I grow all of these in 100% peat moss, works very well once you have plants started!  
  
Here's a typical example of the type of success I get germinating these: maybe a few will pop.  In rare cases, all of them sprout, and it's happened several times!  Maybe you really do need to keep them warm for 6-8 weeks and then cool them down, I really can't confirm this.  All I know is that in one case, the seeds were sown in late November, when air temperatures didn't exceed 60F, and then two months later, everything germinated! This species belongs to hypogeous germination type. Means first it grows a small bulb mostly underground and then the first true leaf emerges.

Mostly in spring but every variation is possible.  

  
With this type it is most easy to observe the action if you place it in some plastic bag together with life Sphagnum.  
Within the mentioned warm period yo will see small white thread grow out of the seeds and form a small bulb at the end.  
Sometimes just beside the seed.  
Then I disperse everything on my hydroponic systems or in pots or out in the garden: cover it with some substrate and let nature take its course.

got the darn thing to flower (finally!) for the first time.  So far, besides getting them to germinate, Lilium pardalinum volmeri seems relatively easy to cultivate, but they do need part shade.  They're less sensitive to warm soil temperatures than darlingtonia, but it's still better to keep the soil cool and hydrated at all times.  Waterlogging the soil is almost certain death, so be sure not to sit them in water for long periods of time!

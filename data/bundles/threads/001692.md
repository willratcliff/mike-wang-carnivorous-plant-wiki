S. rosea is generally very difficult to find in the wild, unless you're in Liberty Co, FL where populations are huge and plants can be found everywhere.  In general, I know of only one other site where S. rosea is equally plentiful, and it's on private property.  
  
At most sites, S. rosea is patchy, and you'll find a few plants here and there.  For the most part, they're rather uncommon and you're lucky to find one or two.  They seem to inhabit the edges of permanently flooded areas, edges of rivers/creeks,  or in the hilly habitats, they can be found in boggy seeps. They can inhabit the wettest parts of a bog, so long as the water is moving.  If the water is stagnant, you'll likely find them on the edges of the flooded areas.

about 15 years ago or so, I acquired many different clones of montana from Transylvania Co, NC.  They were genetically diverse and had lots of great looking individuals in the mix, here's a pic of my mother plants from about 10 years ago:  

![](../../images/c6/c621c43cf268fead87b8fc8f30c7b1f060b790e6c0a7a3eecac766c672afc680.jpg)

  The plants thrived for years!  
  
In 2014, I moved to a new location that had tons of woodchips everywhere in the landscape.  That winter, it rained really hard and we had  an enormous fungal bloom with clouds of spores erupted from the woodchips.  For the first time in my life, I lost an enormous amount of plants: all of the plants in the collection got infected with some sort of fungal disease!  All the other species fought the fungal disease off, but that winter, I lost around 70% of my purpurea collection!!!  
  
  
 Fortunately, I backed up a few purpurea populations via seeds and got them going again.  I didn't have too many montana crosses in storage, but I did have HXC, so I grew it out!  Most of the individuals from this batch weren't great looking, but one individual was 3X the size of the others, and had some great shape to it!    
  
That's what you're looking at right here, S. montana HxC 'best clone' Transylvania Co, NC.  We'll have to wait for the fall traps to see this clone in its prime, but right now, it's starting to look nice!  Pic taken 8/6/21:

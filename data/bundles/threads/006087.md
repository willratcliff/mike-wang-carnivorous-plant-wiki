Occasionally,  I go up to my wife and tell her "I have a confession to make..." and then she gets all serious and is thinking "that dude better have not cheated on me" or "where's my baseball bat for when he tells me he was seeing another girl?"  Of course, after being forthcoming that I have a confession to make, it'll end with something not so intense like "I got roped into buying some strawberry plants even though you told me not to, I hope you still love me....."  
  
What does this have to do with anything?  Well ladies and Gentlemen, I have a confession to make to you all: see all of these amazing anthocyanin free S. purpurea ssp. venosas? [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/826/purpurea-ssp-venosa-var-pallidiflora](https://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/826/purpurea-ssp-venosa-var-pallidiflora)  
  
This is hard to announce publicly, but I will say it.....I KILLED EVERY LAST ONE OF THEM!  I even got replacements and killed those!    
  
So what's the good news?  The genes haven't been lost and after growing them for a decade, there was a realization that these wouldn't stand the test of time, so something had to be done to improve this variant.   I outcrossed them before they rotted out to a vigorous, disease resistant venosa with the hopes of creating an improved version of AF venosa!  The other set of good news is, as of today, I do have AF venosa back in the collection as a result of this now 12 year project (8 of those years the seeds sat in the refrigerator, shouldn't have taken that long).  Long story short, I should have a new thread on AF venosa in the near future, and I should be able to offer them once they're ready (1-2 years from now).  
  
In the meantime, here are some pics of the AF heterozygous venosa, which have the AF genes but are heterozygotes so they don't display the AF mutation.  They're pretty dang cool looking even though they're not all green!  This one was partially shaded/covered by some S. flava var. rugelii Brooks Co, GA population nearby, and it maintained a nice color contrast even though the rest of the batch, which gets full sun, are looking solid red to totally brown/dead right now!  The flava plants protected this trap from lots of freezing and intense light!  This particular clone has a really nice shape to it too!

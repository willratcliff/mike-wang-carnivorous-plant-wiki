I've been trying to inspire a lot of people to create areolata crosses   
(S. alata x leucophylla) after seeing these plants in the wild: [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/885/alata-stunning-leucophylla-hybrids-jackson](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/885/alata-stunning-leucophylla-hybrids-jackson)  but maybe it's going to take a little bit longer than expected to producing anything jaw dropping.  For one, when you cross the dark red alatas with leucophylla, preliminary results suggest you don't get much "white action" in the seedlings.  On the other hand, maybe I'm just jumping the gun too early because the results observed are from seedlings, which could look completely different at maturity.  
  
What happens when you cross an alba with a dark, almost black alata?  I think the results will be completely different from one cross to the next, but I did select an alba that has blindingly white dominant genes on purpose: I want them dang characteristics to show up in the first generation cross!  
  
Did it work?  Sorta maybe.....well not really!  Theory versus reality is oftentimes very different, but again, we'll see what happens.  Here's a promising seedling in a batch of S. leucophylla var. alba 'Rod's favorite x alata 'dark' Stone Co, MS, pics taken 12/8/19:  

![](../../images/d9/d9fa77eb155e90bc219ccb32bc37c2a8e327aed761c458d6ccc231e56aab0559.jpg)

  
  

![](../../images/f4/f4c1ab50a19d6557ceddcbaaed76f78197073d0a6b887ad8c3f99ff3900a2897.jpg)

  
  
While it does look kinda promising at this stage, it could be complete crap, still too early to know.  The elusive ability for these plants to color up oftentimes can be overlooked by growers, or it might just be so hard to color up that it's not worth the time and effort.  When breeding, I'm hoping to get plants that color up easily under a wide variety of conditions, but that might take several generations of selective breeding:  

![](../../images/dc/dca8570e9d3c8da44eccccbcb6457e5ffeca0ea99799df287968d9d4c11b41d4.jpg)

  
  
Here's a different seedling from the same batch that looks interesting.  I bet the best looking seedling in this batch isn't yet "showing itself," but whatever the case may be, these primary results are definitely interesting for breeders to see:

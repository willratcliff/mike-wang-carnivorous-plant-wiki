This clone is originally from Bob Hanrahan's property in Southern Baldwin Co, AL.  It can produce pitchers up to a meter tall in good years, and is consistently shapely and nice.  The "lip" can turn red, but I think most S. leucophyllas can do this as well.  This clone produces an abundance of divisions over time, and I have about 3/4 of a tub of just this clone.  HOwever, keep in mind this plant has been in the collection for more than a decade:

I got this plant from Mike this year with a big fat rhizome on it. No spring pitchers this season - it sat around all summer looking more like an iris plant than a Sarracenia and then BOOM! FALL PITCHERS.   
These plants respond really well to heat - we had four days of 90-100 degree temps here a couple weeks ago and you could practically watch the pitchers grow they were expanding so fast. They slowed down a lot when the weather returned to a more normal but still warm high of 80-85. This clone has some serious chunk factor to it - very solid pitchers, and the red lip really sets it off I think.

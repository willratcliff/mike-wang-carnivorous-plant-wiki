After an especially early cold snap around the New Year, this March/April was particularly brutal in the Midwest this year, which I think has to be the worst timing for cold damp (snow/rain) weather.  My thought is that the rhizomes are starting to break dormancy and swell and the sustained cold/wet/dreary conditions leave them especially prone to rot.      
  
It was my first season here with a larger potted collection and I fretted about how to overwinter the plants.  I ultimately decided on the using the small greenhouse my home came with.  Initially I was worried about a) the costs to heat and b) temperature swings from warm sunny days.  To try and limit heating costs, I covered the greenhouse in an additional layer of plastic and filled a couple 30-50 gallon barrels with water for a heatsink.  I also ran a crappy "milkhouse" heater to keep temperatures from going below the upper 20s.   Overall, it looks like it worked fine, probably cost me $100-$200 in additional heating costs still.  
  
I'm looking back at my temperature sensor I used to monitor conditions ([link1](https://www.mytaglist.com/eth/tempStatsMulti.html?298e7bf4-ffe0-41cd-be3c-0a95cf8ab44e&temp&F), [link2](https://www.mytaglist.com/eth/tempStatsMulti.html?298e7bf4-ffe0-41cd-be3c-0a95cf8ab44e&temp&F)) and I still feel I'm playing with fire although I had only one plant almost lost (a leuco currently in a sphagnum bath after major surgery). The few nights of temps in the upper 20s doesn't worry me but the consistent high temps/high humidity when the winter sun comes out can't be good to limit pathogen growth.  I think I'm going to bleach the greenhouse before the plants return this winter as a precaution.  
  
Anyways, I'm finally have most of the plants out of the greenhouse and they are slowly maturing as they acclimatize.  Sorry for crappy iPhone shots since I've been too lazy to bust out the nice camera.

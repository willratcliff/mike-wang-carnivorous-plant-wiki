[Sept 23, 2020 13:24:14 GMT -5](/post/44511/thread) [meizzwang](/user/11) said:

I think this is a selfing from the cultivar S. 'Melissa Mazur' as that's the only original veinless rosea I've ever heard of, but it could have come from a different plant, not sure. 

Please rather don't assume the plant shown above is an offspring of 'Melissa Mazur' or from Bay Co.. Christian K. does not give a location and just states on his price list that his plants look similar to 'Melissa Mazur'. There are at least two independent accessions of veinless rosea being cultivated. Both collected in western FL: One from Bay Co., the other from Walton Co..   
Phil W. selected the cultivar ['Melissa Mazur'](http://rziemer.cpphotofinder.com/rziemer/zphotos/Sarracenia%20Melissa%20Mazur.pdf) among his seedlings and published the name in the CPS-Journal 2004. This cultivar should be propagated by division only. Unfortunately, there was some confusion about where this one is from, because he wrote it was from Walton Co. in the original publication. Later Carl M. corrected that this should have been Bay Co., FL.   
The 'veinless' rosea from Walton Co. was later named as another cultivar ['Candlewax'](https://legacy.carnivorousplants.org/cpn/Species/v39n2p63.html) by Nick H. as shown in the Meadowview 2009 Catalog (see also CPN 2010 and the discussions in the [ICPS-Forum 2008](https://icps.proboards.com/thread/1899/sarracenia-candlewax-never-before)). I don't know whether the best clone was selected here or any veinless rosea is meant.   
Anyway, I'm not sure if those cultivars are different enough to warrant two cultivar names. My impression is that this is a result of claiming priorities. Things would be much more clear if Phil W. had named any veinless rosea as 'Melissa Mazur' without location, but he chose to name the best looking clone. I grew some veinless rosea from seed back in 1996 and I still have two of the original clones. One is more pale green while the other has a deeper orange/pink flush of the pitchers/petals. Both are completely veinless and I really like the colour of the young pitchers. Seed grown veinless plants differ somewhat of course, but growing conditions may be more important.

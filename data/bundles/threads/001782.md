Here's another site in Washington Co, AL that's similar to the previously posted site, but to the discerning eye, you can tell these plants overall look slightly different.  For one, plants at site #2 look like they have little to no alata influence, and more rubra wherryi back-crossing.  At the very first site, we did see one or two S. alatas and several S. rubra wherryis, whereas site #2 only contained rubra wherryis.  
  
We didn't see any jaw-dropping red forms anywhere in Washington County, but all things considered, this red clone is still pretty nice:  

![](../../images/8e/8ec221e9bcb5ade113786e415b23f32f812983b6026e4b83fb4ba8df1c74bce3.jpg)

![](../../images/d7/d7d4d583a52a538987a86b87dea431fd4d6f99618b818f72cd8f5862e6ec2cd3.jpg)

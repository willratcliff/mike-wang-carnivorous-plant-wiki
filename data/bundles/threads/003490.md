#### *calen*

Sorry I am suuuuuuper late replying on this haha. Very cool project Brooks and my fingers are crossed! Sorry I won't be able to help provide plants, at least this time. We are having a brutal winter and I don't have the indoor space to force big specimens.  
I do have some experience with forcing though, and can confirm that it's all about temperature and light (as you would expect). Last year I received pollen from a grower (we'll just call him Michael Wangson) when the plants of the same species I wanted to pollinate were juuust barely showing buds emerging from the growth point. Since pollen doesn't keep forever without special treatment, I put the plants in my seedling grow chamber which had an 18 hour photoperiod and temperatures between 70 and 80 degrees. I was absolutely flabbergasted by how fast both flowers and pitchers emerged from the plants, going from barely budding to blooming and pitchering in less than 2 weeks.  
Nothing too shocking about that - warm temperatures and extreme photoperiods make them leap out of dormancy - just proof of concept. This was a moderately tall plant, however - a very tall type like flava would have been much more difficult and would have required me to totally rebuild my grow chamber to accommodate it. Anyway, it was a fun one-off experiment and I am happy to say the seedlings resulting from the cross are now themselves happily growing in the chamber while we still have a frickin foot of snow outside 😊

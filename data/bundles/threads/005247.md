Hear ye, hear ye! MHH fall pitchers have arrived! This is definitely the time of year when this plant really comes into its own. The biggest lid so far is just over 3” wide and 2.5” long, sitting atop a pitcher less than 9” tall (By the way, I have some breeding projects in the pipeline to hopefully shorten the pitchers and exaggerate that ratio even further, but that’s at least a few years out…) It won’t be long until this plant looks like a clump of red and yellow mushrooms once again. Can’t wait!  

![](../../images/f2/f2a56cf6e4c39ff06fc398aa8f2ea6f6f221fc61db945751aedbae325deda45d.jpg)

  

![](../../images/de/de8f1da2dc3cc8ff64f31e633a1f4c56d01e1609b920abfb88342822a94e2861.jpg)

  
  
That lid angle is just stoooopid!! Check out all the white in the tube, btw! Super “stretchy” in both the front and back—you don’t often get that with leucophylla/moorei plants which skew towards a much whiter back than front. Psittacina crosses can do some weird stuff…  

![](../../images/2e/2e8a13d6ce33b2fced27b843ae8ebba04fc2892fdc2be8f7608160a80a5fcabd.jpg)

  
  
Here’s another shot of developing fenestrations—see how they stretch way down and then abruptly stop? Rob Co has made some hammerhead crosses that amplify this “white sock” super well. Very cool genetics in there!  

![](../../images/fc/fcbaa04982816c7cf2da9403bd3f12c3e0384fa0209168537adaf928a1f319db.jpg)

Here's an interesting species of concern from the deep south that grows with Sarracenia in the wild!    I acquired 2 clones from tissue culture, and they're surprisingly easy to grow!  These plants are kept in full sun and grown just like they're a Sarracenia, except they don't sit in water and they get soil fertilized 2X a month during the grow season.  
  
What's really strange is how they reproduce asexually: I guess in the late summer/fall, they send out these stolon like thingi-ma-bobbers, and then those swell up and become new bulbs/plantlets the next season!  I still haven't figured out how to germinate seeds: they supposedly are hypogeal and require warm temps to sprout?  That's what I've been told, but not sure how that all works.    
  
Despite the mysterious seed sprouting requirements, the plants are very easy to grow, but just like carnivorous plants, they can't tolerate high nutrient loads in the soil....but they do tolerate slightly higher concentrations than Sarracenia. I use 100% peat just like with everything else.

That's a good question, I have no clue when the "stolons" emerge.   I'll make some observations during the grow season and report back.  I guess the only way to find out without disturbing the bulbs is to peep out any underground growth that hits the side of the pot.

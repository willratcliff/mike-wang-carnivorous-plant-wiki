I just got back from a road trip with my homie Rob Co of The Pitcher Plant Project, and we had the opportunity to see cobra plants in the wild!  This was the very first time Rob has ever seen any carnivorous plants in the wild, and it was exciting to see his reactions to the various places we visited.  
  
I had visited this exact red darlingtonia site last year, and they are consistently red year after year after year.  There are also green plants at this site, which indicates the red is a genetic factor.  While I have seen red plants at other sites, I've never seen any other site that contains such a dense population of pure reds.  Indeed, this is quite a treat to visit, and it definitely required some off-road vehicles to reach.    
  
  
Here's D. californica 'RED' at the Alpine Farms in del Norte Co, CA.  It's nick-named alpine farms because a huge pot grow was found just above this seep many years ago.  While we were driving up the road, there were people loading their guns, probably hunting some animals, but you never know:

It's all good Gentlemen, Hcarlton: POW!  Sending welcoming and warm vibes your way!  Cocles: POW!  Sending welcoming and warm vibes your way!  
  
So back to the topic....well, sorta: back in 2007, I did stumble upon an active marijuana patch in Darlingtonia country.  What I can tell you is that the second I ran into it, my heart started pounding.   It's pretty scary because you don't know how serious or desperate these people are, and it's very remote country, so things can go south very quickly especially if you're just a plant nerd and don't care about the stuff (eg. I wasn't carrying firearms or protection at the time).  This site is now dubbed "grower's site."   The plants were growing hydroponically: they were planted in pure gravel on the side of a stream, and a hose from upstream slowly trickled water into the root zone.  Much like some wild darlingtonia I've seen, these plants were infested with thrips and powdery mildew.  This is what I saw about 20 feet away from the darlingtonia patch.  Don't worry, the darlingtonia are still there as of today, thriving and there is no longer any weed patches here:   

![](../../images/d8/d8e3f65231a5d48eb4276464fe32d3e9530bdc742d6c947526aa49b37eee8207.jpg)

  
  
Even though these dangers no longer exist, it was a wakeup call and I now carry protection everywhere I go.  Today, the main threats are bears, mountain lions, challenging terrain, and less likely but it happens, desperate squatters (I ran into a camp in the middle of nowhere during the pandemic).

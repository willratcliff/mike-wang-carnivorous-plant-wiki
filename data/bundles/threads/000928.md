While S. leucophylla typically inhabits bogs, I didn't know nor realize they also grow in swamps!  This unique habitat is almost like a gigantic, shallow pond with a creek running through it.  You could see a current going in one direct.  It was almost like an oversized river filled with junk in it.  This is the sort of place where if you fall in, you won't get out because the mud is so deep!  
  
  Trees and tufts of grass grow in the swamp, creating islands where debris gets caught in the plants' roots.  AS dead material accumulates at the base of these trees and tufts of grass, Sphagnum moss starts to grow, which creates ideal conditions for S. leucophylla to colonize.  It almost reminded me of a hydroponics system.   
  
The white trumpet pitcher plants found growing under these conditions didn't seem as robust as the ones in the boggy fields, but they were certainly very tall.  Many were more than a meter in height, although this might also be caused by lack of exposure to full sun.  What was interesting is to see fish swimming in the pools right by the Sarracenias...I would have never guessed this habitat would work for the species.  Surely, S. minor var. okefenokeensis grows in swamps, but S. leucophyllas always seemed to be a bog plant to me.

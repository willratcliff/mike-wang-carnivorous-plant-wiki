So you thought I was out of photos by now, eh?  Not even close!!!  
  
S. rosea is one of those difficult plants to photograph in the wild because just like S. psittacina, they're typically hidden in tall grass.  I probably walked by a lot of them and didn't even know it!  I was always under the impression that they were plentiful in the wild, but we rarely saw them at the many sites we visited.  Perhaps there are other hot spots that have more of them, or they're so hidden by the grass that we few out 4,000 miles just to walk right past them!  
  
  
  
The first few shots are of S. rosea in Baldwin Co, AL.  In each population, there is a general "look" to most of the plants, with slight differences.  From one population to the next, they overall seem to have a different general "look."  In my opinion, these baldwin Co, AL plants look more "venosa-ey" to me when compared to the Liberty Co, FL plants:

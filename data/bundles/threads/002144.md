Okay, so what do you get when you cross 2 really nice S. flava var. cuprea clones with each other?  Mostly, you get outstanding cupreas, a few flava var. flavas, and then 1 in a few hundred, you can get a solid red plant!  Now, if we saw this plant in the wild, it'd be called a S. flava var. atropurpurea, but since we know both parents were cupreas, does that mean it's not an atropurpurea?  I also surmise that both mother clones are originally from the Carolinas.  
  
S. flava var. cuprea 'best clone' originated from California Carnivores, and I think....I repeat, I THINK this clone originally came from the late Bob Hanrahan.  Peter D'Amato did say when S. flava var. cuprea "best clone" (this was labeled S. flava 'red form' when I first acquired it from California Carnivores in the late 90's)  was seen growing "in the field" (maybe at Bob's place?)it was solid red.  In cultivation, it's always been a stunning cuprea for me, never solid red!  However, after breeding with this plant, I completely believe Peter's statement.  
  
Interestingly enough, the "body" of S. flava var. cuprea 'Lois' can also turn reddish under certain environmental conditions, so it only makes sense that this solid red plant showed up.  I'm beginning to believe that some of the Carolina atropurpureas you find in the wild came from cupreas and vice versa.  
  
These traps pictured below just opened and are still darkening up, so they're not at their fullest color potential.  Anyhow, here's S. flava var. "atropurpurea" (Lois x best clone), photos taken 4/6/15:

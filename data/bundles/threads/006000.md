Well, technically speaking, the full name of this clone is S. flava var. ornata 'Lion's Mane' Walton Co, FL.  Just to make a correction on the location given in the cultivar description, there aren't any flavas in Broward County, FL, they don't exist that far south!  Maybe that's where Wes Bucker was living at the time?  Not sure how that got mixed up.    Here's a link to the cultivar description for reference:   [cpn.carnivorousplants.org/articles/CPNv51n2p109_123.pdf](https://cpn.carnivorousplants.org/articles/CPNv51n2p109_123.pdf)  
  
I actually remember Wes Buckner contacting me many years back via text messages and he showed me pictures of this clone with mutant flowers, much like what we see in S. leucophylla Tarnok!  The reason why this mutation is very significant is because it had only previously been found with leucophylla!  This is the second wild type "multi-sepal" mutatant ever discovered, and the only of its type found in flava!      
  
For those of you who are seasoned flava growers, you can see immediately that the trap appearance is a dead giveaway for the Walton Co, FL genotypes....well, it's not super obvious just yet from my photos, but check out the pics of the traps in the cultivar description.

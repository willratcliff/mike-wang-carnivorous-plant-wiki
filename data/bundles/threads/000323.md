Ha! I immediately noticed the change in the thread title and was like - wait - did I miss that the whole time?? Any back story you’ve become aware of that you can share with the group? Not a lot of leuco-related material at all from Mobile Co.  
Regarding pink, light diffusion has a big impact. Pink is one of the colors most enhanced by polycarbonate / poly film. I got a couple “pink” leuco seedlings from Jerry A that I was very excited about. The traps they then produced in the blazing August sun were completely normal with no pink. Then, the fall traps opened and cured during 10 days of cloudy weather we just had and - bam - pink colors showed again! I’ve also learned that many hybrids showing delicate, gorgeous pink subtleties in a greenhouse will lack those features outdoors. Long story short, the pics of L42 with pink mouths are greenhouse grown traps - my guess is these colors are very rare in outdoor growing. Clearly pinks do appear outdoors as well, I think the pink genes just need to be “stronger” to be expressed in direct sun for some reason.

Good question.  I'm just glad I'm not a botanist, as this is a complicated matter when dealing with phenotypic variance, or differences in the physical appearance of a plant under various environmental conditions.  This clone does produce a solid white mouth:  

![](../../images/4e/4e5b652df2ecbb99e03adb3ae3614025c86096c929c473861c0908cbc55ed82b.jpg)

   
  
Since I personally believe the naming system is flawed, I've somewhat amended it for horticultural purposes.  I've chosen latin names for particular clones that depict that individual at its best genetic potential.     
  
The conventional naming scheme is rigid and doesn't allow for phenotypic variance, so this is why we have to ghetto rig it a bit to fit our purposes.  As an example, here's S. leucophylla var. alba 'original chameleon clone' Baldwin Co, AL.  These spring pitchers look nothing like an alba:  

![](../../images/2d/2d2994ba53f4a249e1cb52dea8a6d5d170d92ba2ecca681ab411cb08b22d8479.jpg)

  
But if I were to call this one a regular leucophylla, it would be misleading because it can produce some of the most whitest traps I've seen.  Check out this picture below, the two traps come from the exact same plant, this is the same clone, S. leucophylla var. alba 'original chameleon clone' Baldwin Co, AL!  

![](../../images/fe/febd79a03471c5ee6ee9a527473b41c5201d232e8bbdcfbfc005f0f3e89cbe92.jpg)

Everyone is CHASING that solid white alba that has no veins in it and is pure COTTON white!  Want to know the reality behind albas?  The environment plays an ENORMOUS role on whether it whites up all the way!  That said, there are clones that color up easier than others, so if you're breeding for albas, always select for that trait because they tend to be the ones that can get the whitest under a wider range of environmental conditions.    
  
Take this clone as an example, S. leucophylla var. alba 'peculiar shape,' I really don't know why I called it that but I guess I'm running out of names for these really white ones, so I guess let it be?  Amen?  This clone whitens up pretty darn well, at least outdoors here in Northern California.  In this particular seed batch, the offspring were all over the place: there's a reason you don't see a glut of albas, it's not easy to outcross clones and get nice results.  I'm outcrossing instead of selfing because of inbreeding depression: sure you can self the heck out of a clone and maybe land something impressive, but it'll be at the expense of lower vigor, increased disease susceptibility, and ultimately that won't stand the test of time once we get these super vigorous "hybrid" albas into circulation.   
  
  
  THis is the result of 3 generations of selective breeding, and I think once we hit generation 6 or 7, we might be able to lock in that alba trait and get them to color up even under less than ideal conditions, but for now, that's just a dream.  The reality is, these need to be left undisturbed, kept in full sun, grown well the previous year, and a whole lot of other details we're still figuring out in order to color up like this!  Wild types are incredible, but the original clones are extremely rare in both nature and in cultivation.  One when you start selective breeding for the alba trait, you can take albas to the next level!

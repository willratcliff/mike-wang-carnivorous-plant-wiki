Sadly, I lost my rosea to big mama a good 2 years ago to rot, but I was able to squeeze out some seeds before she got "aced."     The whole point of this big mama cross is to get the fattest of the fat genes displayed while at the same time, the hope was to get some well shaped traps.  I did some selecting from the seed batch, and the 3 that were kept were 2-3X the size of the rest of the plants, displayed incredible vigor, and it looks like 2 of the 3 have nice shapes.

Very sad that I lost the original big mama plant, but those tears have long since dried up now that the offspring seem to be even better!  The craziest fact about these seedlings is that they were sown on 11/16/13, which makes them a few weeks older than 2 years to this day!    
  
As you can see from the photos taken above, the traps were about average to normal sized in August.  I completely neglected and forgot about these plants until one day I walked by and all of the sudden there were these gigantic traps on them.  Where did they come from?  All of them were produced around late October/November, probably because of the heat wave/indian summer we experienced this year.

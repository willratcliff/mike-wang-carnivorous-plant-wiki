The population of S. purpurea venosa from Old Dock, Columbus Co, NC is in full bloom (April 2013)!  Every year, the plants get bigger and older, which means more flowers.  Last year, maybe 5 or 6 of these plants bloomed, but now almost every single plant has a flower on it.  It has taken many years to get them to this stage.  
  
Flower sizes differ, and a few plants have petals that are lighter than others, but for the most part, the flowers are relatively the same.  They are very fragrant during the day, especially when it is warm, but the fragrance goes away during the night.  The individuals from this population are quite genetically diverse, and there's a huge range of trap shapes and sizes.

some new photos, taken 7/21/13  After these plants bloomed, it took a while before they formed clusters of new pitchers.  The old pitchers from last year (fall 2012) lasted until about mid June.  If you leave the old pitchers on for too long, they compete with the new pitchers for space and light, so it's important to get rid of them.

`post #7` · `id=48615` · `2022-07-22 01:28:58 UTC`

I just put them in ziplock bags and place them in the refrigerator!  Thing is, with power outs, fluctuation in humidity with rain during the winter and moving the seeds, they get exposed to the elements and their shelf life is decreased. Even some packets that are stored in the same bag had zero viability, whereas others had at least some seeds sprout after many years.  no clue why some last longer than others when harvested, dried, and stored under identical conditions.

Some of you are probably wondering why this site is called Rattlesnake seep....well, the reason is the second I opened the door, I was THIS close to stepping on a rattlesnake, which was on the small side!  Fortunately, it was spooked and took off before I could get too close to it, but it was rattling its tail so I knew it felt threatened!    
  
It's totally out of focus, but you can see the stripes on the rattlesnake here:  

![](../../images/c9/c90798e94f60e4e099a6c54940b1f77a4474aceab4207d9a9478842e0844f8ec.jpg)

  
  
By the time I got a chance to get a clear picture, all you could see is the rattle!  

![](../../images/51/5143dabcfeb8c34b242d138ac382925d2b915de1e5dab9f7831ce117ec161e99.jpg)

  
  
Okay, so this is a site that I'll probably not ever go back to because when I visited, half of the road had fallen off a steep cliff that was definitely not survivable if you fell,and that portion of the road was uphill!  The other half of the road had been washed out by rain and had deep potholes.  It was extremely nerve wrecking but I took the risk: at the bottom of the hill, I gained some speed and went up the road without any incident!  Had the car lost momentum, that would have been an issue these are dirt roads with very little grip, so you can easy swerve off.  The forest service doesn't really maintain roads that are so deep in the mountains.   
  
Anyways, when I got there, the second scare with the rattlesnake was the icing on the cake, I promised myself I'd never go back there again!  That said, this is a really beautiful site: a fire had come in and cleared out this seep that used to be deep in the woods!  The plants were thriving!

Here's a plant that originally came from Bob Hanrahan's property, which I believe is in Baldwin Co, AL (anybody can confirm?).  Plants from this population under northern california outdoor conditions tend to produce a batch of very small pitchers in the spring and summer, followed by a huge flush of large pitchers in the fall.  I have a few different clones from this site, and they all behave in this manner.  Other clones, on the other hand, produce decent sized summer pitchers and large fall pitchers.  
  
Plants from this population are worth the wait-they're very shapely:

Thanks!  
  
Baldwin County covers a pretty large amount of land, and Bob's property is from the "southern" portion of the County.  This may partially explain why they don't grow very large pitchers for me during the spring and summer..perhaps they're more subtropical and prefer warmer temperatures to produce larger spring/summer traps.  Under warmer conditions, maybe they produce bigger summer pitchers.   
  
In addition, S. leucophylla from many of the northern locations have historically back-crossed with other species many generations back to the point that the only physical change is in the color (ie. pink and red tubes) and slightly different shapes in the traps.  There's definitely some sites where S. alata, S. flava, and S. rosea have crossed into S. leucophylla, and then those resulting plants crossed back into S. leucophylla to the point that the plant looks "pure."    I wonder if some of these individuals produce larger summer pitchers under cooler growing conditions compared to the more "pure" stands of S. leucophylla from say Bob's property?     
  
My S. leucophylla clone AJO1 typically produces large summer pitchers, and the hurricane creek whites can do this as well.  The hurricane creeks are from near Perdido (which is on the northern portion of Baldwin Co) originally before the site was destroyed.

dug up some more photos from the library.   Some of these are already online somewhere, but I figured it'd be nice to consolidate them to this post.   These were all from the same trip as photographed above.  
  
  
The photos below are all from Baldwin Co, AL from several different populations, all within a 10 mile radius.  This is a genetically diverse hotspot, and in any given population, you'll find a huge amount of forms and variations.    
  
Many of the sites that historically existed here, especially the ones you hear of which had gigantic fields of plants, are no longer around.   There are a few large sites, but most of them are very small patches that found a suitable spot to colonize.   Some of these sites were literally right across the street from where S. leucophylla Hurricane Creek white was discovered.  Unfortunately, that site is now a pine plantation, but there are scattered little pockets of plants in the surrounding area.

Ya, I'd rather get bit than over-heat, but most people wear pants and boots.  If you wear shorts and sneakers, be prepared to get cut up and bit, and constantly have soggy feet.  It's the price you pay for wearing shorts in the swamp.  It's hard to forget just how sore your legs get from all the thorny vines opening old scratch wounds on your legs...  One thing I won't ever forget is 3 weeks after getting back from the trip, the multitude of mosquito bites still itched!

S. flava var. rugelii from Ben Hill Co, GA typically is the first of the flavas to produce pitchers early in the spring.  These particular plants are about average in terms of how fast they grow, and they usually have a well defined red throat.  Traps are attractive and have nice symmetrical mouths.

Hi Rick,  
  
Clone A comes from Liberty Co, FL, which is technically in the same horticultural grow zone as all the flavas in GA.  All of these sites can be exposed to extreme cold (ie. during the polar vortex a few years ago, many of these sites were hit with 5F temps!).  Your climate will definitely test the true cold hardiness of any flava clone, but given that rugeliis in GA and Florida are under similar if not the same pressure to select for cold hardiness, I'm not sure you can say a rugelii from GA will tolerate your conditions better than a rugelii from Florida.   
  
In the wild, when you get cold snaps during the late winter or early spring,  the new emerging pitchers and flowers all get "jacked," but the plant eventually sends out new growth and they shake off the setback.  Nevertheless, I'd try out several rugelii clones from muliple locations to see if there truly are cold hardy rugeliis.  There's no way to test this under my conditions, but I wouldn't be surprised if some individuals, even from Florida, do better under your conditions than others.

Today, the vicinity of Cooks Bayou is either filled with residential housing, or was a former pine plantation and is being cleared for development.  In the past, it used to be a savanna filled with incredible forms of Sarracenia, including many red forms.  Plants from this site physically appear to be different from the Bay Co rubricorporas, although they may have similar genetics.  
  
 Once as common as a weed in Bay County, FLorida, these plants are near extinction within that area.  We can only hope that there are hidden populations on private property that are being preserved for the long run.    
  
   It's really interesting how the hood on this particular clone shown below folds upwards and has great symmetry, not sure I've seen this on other flavas before. (closest thing is this triangular lided rubricorpora: [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/1396/flava-var-rubricorpora-triangular-liberty](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/1396/flava-var-rubricorpora-triangular-liberty) ).  Each population of Sarracenia in the wild have unique phenotypes and genotypes, so even though S. flava var. ornata may be common in other parts of Florida, losing this population in the wild has a huge negative impact for the genetic diversity of the species.   
  
   On a more positive note, this clone below is really thriving in cultivation.  Here's S. flava var. ornata Cooks Bayou, Bay Co, FL, Photos taken 5/13/15:

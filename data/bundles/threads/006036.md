There are barely any pictures out there of this clone, so I thought I'd share some!  Apparently, there is some confusion out there as to whether or not this clone is a giant form, or if Dr. Konig distributed multiple clones, one of which was a giant and the other being a regular form.  
  
Other than knowing that this clone is rather rare, I had no other information about it when I purchased it.  The first season the plant took quite a bit of time recovering from shipping and it didn't grow much.  Fast forward a year later, It's super strong now and has incredible growth momentum: this plant is pushing out tons and tons of traps, some of which are relatively big!  Is this clone that's circulating in the US a giant clone?  No idea, it supposedly doesn't produce traps as big as hummer's giant but it for sure does produce an abundance of pitchers and forms quite a specimen.

After growing many different clones of Cephalotus, I'm actually a bit surprised by which ones ended up being named as cultivars for their ability to get dark (eg. eden black, OG black).  So far, in my limited experience, both aforementioned clones have not really colored up, while other clones not known for getting dark and also growing under identical conditions (same soil, water, light, nutrients, etc) are darkening up without trying!  Take Dr. Eberhard Konig as an example, not known for large traps (which it's now producing) and also not known for dark traps, which it is also producing!  
  
Keep in mind, it just started getting cold, so in a few weeks, these may color up way more!  Typically, cephalotus color up their best with full sun and very cool nights/luke cold days.  Photos taken 10/22/24:  

![](../../images/c0/c0b38aabaff5b14a76918053727ef9fdea534d90b7cfa1e67a455e9f2dfa4dc8.jpg)

  
This is the "dark side" of the plant...not the side that has the darkest traps, quite the opposite!  It's the side that doesn't get direct sunlight:  

![](../../images/49/49e2ad7751df852020d14424a7783f0cd92b177e923495b9a90c06915add962f.jpg)

  
  
This clone is quite spectacular and a joy to grow!  Easier to cultivate than some of the more finicky clones, although don't get me wrong, it's still pretty darn challenging to grow this one!

Here's S. leucophylla from the only known extant (still living) population in Georgia.  The plants are on private property, so the best way to see them is to grown em', since I've read it's unlikely the land owners will allow visitors unless they're government officials or scientists.    
  
  A lot of work has been done by Atlanta Botanical Gardens (ABG) to help save this population, including experiments with long term freezing of seeds and in vitro propagation of seeds.  In a previous post, we have seen that ABG has been very successful in establishing a bog filled with these plants.  I'm very impressed by how large the spring/summer pitchers can get, and wonder if this is genetic or environmental?  My plants are still too young, but time will tell...  
  
  
In the meantime, here are a few of my plants-I have 4 different individuals, but they're all very similar.  These genetics originally came from Barry Rice and Atlanta Botanical Gardens.  After going through many different hands and collections, they finally ended up in my pool of plants:

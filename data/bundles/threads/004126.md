Turns out, yellow flowered leucophyllas might be more common than we think...at least in Washington Co, AL.  This spring, many clones that haven't ever flowered finally produced a bloom for the first time and man, there's a lot of yellow flowered ones!  To recap. much of what we suspect regarding the origin of the yellow flower (at least from what has been observed in Washington Co, AL), it appears this is the result of hybridization with S. alata and then back-crossing with leuco again.  Why do we think this?  Well:  
1) the shape of the traps are tall and alata-esque.  
2) S. alata grows in the same habitat and readily interbreeds with S. leucophylla  
3) Production of large spring traps even under cooler growing conditions are characteristic of some  S. alata populations.  
  
  Interestingly, the most amazing, jaw-dropping leuco albas from this population tend to have yellow flowers.  When I say yellow, it means with some red pigments blushed into either the petals, sepals, or both.  I'm not sure if that was even the proper use of the term blush, but you probably get the point!  Anyways, on the other hand, S. leucophylla 'Schnell's ghost' seems to not have any blushing action in the petals.    
  
So what's so special about this original yellow flowered leuco alba from Washington Co, AL?  I'd say the traps are pretty spectacular, including the spring traps as seen below!  the fall pitchers are even whiter and more glorious, but we'll have to wait a few months to see that.

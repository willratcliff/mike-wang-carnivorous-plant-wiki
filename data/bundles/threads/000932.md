S. leucophylla 'pink' is an unusual variant, and there are many different clones out there.  Perhaps the most famous one is Phil Sheridan's "burgundy" which can be found in collections throughout the globe.  These pink plants in cultivation sometimes look like "regular" leucophyllas, and at other times, they are pink throughout the grow season.  They sort of remind me of S. flava var. rubricorpora: if you disturb the roots, divide the plant, or stress them out, they tend to turn green, but when left alone in full sun, they color up well.   
  
Another nice aspect of these pink clones is that they typically produce strong spring pitchers (at least under my conditions).  Some leucophylla clones produce phyllodia the entire season until the fall, when they produce gigantic traps!

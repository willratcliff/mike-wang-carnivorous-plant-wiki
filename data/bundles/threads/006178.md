Let me tell you this much about what was sent to me as Platanthera psycodes: aside from being pretty much impossible to acquire, I paid a very hefty price for these plants, knowing that after searching for them for over a decade, only once did I find it available.  I can now understand why: it takes FOREVER to produce side shoots!  There's one nursery in MIchigan (I think) that historically produced these, but I have been following up with them for years and they have never produced anymore plants for sale ever since mentioning it on their website.   
  
 When I first got them, they were pretty small, with maybe 3" roots and they appeared weak and on the decline, roots were just a little bit thicker than dental floss and I didn't think these had any chance.  After the first year of growth, I dug them up and was surprised at how much they had grown, these were surprisingly vigorous and they seem easy to grow so far!  I treated them just like any other platanthera: they're grown in 100% peat moss, only use reverse osmosis water, and fed very lightly with maxsea after the leaves have fully emerged in the spring.  By mid summer, the feeding stop.  How do I know this is the right thing to do?  Look at the pictures below, you be the judge if it's working!  They do not like full sun, these are part shade type plants!  Also, they do not like sitting in water, otherwise you'll get root rot, but the soil does have to be fully hydrated at all times.   I burned (over-fertilized) the plants the first season since they were still kinda weak and not able to take a lot of food, so you have to grow these like carnivorous plants (feed very lightly).      
  
  Every year, they only produce one leaf per plant, which is round, low to the ground, and nothing else emerges for the rest of the year!     After the second year of growth, the root system was getting pretty big on these plants and I thought it would bloom for sure, but again, not a single plant produced a flower during the third year (this past grow season), it was the same dang thing: a round leaf low to the ground and that's it for the season!    
  
Okay, so I dug them up a day or two ago and they are now 2-3X the size from last year, and 2 of the biggest plants finally produced new secondary growth points that I was able to separate!  It only took 3 years to get 3 divisions from my mother plants, LOL  yeah, it takes forever to reproduce these things.    Now if these don't bloom next year, I don't think they ever will, I mean look at the massive size of these things!  
  
The root system looks nothing like the white fringe orchid or the yellow fringe orchid, so even though I haven't seen these bloom, I feel like the ID could be correct.  we will see hopefully this next grow season!

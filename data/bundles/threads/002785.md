One of my favorite rubra ancestrals is from Taylor Co, GA.  Unlike some of the other rubra ancestral populations, It looks like the "precursor" to rubra ssp. rubra, although this clone can get as big as rubra gulfensis!  
  
This particular clone came from private property in Taylor Co, GA and was collected in the early 2000's.  At the time, the site was on a "boggy" riverbank where tree roots were holding up peat moss and supporting sphagnum.  P. primuliflora also grew there, but it was heavily shaded.  The plants were barely hanging in at the time.  
  
  Sadly, we went to visit the site in 2011, and there isn't a single plant left at the original site.  The trees grew so thick it was near impossible to get to the site.  Instead of part shade, it was deep, dark forested shade, and the river had changed course and was no longer keeping the entire area as waterlogged as before.  However, Sphagnum still grew at the site.  Fortunately, there are other sites that still are extant in Taylor Co, GA.  
  
I've had this exact clone in my collection since the early 2000's and it never did well for me...until this year!  Finally, after years of struggling, it finally got established and produced a very strong plant.  What's the secret?  Keep the soil wet, not waterlogged during the grow season.  In the wild, the water is constantly moving, and these plants were growing on the riverbanks where there's more oxygen.  You wouldn't ever find them growing submerged in water.  
  
During the spring, S. rubra ancestral has these little dark red flowers with the sweetest raspberry scent that can be smelled from a distance...it's a freakin delight!

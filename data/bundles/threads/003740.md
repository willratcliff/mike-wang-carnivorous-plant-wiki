Solid red flavas have multiple different pathways of origin, but one pathway that I've been exploring for the past decade or so is by crossing 2 different rubricorpora clones with each other.  In the past, I've made perhaps more than 10 different rubricorpora x rubricorpora crosses, and so far only 2 have yielded solid red plants.  For more information on the first cross, check out this post: [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/273/flava-var-atropurpurea-liberty-fl](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/273/flava-var-atropurpurea-liberty-fl)   
  
here's a different clone of a solid red flava using the same concept: 2 different rubricorporas were crossed together.  In this case, the parents to this all red plant are S. flava var. rubricorpora (L x best clone).  What's also notable is how the interior of the trap is greenish, but I can't remember if this fades away or not as the trap ages.  From this cross, I literally grew out over 200 plants, and 2 of them turned out solid red.  This suggests getting red plants from such a cross requires growing out a large number of individuals.  
  
  
   The traps just opened, so they haven't fully colored up yet, photos taken 4/24/17.  This clone is vigorous and performs well year after year:

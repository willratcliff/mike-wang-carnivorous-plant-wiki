Here's a really interesting naturally occurring hybrid from Onslow Co, NC!  It is believed that such plants are the ancestors of the atropurpureas that exist naturally in the wild: for more information on that hypothesis, check out this thread:https://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/4294/hypothesis-color-forms-hybrid-origin  
  
Knowing this, if I were to try and recreate an atropurpurea ex situ (well, I am trying), this clone is the very first on my list to utilize (well, I've utilized it this year so it's technically in the works) because it has the all red traits and water it down enough....you can get the flava shape....hypothetically!        Yes, it gets solid red from head to toe!  Nope, this isn't a naczii (rosea x flava) but a catesbaei (flava x purpurea ssp. venosa)  with location data, which is very rare in cultivation! Most catesbaeis offered in cultivation are man made seedlings.

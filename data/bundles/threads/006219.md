Here is a brand new discovery...I mean literally I found out about it a few days ago!  For those of you who are new to the scene or even for some seasoned growers who may not know, S. flava var. maxima (zero red pigments in the throat) is extremely rare coming from rugelii populations.    It's more common to find in flava var. flava populations in VA all the way to SC, but as soon as you hit up rugeliis, for whatever reason, it just doesn't show up as often in nature.  In fact, out of hundreds of thousands of rugelii plants that I've seen in the wild, I've only seen one or two maximas!  
  
So in case you haven't read the original thread on the rugeliis from Brooks Co, GA, these genetics in general were just recently introduced into cultivation in the past few years.  They originate from private property and there are 3 main populations on that property.  This relic patch of rugeliis was once common but is now extremely rare in GA: the brooks Co, GA population is thought to be the largest population left in the state of GA!  While I was a bit surprised to find a maxima in such a small sample of seedlings (I was given maybe 40 total?), it makes sense that this would show up from the Brooks Co, GA population, given it's size and genetic diversity.  
  
So why is this horticulturally significant?  Well, it looks like we just stumbled upon another super yellow individual! Maximas, just like anthocyanin free individuals, tend to get more yellow than the other variants, and this clone is no exception!

Some very interesting S. leucophyllas come from this region, and most of what I've seen is in Europe.  The European plants remind me of genotypes typically found in Santa Rosa County, FL: fat and stout.  Calhoun County seems to be isolated from other populations of Sarracenia, and some spectacular roseas and giant psittacinas have come from that area.  There have been reports of S. flavas in Calhoun Co, although I don't know of anyone who has seen them in recent years.  
  
  Unfortunately, there's a reason you don't see much in cultivation from Calhoun Co these days: most of the populations in this area have been destroyed.  Very few parcels of land haven't been farmed or converted into slash pine plantations within the vicinity, although there is one private preserve that I'm aware of.  However, there's no knowing what the status of these plants are today on that preserve.     
  
   This particular clone below reminds me a little bit of S. leucophylla Helmut's Delight: [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/835/leucophylla-helmuts-delight](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/835/leucophylla-helmuts-delight) , although these are definitely not the same.  This Calhoun Co, FL clone has some very nice bright white pigments, with a profusion of red veins.  Although the white doesn't seem to go too far down the trap, it is at least not diluted with red pigments.

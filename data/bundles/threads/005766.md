Here's a somewhat new registered cultivar that I just acquired very recently.  When it arrived, the name was super accurate: the traps were super duper big, bruh!  The pics in the cultivar description don't quite capture just how enorous the traps can get, it was a bit shocking!  This clone can easily be considered a GIANT anthocyanin free form!    
  
  After the first season of being grown outdoors here in Northern California, the plants didn't produce giant traps, but that was expected, since these needed some time to get established and get acclimated.  It also doesn't get quite warm enough in my microclimate to grow roseas to their fullest potential, but I still think a big, healthy plant that is fully established can size up here!    Can't wait until next fall, looking forward to updating this thread!

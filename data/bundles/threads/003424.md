Here's a standout leucophylla that has an excessive amount of white to the pitcher plus some pinkish pigments mixed in!  I bet the pink colors come from rubra wherryi or alata way down several generations ago.    
  
When the traps first open, the lips are pure white, but as they age, they become pink!  Also, as the pitcher ages, it seems as though some of the veins color up, so you have this bright white trap with a lightly contrasting pink venation going on.   
  
Is this a leuco var. alba?  Probably, but I'd like to give it another season in optimal conditions to confirm.  On a similar yet slightly different tangent, it's my opinion that albas aren't over-rated, but super white "regular" leucos are under-rated!    This plant is still relatively young and using some Philcula terminology, the plant had yet to reach "vegetative maturity."  
  
Some photos taken 10/10/16.  Notice in the first picture, the older pitcher to the left has pinkish lips, while the new trap to the right has pure white lips:

From what I understand, there are only two verified specimens of S. psittacina var. okefenokeensis f. luteoviridis: one from Gulf Co, FL and the other from Baldwin Co, AL.  It has been reported that the site where the plant from Gulf Co, FL came from  has been destroyed, but luckily, John Hummer had the foresight to preserve a division.    
  
  
 Fast-forward a few decades later, I've grown out some seedlings from John Hummer's original clone, which was used to describe the variety, and here they are!  Unlike the regular S. psittacina var. psittacina f. viridescens clone that I have, which is painfully slow to grow, these are actually quite vigorous and fast growing.  This plant is beyond rare...finding an AF psittacina is pretty hard, but finding the giant, antho-free plant is beyond impossible, until now:

Essentially, ALL S. psttacina var. okefenokeensis f. luteoviridis Gulf Co, FL clones in cultivation (and in existence) originated from this one plant, this is the exact type specimen that was used to describe the variant!  Over a decade ago, we went to visit the site this clone originated from, but it was long gone.  Bay Co and Gulf Co have been absolutely plowed till kingdom come and most of the previously wild land there was coverted to slash pine plantations.  I don't really want to know what that area looks like today, I'm guessing it's far more developed than when I saw it.  
  
Anyways, back to the main subject: One thing you'll notice....it's not giant!  Well, it's genetically a giant form, but you need exceptional care and conditions to get it to size up.  This is true for all giant clones of Sarracenia, generally speaking.  
  
Anyways, in case you wanted to know what the exact type specimen looks like, here it is!  I've had it for several years now and it's vigorous, not super rot prone (so far) like all of the others, and overall a good looking plant.  It has taken a long time but divisions are finally available, albeit they are small.    Pics taken 1/8/25:

salpinx-not positive what your question is, but here's some more details to clarify things.  Jerry has made a ton of purpurea crosses-he didn't keep track if it was Rosea x venosa, or Rosea x Rosea, etc.  In the end, out of the many different seed batches, some individuals turned out to be gigantic, and this is where Rob's oriignal "big mama" comes from.    
  
I think the real question is do we call any gigantic "rosea dominant" plant "big mama" or is it just one clone?  If we just label one clone big mama, what do we call the rest of them?  It's a pretty messy situation in my opinion, and I don't have an answer on that one.  In any case, all of these various rosea hybrid clones can produce obscenely gigantic pitchers.  Keep in mind that there are some pure S. rosea from Liberty Co, FL, for example, that can also get gigantic.

some of you by now have realized that it's sometimes tough to find a good clone of S. flava var. rugelii (outside of MW's collection of course 

![:P](//storage.proboards.com/forum/images/smiley/tongue.png) [not mirrored]

).  I've been working hard, selecting through hundreds of clones, and with some populations, you get a lot of winners, but with others, it's tough to find something that performs well year after year.  
  
One of the best performers so far has been S. flava var. rugelii clone A from Liberty Co, FL, it has withstood the test of time and been grown well in many different collections throughout the world.   
  
 Fortunately, I stumbled upon another clone from Liberty Co, FL that's very similar, but it looks just slightly different.  Originally, I completely ignored this plant, it was one among hundreds of different clones, but then one year, without any special care, it shot out a bunch of gigantic traps!  Ironically, it was only noticed in the winter when the big old dead tras were being removed!  It's surprising how sometimes, you have to go through so many plants to find a good one that performs well.  
  
After being identified as a potential candidate for propagation, this clone proved to be a great performer year after year!  Not only can it produce large traps consistently just like clone A, but it's also productive (ie. spits out lots of traps), has a very well defined red throat, and also has great, shapely traps!  Edge of the lid is lined with red.    
  
For breeders and collectors, this clone, just like S. flava var. rugelii clone A, is one of those backbone genetics that I believe will have a great impact on future generations of crosses.

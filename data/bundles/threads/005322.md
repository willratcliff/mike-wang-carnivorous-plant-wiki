Is this thing an alba?   Still not sure, so it hasn't been labeled such.  I'm probably spilling the beans on this clone a little pre-maturely, but even the unopen trap is pretty jaw dropping, so here it is!  
  
Just like the rest of the spectacular clones from washington Co, AL, we likely have  historic introgression with S. alata to thank for such crazy amounts of white on the trap.  The hypothesis is that hybridization with alata helps augment the white coloration, and from a few generations of back crossing, the increased white is maintained, while the hybrid shape of the trap slowly goes away.  That said, this trap still resembles some aspects of alata: fall traps develop about 2 weeks earlier than other populations, there's sometimes two sets of fall traps instead of just one (hybrid vigor), traps are taller and skinnier, and some of these clones end up with yellowish flowers!

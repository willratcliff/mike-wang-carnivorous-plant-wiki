I could have sworn I saw someone mention this clone on the forum a while back, if anyone has a link, feel free to add it to this thread!  
  
If you ever tried making an excellens yourself, you'll know it's a PITA: getting those flowers timed right is a bit challenging, even when you have a massive Mike Wang collection with thousands of options.  That said, it's not surprising that I've only heard of one report of a single site having S. Excellens in the wild.  first off, it's rare to have minor and leucophylla together, and  perhaps the weather has to be just right in the wild to get them to bloom at the same time.  Maybe there was one year when the leucophyllas bloomed unusually late?    Even that, what are the chances that an insect will pollinate the two species together?  turns out, the chances are very low but it did happen!   
  
 I've never seen this plant in the wild, so this is just a report from my source.  If you've ever seen Excellens in the wild, I'd love to hear about it!  
  
S. Excellens, county and state unknown, but it's known to be near the eastern most location where leucophyllas exist, so I think this is from either Franklin or liberty Co, FL:

Edit: this thread is for tuberous sundews, not just Drosera peltata.  
  
  
Checked the tag, and it says these plants are from 1996!  This clone is probably older than some of you on this forum!  
  
A little history: this clone originally came from Art Junier, and he obtained it from a tissue culture source.  I've had this plant forever now, and it's been completely neglected until now.  It was left in the same soil, un-cared for, and neglected for about 14 years, and then last year, I finally gave them some new substrate and paid some attention to them.  It grew among the Sarracenia and was water-logged all summer long every year for many years straight.  Still, it somehow survived all the abuse, probably because this is the Drosera capensis of the Southern Hemisphere.  
  
When Art grew it, they were giant plants that were almost unbelievable!  I've never been able to get these to grow big....they're never more than 6" (15cm) tall for me.  Maybe they'll be bigger this year since they've been babied.  These have historically been exposed to 116F (47C) during the summer and 27F(-3C) during the winter.  Were they damaged?  Honestly, I never really paid any attention to them, so I'm not sure, but they definitely survived.  
  
Someone else's weed is another's treasure, that's for sure!  Photos taken yesterday, 12/7/14:

Some new photos of tuberous droseras in the collection!  Sheesh, what a PITA to grow!  What I've found is that in my climate, these do best in morning sun with mid afternoon shade.  Reason being, one 70F+ day will make some of these go dormant, but not if they are in the shade and the tubers stay cool.  It's just enough light to bring out colors and keep them healthy while at the same time not enough to bake them on those strange, anomalous warm winter days that we've been having over the years.

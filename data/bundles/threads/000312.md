Most of the clones pictured below can turn solid red, and sometimes, by this time of the year, they are solid red.  However, most of the times, they start to form some darker pigments near the mouth at this time of the year.  During the fall and right before they go dormant, the plants typically turn solid dark red to dark purple.    
  
Some plants are completely green without a trace of red pigments in the spring (except in the veins and under the lid).  If you're growing a dark red or black variety of S. alata and it's completely green at first, this is normal.    
  
If you grow these under a greenhouse with twin wall polycarbonate glazing, then they typically can be solid red throughout the grow season.

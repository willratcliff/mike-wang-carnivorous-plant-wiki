For those of you who don't know, the late Phil Mann was a world famous carnivorous plant enthusiast/botanist/horticulturalist/biologist.  A full write up on him can be found here: [www.carnivorousplants.org/about/Awards/PhillipMann](https://www.carnivorousplants.org/about/Awards/PhillipMann)  
  
Anyways, what's the history behind the clone Cephalotus follicularis Phil Mann 1?  Well, I'm glad you asked, and even if you didn't, now you're probably wondering so here's the story: The seeds were imported via the ICPS I would have to guess around the early 2000's.  These seeds came from Phil Mann's personal cephalotus collection in Australia, which was reportedly quite diverse.  Nobody can say which plant and which location these seeds came from, that information wasn't given.    
  
The seeds were germinated in the US and propagated at the botanical conservatory at UC Davis.  Many of the resulting seedlings were then sold at the annual arboretum sales (the UCD botanical conservatory had an annual sale in conjunction with UCD's arboretum annual plant sale).  Out of the many seedlings that were sold via the annual sales, only one survived the test of time as far as I know, and that one clone is now known as C. follicularis UCD.  To the best of my knowledge, that clone also traces back to the late Phil Mann!      
  
Out of the seed batch from Phil Mann, 5 exceptional clones were kept, Phil Mann 1 (PM1) being one of them!  This clone produces very large traps and quite possibly is a giant, but that is yet to be seen.  When the traps are regular sized, their shape is like a standard, normal cephalotus, but once the traps get on the bigger side, they have more of a long shoe-like shape to them!  That is very similar to the Denbarker locality clones that are in cultivation, and it has me wondering if they're all from the same population, but I guess we won't know without hardcore scientists doing genetic studies to figure that out.

Here's one of the more sought after variants of psittacina!  It's pretty amazing that these plants exist in the wild floating in swamps, using their old pitchers to stay buoyant!  Originating from private property, these plants were "discovered" in 2014 and entered cultivation at that time.    
  
Of course, the elephant in the closet: should you keep your parrot pitcher plants submerged in water, or keep it on the drier side like leucophyllas, alatas, etc?  For whatever reason, I've NEVER been able to grow psittacinas waterlogged or with their feet wet for long periods of time, but of course, the wild has shown that it can be done, and in some climates, growers produce all of their plants sitting in water all year around with minimal rot issues!  I've seen many growers grow their psittacinas aquatically, and we're not talking about in ponds...literally, they have less than a gallon of water, and the psittacina is just floating in that stuff!  Again, put my plants in that situation and they'll rot in a week or two.  
  
Anyways, these floating giants aren't just intriguing because they're gigantic and found naturally in an unusual habitat, they're also super beautiful RED!    
  
S. psittacina var. okefenokeensis 'floating giant' Marion Co, GA, pics taken 6/6/22.  These are the late spring pitchers, which aren't showy compared to the fall.  I'll for sure keep this thread updated!

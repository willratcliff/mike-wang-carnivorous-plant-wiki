As many of you probably know, tons of standout clones from Franklin Co, FL are circulating in collections, and quite a bit of diversity originates from this area.  It's also somewhat well known that many GIANT leucophyllas came from here as well!  I have a very strong S. flava 'suspicion' that drummond's giant came from this area.  
  
So what does the rest of the population look like as a whole?  For years, I've neglected the mother population and crammed them into a community tray because space was limited.  Now that space is, for all intents and purposes, unlimited, a lot of the neglected plants were transplanted last winter and given room to spread out their feet!  well, that's not completely true, they're still pretty packed because maintaining this many plants takes a lot of effort, but at least they were given room to produce fall traps!

Beautiful leuco's! I wish I had room for more plants....  
  
Do you have problems with your leuco plants overeating in late summer / early fall? That's a major problem here in eastern North Carolina. My leuco's and moorei's attract a *ton* of moths, much more than they can handle. The pitcher develops a dead patch which spreads completely around, and then the top half of the pitcher dies. I'm lucky if a pitcher will last 2 weeks before this happens.  
  
Next year, I'm going to try the cotton ball plug routine: let the pitcher catch just a few bugs, then stick a bit of fluffed-out cotton ball in the mouth. That should still allow air flow in/out the pitcher but stop more bugs from falling in.

Yep, as an outdoor grower I have the same scenario. And, you're right, although it makes for very well provisioned plants for the next season, it really can be annoying. I get it with all species and here in New Zealand blue and green bottle flies are the ones that queue up to fill pitchers to overflowing. In my collection, rubra ssp gulfensis is one of the worst, if that's the right way to say it. They will chug down flies quick smart and within days they start to degrade. In the end, I just accept that its the natural circumstances and enjoy that aspect of outdoor growing.

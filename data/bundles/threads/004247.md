Leuco fever is in the air....I can smell it!  If you've ever smelled that fresh, fragrant aroma exuded by a fattie leucophylla trap (many of you have), then you know what I'm talking about...it makes Sarracenia fanatics go koo-koo!  Or how about a field of leucophyllas...or even a tray of them?  The sweet fragrance can be very strong.  
  
Nonsense aside, S. leucophylla 'Stretch' Covington Co, AL isn't particular flexible, but it does have them white pigments stretch pretty far down the petiole.  It's not solid white, but that's okay, the genes are unique and when I see this characteristic alone, I'm thinking, is this possibly a good recipe to create a plant with lots of white that stretches way down the trap?  Where the white fades away, can we get that to fill in if we do a few crosses with this plant?    
  
No idea, but that's some exciting stuff to ponder and to experiment with.  Aside from this "stretchy" characteristic, this plant is producing fall traps about 2-3 weeks before most other variants.  Perhaps this is a good candidate for more northern climates, where fall is much shorter and you don't always get sufficient time before the frost kicks in to develop nice fall traps.

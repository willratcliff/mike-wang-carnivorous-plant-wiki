Here's a random clone of S. leucophylla from Baldwin Co, AL that I'm starting to like a lot.  It's not particularly crazy white like the albas, but something about the veining and how the white goes down the trap a bit makes it attractive.  Some S. leucophylla var. alba plants can produce red veining in their traps and look like a regular leucophylla, but then they do their chameleon thing and turn solid white during the fall when mature and under optimal conditions.  Not this plant: it's just your every day S. leucophylla.  This one won't turn solid white in the throat.  Will the white travel further down the trap?  Hopefully, in time we'll see.

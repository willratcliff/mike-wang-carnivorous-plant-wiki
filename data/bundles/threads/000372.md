Here's a really nice clone of S. jonesii originally from California Carnivores.  It's a lot different from any other jonesii I've ever seen in the sense that the pitchers are very skinny and don't have that "bulbous" look to them.  The traps can also get a bit darker as they age in the fall.  
  
I've had this clone for more than a decade, and for many years, it would grow well during the summer, and then crash every winter due to rhizome rot.   During the spring, I would be left with maybe a little growth point that was salvaged, which would end up being a nice plant by the fall, and the repeat the rotting.  Moral of the story is, if you have S. jonesii, don't keep it sitting in water during the winter-ever since I figured that out, it hasn't ever rotted on me again!  
  
Everyone's grow environment will be different, but if you have the same problem, the aforementioned solution may work for you as well.

Jeremy,  
  
Here's a couple things you can do to minimize rotting during the winter.  First, all S. rubra subspecies seem to be prone to this since they often grow in tight clumps.  In our plants we've had more problems with S. rubra ssp. rubra rotting out.  First suggestion (and I realize lots of folks fall on both sides of what I'm about to tell you) is to cut off all of the leaves in the winter.  Here in Oregon we ususally do it right about now in January.  This does three things.  It opens up the crown to more air circulation, rinsing effect of rain, and allows more UV light, which inhibits fungus growth, get to the crown.  It also gets rid of most dead material that can be the host for Botrytis.  You'll also find that your new season growth looks better due to more light getting to newly forming leaves.  We actually clip all of our Sarracenia except for purpureas (they get a good trim) and psittacinas.  We also leave some hybrids of these two.  Everyone else get a haircut!  
  
The thing that helps comes from an old piece of advice from Adrian Slack in his books on cp.  He recommended to divide any in the rubra complex before they clump to any significant degree.  His reason was due to exactly the problem your having.  
  
Because you're in North Carolina, I'm making the assumption your plants are outside.  If they are in some kind of cold frame or basement, they would also need to be sprayed well with a fungicide.  
  
Keeping them not as wet as Mike suggested really helps too.  If you have them in some kind of cold frame you really don't want them in water.  If outdoors, just keep the water much lower, or only top-water in the winter.  You just have to make sure they don't dry out if you get a warm spell.  
  
Hope this helps.  
  
Jeff

That's really odd that you're getting rotting during active growth.  It seems you have some specific kind of pathogen, maybe some kind of Phytophthora root rot.  What does the rhizome look like when this happens?  Do you see fuzzy gray patches?  Black slime?  Every see any bugs on them when this happens?

It's a quanternary ammonium compound found in many common disinfectants.  It works well with tough plants like Sarracenia, but can be a bit hard on more delicate stuff like Pings and sundews.

This S. x moorei is famous for being able to produce gigantic white pitchers with a reddish throat.  Cedric did a great job growing this plant and photographing it to its fullest potential.  Usually plants growing in the wild look more outstanding than what we have in cultivation, but with this plant, it's just the opposite.      
  
A little history behind this plant as I understand it: the clone originated from Bud Wilkerson's property in Walton Co, FL ( and Brooks Garcia was the lucky man who originally obtained, named, and distributed the plant).  As of 2015, it's incredibly rare and has fetched some enormous sums of money both here in the states and in Europe due to the extreme demand and low availability.  It doesn't seem like a plant that readily produces a lot of divisions, but it's still hard to say.  
  
It took me years to finally get a piece of this plant, but I finally received a small, happy piece last winter.  During 2014, it grew a bit but didn't produce any pitchers worth photographing.  Finally, spring of 2015, the clone finally pushed out something worth seeing!  
  
These traps below aren't at their fullest potential yet....much like S. leucophylla, the prized flush of pitchers are produced in the fall.  This is still a pretty small rhizome, so it may be another year or two before I have this plant at its fullest potential.

It has taken many years to build up a good number of divisions of this clone.  I originally got my division from Wes Bucker, probably in the early 2000's?   He sent me a healthy but very small division.  This clone was very popular many years ago when a grower in France produced gigantic traps with this clone in a greenhouse!  Outdoors in Northern California, it's been a bit tough to get the red throat to color up, and the traps haven't sized up for me, but that's probably because this clone thrives in warmer temperatures/conditions.  My microclimate here is like growing in Washington State: our summers rarely go above 83F!         
  
Now that the traps have aged quite a bit,  the yellow tinge has become even more pronounced!  That yellow coloration shows up in some hybrids, I'll try to snap some pics when they get bigger.

There are probably several  venosa clones out there that can get near black under LED lights or greenhouse conditions:even from my own collection, I bet you could take a few of the clones that have never gotten dark out there in the past decade, place it under LED's or greenhouse conditions, and get some really dark looking plants!   That said, what about plants that can get really dark even outdoors without a fancy artificial suntan?  I have seen pictures floating around on social media, particularly from Jeremiah Harris, where a plant was near black, and that one to me looked like it's genetically dark (how do I make this suspicion?  just a gut guess, I don't really know for sure).  
  
Anyways, what I do know is that if you want to test the GENETIC potential of a dark venosa clone, grow it outdoors and see if anything does turn dark!  I made a TON of venosa crosses and one of them has yielded what appears to be a genetically dark clone!  How do I know?  
1) this is outdoors, not even in full sun  
2) under identical conditions, most other siblings aren't getting dark, suggesting a genetic component to this coloration  
  
  
There may be more than 1 dark individual in this seed batch, but so far, there's one that I'm keeping my eye on!  This is a cross between two genetically different red clones of S. purpurea ssp. venosa, I made around 8  different crosses and no other so far have produced anything like this!

Here's another clone of S. flava var. rubricorpora from the Liberty Co, FL population.  This is your typical rubricorpora in the sense that it produces maybe 2-3 pitchers per season, and then that's it!  The upside to that is those 2-3 pitchers can get really big.    
  
My clone N went though some rough times last year, so while it didn't get really big, it did produce some beautiful traps.  
  
S. flava var. rubricorpora clone N, Liberty Co, FL.  Photos taken 4/28/13.  These pitchers just opened a few days ago, and interestingly, it's pretty far behind in comparison to other clones from the same population.  The red color on the 'body' will become more intense in the next week or so:

#### **Mike Wang** (`@meizzwang`)
`post #4` · `id=2618` · `2013-05-01 04:09:17 UTC`

While in some cases, it seems to be a genetic trait, I think on this clone, it's likely  environmental.  That kink might straighten out as that trap gets more lignified (thicker), and in previous years,there were no kinks.  Here's a semi-crappy shot of the same clone taken in 2011...it's good enough to tell there's no kink: [www.flickr.com/photos/63000221@N05/5849967293/in/set-72157626875605651](http://www.flickr.com/photos/63000221@N05/5849967293/in/set-72157626875605651)   
  
On the other hand, with S. flava 'Burgundy,' that kink is consistently there year after year, which leads me to believe it's genetic in that case.  I haven't seen any correlation between color and kinks

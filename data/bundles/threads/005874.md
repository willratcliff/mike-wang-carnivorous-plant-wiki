Here's a small population of S. rubra wherryi that originates from private property in Baldwin Co, AL.  At the time of discovering the site, which contained wherryi, psittacina, and leucophyllas, we contacted the owners and asked them if it was okay to enter their property to see the Sarracenia.  They were super nice and had that very friendly southern hospitality, but were also a bit on edge because historically,  there were quite a few people who would tresspass and  harvest ALL of the pitchers from the field for the cut flower industry!  They were a bit surprised by our interest  because at the time, the illegal harvest of the white trumpet pitcher plant had died down to almost nothing: it was most rampant in the late 1990's/early 2000's when there were still an abundance of leucophyllas everywhere.  By the time we got to visit this site, it was one of the last remaining ones in the area, and only a fraction of what used to exist was still in tact.  The rest had been planted with slash pines!  
  
While the owners knew about the plants because of the historic poachers from the floral industry, they weren't aware of how to manage them or care for them.  After a lengthy, friendly talk, we ended up educating them on how to manage the site, the value of the plants, their rarity in nature, and they went from not really knowing much about them to excited to preserve them!   At the end of the conversation, after they realized we were more concerned about the preservation of their plants than they were,  they  gave us permission to enter the property and sustainably acquire some genetics, which is how this population got started!

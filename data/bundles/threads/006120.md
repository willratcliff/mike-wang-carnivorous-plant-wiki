So, here's a really genetically interesting flava that originates from private property in Brooks County, GA.  Normally, when we think about ornatas, we think of atropurpurea, cuprea, or rubricorpora back crossing with flava rugelii.  Many, many breeding experiments done by tons of different growers have yielded ornatas with using such parents.       Taking it a step further, In case you want to go down a rabbit hole on the origins of color forms which have given rise to ornatas, check out this thread: [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/4294/hypothesis-color-forms-hybrid-origin](https://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/4294/hypothesis-color-forms-hybrid-origin)  
  
Now, back to ornatas: we normally think they're just watered down atropurpurea, cuprea, or rubricorporas, right?  Well, this is a unique case that suggests, just like really bright albas and all color forms of Sarracenia, there are multiple "pathways" to get an ornata.  This requires we listen to Quado and **OPEN YOUR MIIIIIIIND**....  
   
  
Yeah, that was pretty spooky, right?   Well, I wouldn't be surprised if this is a common reaction to those realizing that a  wild S. x harperii (flava x minor) most likely back crossed with flava and yielded this ornata below!  So, in a nutshell, minors can create ornatas?  It appears so!!!  Sure, something else could have mixed in historically to create this ornata, but as far as I know, this site only contains flavas and minors and it's a pretty enormous property.

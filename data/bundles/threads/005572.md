Here's another accession from the same County (different site).  They all look very similar, but individuals from each population have subtle differences.  
  
From a grower perspective, I'm beginning to really like these genotypes from Maine!  For those of you who have grown purpurea ssp. purpureas long term, they can be a bit challenging to keep alive past the 10 year mark.  One of my old timer homies mentioned, from his experience, all of the purpurea ssp. purpureas he has seen in situ grow in very cool soil temperatures!  Peter D'Amato also mentioned to me about 20 years ago that he had some challenges with purp. purps whenever there were heatwaves.    
  
From my personal experience of growing genptypes from probably 30-40 different locations, where they come from has a big role in whether or not they'll rot out under my conditions.  I haven't seen a correlation between heat waves and rot, I've just seen entire populations rot away despite being extremely healthy prior to infections kicking in! That said, I'm intrigued by the idea of keeping the soil temperature cooler, that could possibly be the key to solving this challenge with some of the populations.  Even on cooler days, the soil temperature in full sun can really skyrocket!  
  
That said, so far, these genotypes from Maine don't go dormant very early like the others (which can cause rot, especially if it's way too early), and they seem like they might be less rot prone, I guess we'll see!    These are from York Co, ME, pics taken 8/30/22:

I don't think that purpureas in general are short lived, they can probably survive hundreds of years! Maybe quite a few genotypes perfer cooler soil temperatures?  No clue, more experimentation has to be done.

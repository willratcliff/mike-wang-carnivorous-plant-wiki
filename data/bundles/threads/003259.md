Another super crazy white clone!  Edit:this thread is to cover multiple different alba clones from Washington Co, AL...  I'm starting to think that these very bright white genes are recessive because a couple of the absolutely brightest white clones I have are not very vigorous (ie. Mike King's S. leucophylla var. alba MKL042).  Low vigor suggests the individuals are the result of selfing somewhere down the line and with selfing, you tend to see a lot more recessive genes show up in the phenotype.  To support this hypothesis, albas in the wild in general are relatively rare and uncommon even in large stands of S. leucophylla.

updated photos of S. leucophylla var. alba 'yellow flower, thick neck' Washington Co, AL.  As we have been discussing in other posts in this forum, the washington Co, AL population in general might be a good candidate for leuco lovers who live in more northern climates and have a shorter grow season. The first of the fall traps on S. l. var. alba Washington Co AL "Solid White" are about to open. Like many of its fellow Washingtonians this clone produces a steady series of good pitchers spring through summer, takes a small breath, then pumps out big fat fall traps around now. This is in marked contrast to many other leucos which have three distinct phases: spring pitchers, phyllodia (often with summer pitchers mixed in), and fall pitchers. None of my WA Co. plants produces significant phyllodia (hallelujah!), a trait that we think comes from alata/rubra introgression. These are about to pop, so will have more pics in a week or two.

The parent of many crosses, it's surprising there isn't a single thread on the forum about this clone, so I figured I'd start one!  I found a bunch of pictures on Bob's site: [cpphotofinder.com/sarracenia-wilkersons-red-3312.html](http://cpphotofinder.com/sarracenia-wilkersons-red-3312.html)  but it appears a few of those might be imposter clones.  
  
S. moorei 'Wilkerson's Red' originated from one of the wilkerson's property (probably Leah wilkerson's if I had to guess based on my cloudy memory) and Brooks Garcia acquired a division of the plant from the wild with permission.  I've had this clone for quite some time now, but I don't think I've ever gotten a division from it, so maybe that's why it's not really around.  However, lots of hybrids have been made with this clone.  
  
It hasn't looked all that great under my care until this year, probably because it hadn't been repotted until last year.  New soil and a full year of getting established in the pot before pushing out these spring pitchers definitely made a difference!     Photos taken 5/30/19:

There is precedent in the International Code of Nomenclature for Cultivated Plants recognizing un-published cultivar names if they have been in widespread use even if an older official published name is known.  By that standard (accepting a replacement name simply because it's widely used) then you can bet they would not have any problem giving official recognition to widely used cultivar names even if they were never formally published by an actual plant registry.

I think any International Code was dispensed with many decades ago when we reached about 100 un-published Venus flytrap names 😁

I do think it's time for a revision of the rules anyway.  As the rules are now, cultivar names can be validly published via a nursery's print catalogue, but not via online publication only.  This may have made sense in the early days of the internet, but nowadays many nurseries publish their catalogues exclusively online.  Ironically, it's much easier to use the internet to check whether a cultivar is already in use or not before applying it to a new selection.  Relying on printed descriptions only makes it rather challenging to know if a name has already been used unless it's popular enough to have a solid internet presence.  
  
  
  
At the end of the day, the thing that really bothers me is all the modern plant cultivars that have zero online evidence of how they were developed.  For example, today I was looking at Bergenia hybrids.  I wanted a selection that would be suitable for making tea from, but because I have no way of knowing which species were used to produce any given hybrid I can't easily pick one that will suit me needs.  On a related note of bad practices; I'm very bothered that so many nurseries list plant cultivars with a species name, but also refer to them as hybrids.  If they're hybrids they should not be listed as the species, and if they're the species they should not be listed as hybrids.

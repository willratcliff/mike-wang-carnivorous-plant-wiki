It's been a struggle to grow this plant.  Just as it produced an enormous rhizome with incredibly large traps, the whole thing rotted out on me!  Tiny side shoots were saved and now, about 3 years later, there's something to show!  
  
In general, there seems to be two genetic means of gigantic-ism in S. leucophylla (if that's a word): either via hybrid vigor, or inbred lines containing the genes for giant traps.  If I'm interpreting what I see correctly,  with hybrid vigor, you get an abundance of large traps and very fast growth, but with the inbred lines that have giant genes, you get slower growth overall (ie. dummond's giant), and  those plants tend to need very perfect conditions for many years straight for them to consistently perform.   Regardless of the type of genetics, true giants are very rare in both nature and in cultivation.   
  
S. leucophylla 'GIANT' Washington Co, AL is an incredibly vigorous clone, and as mentioned above, it's very susceptible to rot.  I now keep it growing in zero standing water, yet the peat is kept moist at all times and it loves it!  I suspect this plant is giant due to hybrid vigor and has rubra wherryi mixed into its genes, and this would also explain the tendency to rot under very waterlogged conditions.  However, it's just a guess, I'm not positive.

The Bay County, FL plants that I've grown so far tend to have shorter, squat plants with fat heads.  This is different than the liberty Co, FL plants, which can also have fat heads, but tend to have longer traps.  
  
Another interesting thing about the Bay Co, FL plants is that there tends to be a lot of "greener" plants, or plants with less pigments.  These aren't Antho free, but they could be a result of AF plants interbreeding with regular plants a generation or two ago.

Here's a new accession of S. psittacina Bay Co, FL that originated from a different population than from the ones photographed above.  The ones from above are from an extirpated site: those have long been turned into a slash pine plantation, which has been harvested and replanted several times by now, giving almost no chance of even stragglers to survive.  
  
It was a bit surprising seeing a somewhat large plant emerge from this batch of plants!  There are great shapes, colors, and sizes!  They don't look that great just yet, so I'll have to update this thread once they get bigger.  Also need to clean them up, but haven't had a chance to do that just yet:

Here's an un-named clone of S. leucophylla var. alba.  What's peculiar is that this plant is actually producing some very decent spring pitchers.  I'm growning thousands of different leucophylla clones, and have learned a few secrets to get these to produce good spring pitchers:  
1) leave on all the pitchers from last year until you get new growth, especially the ones that were filled with insects  
2) if you have a greenhouse, warm it up in the spring, leucophyllas will respond with huge, colorful pitchers  
  
  
This was one of the odd winters where we had one day of slight frost and nothing more than that!  I believe this unusually warm winter is why so many of the leucophyllas have produced some very nice spring traps.  Of course, genetics plays a huge role:hurricane creek whites tend to produce huge spring traps no matter what!  
  
Anyhow, here's the un-named clone of S. leucophylla var. alba Baldwin Co, AL.  Of course, the pitcher can get WAAAAAAY whiter during the fall.  Photos taken 4/1/15:

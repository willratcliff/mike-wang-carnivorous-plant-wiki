Here's a rather unique genotype of S. purpurea ssp. purpurea from Tolland Co, Connecticut.   I recently acquired it from my good friend Rob Co (who runs the pitcher plant project blog: [thepitcherplantproject.com/blog/](http://thepitcherplantproject.com/blog/) ), who graciously gave me one of his bigger plants!  
  
  I can't quite pinpoint it (just like S. montana is unique, but very difficult to describe), but there's definitely something unique to this plant.  The shape is similar to most purp purp's, but  the way the red pigments are distributed on the pitcher isn't seen in other clones.  This could be all environmental, but who knows.  
  
Also, many of my purp. purp's this year have gone dormant already (regardless of what location they are from).  Anyone else experiencing this?  They normally don't go dormant until late august.  
  
Anyhow, In the photo below, check out that pitcher to the right and the one furthest in the background, and notice how the red outlines the tip of the trap:

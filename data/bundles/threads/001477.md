For those of you who have had the chance to grow S. flava var. rubricorpora from seeds, you've probably experienced the natural phenomenon of S. flava var. rugelii and S. flava var. ornata showing up here and there from "pure" rubricorpora mother plants.  With some individuals, I've had the vast majority of the offspring turn  out to be rubricorporas (ie. clone L x best clone), whereas with other crosses, I've had a mix of different seedlings.  In other cases, I've had about 70% rubricorporas and 30% ornatas, and no rugelii's.  Every cross has yielded different results.    
  
What you end up with greatly depends on the specific parents that you use to produce the offspring.   In other words, just because I was able to get some ornatas from crossing 2 different rubricorporas together doesn't mean you'll get the same results, unless you use the exact same parents as I did.    There also seems to be several different "pathways" to producing the same plant.  For example, you can cross S. flava var. rugelii with rubricorpora and still get rubricorporas! That same cross with different individuals can result in ornatas!  Again, the results are highly  dependent upon the genetics of the parents.  You will also likely get rubricorporas from a rugelii that's selfed, so long as the rugelii mother plant has rubricorpora "hidden" in its genes.  Yes, it is confusing, but that's genetics for you!  
  
  
Anyhow, here's an exceptional S. flava var. ornata from Liberty Co, FL.  While a lot of ornatas, including this one, look like watered-down rubricorporas, they're just absolutely fantastic!  Notice the rubricorporas and flava rugeliis in the background which suggests the more "typical" result from crossing rubricorporas with one another:

You got that right MW. All these individual clones below are the result of germination of the same rubricorpora seed stock collected at the same time at Sumatra, Liberty Co. Including the last one....   
  
I don't wish to speculate on the historical lineage of respective flava varieties, but the variability within the variety 'rubricorpora' of Western Florida is pronounced and a range of offspring are thrown from within the populations (including the intimately aligned but starkly superficially different var. *rugelii*) as evidenced both in the field itself and in cultivated specimens that differ only insofar as where they are grown. All these specimens are grown in the same outdoor conditions (purely because of photo availability, 3 of the plants are also grown in a green house as seen here). The specimens siblings included below range right through from fully infused purple representations (which regulars will have seen enough of in my other posts on the atropurpurea/rubricorpora discussions) to those that have very little red in various elements of the pitchers or almost none at all.

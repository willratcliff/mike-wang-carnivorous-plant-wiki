I remember seeing a pic of a psittacina online probably 10-15 years ago from one of our European friends, and the clone they had were extremely bulbous!  This made me wonder, are there populations or specific counties that have bulbous-headed parrot pitcher plants?  
  
I don't think so, the bulbous characteristic is prevalent in many different populations, but perhaps they're not as common as the non-bulbous shaped plants.  Also, just like S. rosea, I think the bulbous trait can be very plastic: phenotypes within a single, proven bulbous clone may vary depending on the environment and the relative maturity of the pitcher.  However, I don't think this generalization holds true to all bulbous clones: there are probably some out there that are bulbous all the dang time!

`post #18` · `id=50791` · `2024-07-22 17:00:39 UTC`

After a rough grow season in 2023 for the psittacinas, they're really making a come back this season probably due to global warming!  It has been unseasonably warm overall, which is really what the psittacinas thrive in.  Last year, we had some cool nights dip in to the mid to low 40's at night, and that stunted and killed off quite a few plants (they can take much cooler temperatures when dormant, but when actively pushing out fall traps, such cold temperatures can be quite damaging).  
  
Normally for me at least, they don't look this way until about September, which is when they start to produce their best looking traps of the season.    Pics taken 7/16/24:

Ever since Hurricane creek white became the most popular and famous alba form of leucophylla, there's been a  craze for very white plants.  Problem is, these plants are difficult to get to color up, and they sometimes act like chameleons, producing only very bright white fall pitchers.  
  
 Just like hurricane creek white clone F, there are some leuco var. alba clones that can produce bright white spring and fall pitchers.  I guess the take home message is that like all color variants (ie. flava rubricorpora, or flava atropurpurea as an example), the plants need to acclimate for a good year under your environment before they perform well, and they need good conditions: full sun, some warm temperatures, and mild weather overall.  Keep all old pitchers on from last year's growth until you have 2-3 new spring pitchers.  
  
Here's S. leucophylla var. alba 'select clone' from Washington Co,AL.  This is genetically different from Mike King's MKL042 clone, but I believe this is also a yellow flowered variant!  Traps just opened, so they may turn whiter as they age.  Photos taken 4/24/16:

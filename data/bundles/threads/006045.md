Of course cephalotus OG black and Eden Black have made huge waves and are some of the most popular of the color forms of Cephalotus, but what most don't mention is that they're not necessarily the easiest to color up.  In my mind, a clone that is of high quality will color up relatively easily, all else equal.  As an example, grow all the dark clones you have in the collection under identical everything, the ones that color up the most consistently, even as reported by other growers under a wide range of conditions, are the ones I think are worthy of being a named cultivar.  It also helps if they're easy to grow, vigorous, and relatively less finicky than other clones.  
  
Here's a not very well known clone (I guess?), it certainly isn't "hyped out" and this clone is far superior, in my opinion, to any of the dark registered cultivars! It's vigorous, forms nice clumps relatively quickly, but most important, consistently colors up way easier than many of the other dark clones!  Even without cool nights and super bright days, this thing had already started to darken up, but I did wait a few days for it to get a little darker before photographing it.  It may still continue to color up, we will see!  
  
BTW, I'm starting to see a clone called "black purple" circulating, although I have no clue whether that is the same clone or if that's something entirely different!

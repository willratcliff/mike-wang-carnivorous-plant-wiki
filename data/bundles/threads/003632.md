You've hit the nail on the head, with the pigment issue. In general, pigment is a poor criteria in taxonomy. Pigment genes too easily change within a species, sometimes even on one plant. Even leaf shape is often an unreliable taxonomic guide; oaks and maples can show great diversity within a species.  
  
We've got "roseas" (actually purp. venosa burkii...heh heh!) from Baldwin, and they have a lot of variation on pitcher shape, flower color, etc. Some have hoods that fold like "montana", while others are wide open. When one compares these southern plants with, say mid Atlantic types, there seems to be greater range in morphology in the southern plants. Well, there used to be.  
  
Pretty flower, whatever it is!

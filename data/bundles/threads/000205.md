hi Mike!  
Yes, environmental conditions are important, but there are also other factors to consider.  
first of all:the health of the plant. An healthy plant has much color and strenght, obviously.  
The second factor is the time the plant has been in a pot with the same substrate and the conditions of this; in facts, plants non-repotted every year are much vigorous and healthy, so we can note much color and taller pitcher the next year! another important factor is the water temperature and its quality. a cold water is always good, plants will grow taller pitcher and roots will be white and healthy! I use large pot saucers in wood and polystyrene to ensure always fresh water! in addition to this the substrate will be always fresh so we don't need to repot every year. in cold temperatures fungi such as Pythium develop much less, peat deteriorates less rapidly! Another factor that i consider is the composition of the substrate: I use a multilayer substrate, on the bottom using only  one inch of sponge; after (going up) i use peat quartz sand, perlite and chopped bark for other 2 inch; finally i put peat, perlite and quartz sand. if i got it, i'll put live sphagnum. in the coming years I will replace the peat with coconut fiber, in my small way, I want to preserve peat bogs, where  carnivorous plants grow. I use also pots made ​​from various plastics, making them by similar to an hydroponic pot.   
Another factor to consider is catches of the plant: more catches, more is healthy!  
another is climate factor: cold night and warm days make the plant much colorful, the greenhouse is also another important factor: with her, plants grow very much faster!  
it is not only a plant in a pot that we have to see, but a set of many factors and passion that make the difference!  
sometimes it happens that we see very different pitcher from the same rhizome, but we have to think that the plant does not print pitchers like a photocopier!

A friend of mine has a 'Claret' of sorts. The plant was a seedling produced at Marston Exotics post Adrian Slack and purchased as such during a visit to the UK in the 1990s. There is some question as to whether the seedlings were labelled by the nursery as cv S.'Claret'.  
  
So, while technically not the "cultivar" it is very, very similar and could easily be confused for Mike King's clone F25C though it 100% is not. My friend's clone, a real beauty in its own right, is correctly ***not*** labelled as S.'Claret'. As you guys have pointed out, clones can be highly variable in and of themselves without the added complication of misidentification!! Certainly nico and Mike, at a similar stage of development your clones appear quite different from my friend's one and Pandalf's.  
  
If you look closely at the photos below you'll notice the clone I refer to has a notably long terminal spur - much longer than the other clones in question including those grown by Mike King. Possibly this one is better? Guess that's in the eye of the beholder

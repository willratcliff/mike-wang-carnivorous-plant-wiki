The exact history behind the genetics of these plants is a mystery to me, but maybe others might be able to chime in?  There may be okee giant genes in here, and they did look abnormally big at one point, but currently, they seem to be like regular minors.  In any case, I'm pretty sure these plants resulted from a man made cross.  
  
Rumor has it an anthocyanin free S. minor was found in Georgia, and there may be a antho free minor giant out there in someone's collection, but I have no confirmation of this.  I know that Mike King has one of the original clones of S. minor antho-free, but it's a "regular" minor.  
  
Anyhow, these seedlings are quite robust and fast growing.  One thing that really caught my attention is how bright yellow the pitchers get-it's rather incredible!

Hi Mike:  
  
There's no mystery or rumor here.  We reported the discovery of the green anthocyanin-free Sarracenia minor in CPN "Sheridan, P. and W. Scholl.  1996.  Noteworthy Sarracenia collections II. Carnivorous Plant Newsletter 25: 19-23." Furthermore, the genetics of the green plants (a recessive allele) have also been published in CPN and HortScience (you can check out our research link for the citations at []().  Jim Bockowski produced some heterozygous S. minor by crossing the green plant with Okee minors.  We raised the F1's and have been producing green F2 progewny for years.  Mike King got some of this seed and that is how he obtained the plants.  
  
Sincerely,  
  
Phil Sheridan  
Meadowview

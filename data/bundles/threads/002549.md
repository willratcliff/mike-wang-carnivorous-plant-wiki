I haven't been able to get this thing to color up literally for years until now!  Why is it all of the sudden coloring up?  Not sure, but maybe it's because the plant is now growing in full sun from sun up till sunset (before, it was being shaded by other flavas, and didn't get direct sun until around 10am).  Not surprisingly, this all red clone is medium to slow growing, rarely produces offshoots, but has a unique bright red color.  It seems "atropurpureas" (notice the quotation marks) come in so many different shades of red, and I'm really liking this bright red tone!  Normally, these "atropurpureas" produce nice spring and summer traps, but usually by this time of the year, they are producing phyllodia and most of the good looking traps are crispy by now.  This clone, on the other hand, produces excellent late summer traps and are still looking good right now.  The late summer trap production suggests this plant likely has another species' genes mixed in there many generations back (ie. leucophylla, rosea, or both).  For certain, the genetics of this individual are unique, and its ability to produce late summer traps gives it the potential to produce hybrids or even pure species remakes with an extended show.      
  
During the spring, the new traps emerged on the greenish side and then slightly colored up but not completely.  On the other hand, these late summer traps emerged almost solid red!  The top of the trap opened up around 80% red and it's now almost completely solid red.

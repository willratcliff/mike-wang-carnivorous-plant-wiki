Here's a somewhat new selection of alata var. atrorubra that surprisingly colors up during the summer here in Northern California!  It's surprising because most alata clones grown outdoors here won't color up until the fall, but this clone, despite being divided this past summer, is defying the rules!  Given the "strength" of the propensity for this clone to turn red, it would be interesting to see how that translates in breeding projects.  When you're trying to produce something new and original, utilizing genetics that haven't yet been bred with or special plants with unique traits is the way to go!

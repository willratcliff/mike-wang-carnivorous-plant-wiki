Well, relatively speaking, it's not all that bright white right now, but it can produce very bright white traps during the fall.  This is the result of crossing S. leucophylla var alba (mother plant) with a regular S. psittacina (pollen donor).  Do a quick google search on S. x wrigleyana and you'll see most of them are red with white speckles.  In contrast, this clone has the majority of its "head" "painted" in white with red veins, and much like leucophylla fall traps, this clone's most spectacular show is also during the fall.  The white color can travel further down the petiole.

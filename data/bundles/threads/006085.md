Anyways, here  is a link to the history of this clone: [cpn.carnivorousplants.org/articles/CPNv44n2p90_92.pdf](https://cpn.carnivorousplants.org/articles/CPNv44n2p90_92.pdf)  
  
The point of this post is to do a grow report on Cephalotus follicularis Bubble Giant so those in the future who are contemplating whether or not to give it a shot....dude, if you like Cephs, you gotta try this one! I won't lie, it has been challenging for me to grow it and I did kill my mother plant and had to give it a second shot.  I'm currently on that second shot now because growing it was just that satisfying!  I was also equally super bent out of shape when my seemingly healthy mother plant got aced out all of the sudden due to sudden cephalotus death syndrome.  I had only had the plant for a few months!    The culprit?  Too hot in the fall: don't give them direct sunlight for too long, especially when it's warm, it'll eventually take them out and for sure burn them!  
  
So far, what I can tell you about Bubble Giant is that when it's not in "beast mode," it's really hard to tell this plant apart from a typical, so if you have a bubble giant and are suspicious, as long as it came from a reputable seller, you probably have the real deal even if it doesn't look like it!  That said, once it gains growth momentum and the traps start to size up, maybe depending on environment(?) they start getting that "bubble" shape and they also size up too!  Given that the stem tuber of my mother plant wasn't bigger than average, I confirm with ease that this clone is a proven giant: a clone that can produce relatively large traps even on a regular sized tuber is a dead giveaway, ladies and gentlemen.

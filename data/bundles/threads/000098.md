Here's a seed grown population that came from Bruce Co, ON (near McGregor Point).  A marl bog is a wetland habitat that has a basic pH-remember, most Sarracenia bogs are really acidic.  
  
Under such basic conditions, it is speculated that S. purpurea ssp. purpurea has more vibrant colors, and is a lot more stunted-the plants tend to stay very small and dark red under such conditions.  
  
I'm growing one other marl bog form from Docus Bay, ON, and I speculate that the basic conditions selects for different genotypes.    The shape of the pitchers on the dorcus bay plants look significantly different from all the other populations I'm growing, but again, this might just be a unique population.    
  
  
The population in the picture below definitely looks slightly different from other populations, but it's hard to say if that is genetic, envirnomental, or both.    
  
One other weird fact about the marl bog population from Bruce Co, ON-seeds only took 4 weeks to stratify to get nearly 100% germination.  Most other purp. purp. seeds from other populations had poor, inconsistent germination after 4 weeks stratification-the plot thickens!

Yeah, when they're smaller, they all look alike, but as the pitchers mature, the unique shapes become more pronounced.  Each different S. purpurea ssp. purpurea  population that I've grown out has its own set unique set of characteristics.  The huron Co, ON plants, for example, seem to have a more bulbous growth character and the plants in general don't seem to get very large despite their vigor.  The Lake District of Muskoka plants, on the other hand, have more narrow and long pitchers, and are extremely fast growing.  Plants from Tucker Co, Pennsylvania have the most bulbous traps I've seen so far.

Thanks Rhizomatous!  Several years back (maybe almost a decade ago?) we had a heat wave where it was 112F for several days, and then it dropped down to the low 100's for about 2 weeks.  I didn't lose a single S. purpurea ssp. purpurea plant from the heat.  
  
Keep in mind, there's many different genotypes of S. purpurea ssp. purpurea.  The ones from New Jersey and Virginia can likely take the southern heat.      
  
I've also heard that other people have had trouble with S. purpurea ssp. purpurea overheating (even here in California), but I haven't had any problems with them.  I have some friends in Alabama who are experimenting with growing quite a few Canadian purpureas, and I'll let you know how it turns out.  I've also sent several people in Florida some S. purpurea ssp. purpurea....anyone else in Florida, Georgia, or a Southern State have experience with S. purpurea ssp. purpurea?

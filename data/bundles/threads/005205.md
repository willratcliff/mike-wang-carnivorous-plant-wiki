AS some of you fellow homies may know...okay, okay, even if you're not a homie, but just a regular, normal human being who likes to get their nerd on, you might still know....Virginia is the northern most location for flavas, and they're really getting jacked up there, brah!  Very few populations are still extant, but fortunately, Dr. Sheridan has done a lot of work to protect them from going extinct.  
  
That said, red throated flavas in the Carolinas are quite rare in cultivation, at least in the US, and taking that a step further, super high quality red throated flavas from Virginia are very scarce.    
  
Here's the holy grail of red throated flavas from Virginia, this is the absolute best clone from that State that I've ever seen!  I almost couldn't believe my eyes when I saw the first trap open  this year!  S. flava 'red throat' Sussex Co, VA, pics taken 5/12/21 and a few days later:  

![](../../images/2e/2ec376a247316396abc87f97f03f2796b549803b6bae411eea1a4b9e9a583a27.jpg)

  
  
I guess you could call this thing an ornata or even a rugelii, but the whole nomenclature thing is a mess, so I like to make up names that are simple and less questionable:  

![](../../images/e0/e07674c0ad75352ce017f2bc174ff171d833ff4a73d6a02ead42621642edbb0e.jpg)

  
  

![](../../images/f4/f46d90cf1567cf04e8070bea05f921f05c45369f7fb5cffc2455c1a74c2913ca.jpg)

  
  
Check out the red lining around the lip, incredible!  This is after the trap has aged a little bit, and it's still a small.  As the traps get much bigger, they tend to be way more impressive.    First thing that came to mind was "YETHSSSSS!!!!"  followed by "YASSSSSSS!!!!"  No exaggeration, it's truly that exciting:

I feel like these previous pictures, as well as the pictures below, don't even come close to how amazing this clone can get when it produces large traps!  I wish I took a picture of the trap that this thing had on it when I got it, it was mind blowing!  
  
  Can't wait until this clone actually produces large pitchers, everything photographed so far has been under a foot in height and most of the traps are pretty darn small.  Might take another year or two, but mark these words, this clone is something else!  Perhaps it's time to hoard the divisions a little bit, which I never do: the first priority is usually to distribute plants, then get something to look good....eventually.   Oftentimes, the mother plant gets exhausted from being divided, but the divisions themselves can size up even quicker because they have better growth momentum, root systems, and less disturbance overall, like this one:

Here's another clone of S. leucophylla that I'd like to propagate and have tested in more northern climates.  To recap, I suspect that leucophylla clones that produce nice spring traps under cool conditions might be the best candidates for northern growers.  
  
Is this plant producing nice spring traps due to environmental conditions, or is it genetic?  I don't know for sure, but it appears the plant had some really nice reserves in the rhizome from last season's growth.   All i know for sure is that we're having a relatively cold spring, and very few other leucophyllas are producing noteworthy traps right now.    
  
Keep in mind, this clone below, like all of the other covington Co, AL clones, is likely "chameleon-like" in the sense that it may or may not produce alba looking traps depending on environmental factors.  Photos taken 4/9/18:

S. leucophylla 'eary emergence' Covington Co, AL has both nice spring and fall traps!  That doesn't always happen with plants that produce nice spring traps: sometimes the spring traps come at the expense of not producing nice fall traps.

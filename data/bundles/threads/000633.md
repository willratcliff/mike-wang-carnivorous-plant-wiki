Almightydolla has an outstanding "chameleon" ornata from Miramar Beach, FL (walton Co, FL) that produces an amazing throat and heavily veined lid underside.  Here's his picture for reference:  

![](../../images/46/46dafc801f061af6d993b3225abbeaca93258948506628f9e8dc0168f80b9a79.jpg)

   
  
Calen made a very intriguing hypothesis about the veined gene and the red throat gene here: [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/4166/flava-ornata-improved-black-outlaw](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/4166/flava-ornata-improved-black-outlaw)    Most likely, there are multiple genes involved in each expression. Having both expressions ***DOMINANTLY*** (if that's a word) showing up in a single plant isn't very common, so it'll be interesting to see if these two expressions "compete" against each other, or if there's a specific cross where you can get a lot of offspring with both outstanding red throats and very heavy veins.     
  
With the Walton Co, FL population, it seems the heavy veins and well defined red throat characteristics require the right environmental conditions to show up.  Is it possible to make a plant like the one almightydolla has that colors up very easily?  
  
  
I suspect there are many of these extraordinary red throated, heavily veined clones in cultivation, yet bringing out those colors requires very specific environmental conditions.  In other words, they don't always look so amazing.  
  
  Here's some pics of several different S. flava var. ornata clones from Walton Co, FL that have produced very similar red throats, but are now just barely showing this characteristic.  The following pictures depict plants that don't have "cut throats" but rather red pigments can fill in on the neck, part of the underside of the lid, and interior of the tube.  Like the famed chameleon clones of S. leucophylla var. alba, these our oustanding when fully expressed, but the genes need just the right conditions to be expressed.  Is it possible to make this expression dominant and have the plants color up easily?   Photos taken 6/9/18:

Here's S. leucophylla 'red' originally from California Carnivores.  Unlike the burgundy and dark pink clones, the color on this plant is blood red, which makes it an excellent plant for breeding.    
  
This year, the traps produced on this clone during the spring/summer seem to be just as large as fall traps, but perhaps this is due to the weather we had this year.  Traps on my leucophyllas last year in general were a lot bigger than this year.  However, trap production this year is a lot more plentiful, including this red clone.  Many years back, this clone didn't do much, but now it's really happy and consistently performing:

now for the sake of entertainment, if I'm being dishonest with myself, this clone is fast growing and performs well.  Now on the other hand, If I'm being honest with myself, this clone is has a medium growth rate but with good care, it can perform well.   I've had this tray for several years and while it has been cared for properly, it has been on the sidelines and pretty much ignored for years: I don't sing to it or do anything special.   the plants have been borderline stressed out this season from slightly not enough water but other than that, they have been kept in good shape and otherwise watered the way Jesus would want them to get water.   Despite an unseasonably cold summer, this clone really performed well!  
  
All weird jokes and borderline koo-koo thoughts aside, I got this clone from the late Legend Peter D'Amato way back in the late 1990's: I would spend 2 days a week up there for the whole summer and he paid me $8.33/hour in plants!  It added up and it's the reason I was able to obtain a good amount of their clones from back in the day!    
  
This clone likely originated from the late Bob Hanrahan's property in Southern Baldwin Co, AL.  He had native leucophyllas on his property and would propagate, dig up rhizomes, and sell them in bulk to Peter.  There's a very good chance this clone came from him but even when I quizzed Peter a decade ago about the origin of this clone, he wasn't so sure so it's origin is somewhat of a mystery.  Anyways, this clone has been around for a very long time and I've sent it to so many growers, and other growers have spread it to others, so it's likely lurking in several collections to this very day.  If it isn't and everyone failed, I'll propagate it and get it out to those who are interested.

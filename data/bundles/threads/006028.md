Here's what I thought was a random clone that was acquired more than 20 years ago.  It came from my friend Art Junier and at the time that I got it, neither he nor I realized this clone was special.  Unfortunately, it didn't come with any location data....all the tag said was "S. alata" but if I had to guess, this is likely a division of a wild plant.  
  
  It remained in the "neglected" section for about 15 years until one year, it produced GIGANTIC traps seemingly out of nowhere!  As soon as that happened, I tried to propagate it, but EVERY division rotted out except one side shoot on a rhizome cutting, which was barely alive because the rhizome was rotting!  I separated it from the rhizome and saved it.    
  
About 3 years of babying this tiny division, I was able to start getting divisions from it and fast forward maybe 5 years later (today), there's finally a good amount of divisions of it in the collection!  
  
I haven't gotten this clone to size up all the way again because the focus was propagation, but now I'm going to let a few grow out.  In the meantime, there is this pretty big trap that was produced somewhat recently: it's not gigantic like it got in the past but certainly bigger than normal.  This clone is super vigorous but as mentioned earlier, it's extremely rot prone.  How do you keep it from being so rot prone?  one trick, at least here in Northern California outdoors, is to NEVER sit it in water but do keep the soil fully hydrated at all times.

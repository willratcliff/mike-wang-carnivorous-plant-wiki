If I'm being dishonest with myself, this clone is super easy to grow.  Now if I'm being honest with myself, this clone is for sure more temperature sensitive than others: I lose divisions of it every year!   Unless you have climate control, it's really important to keep this plant on the cool side when it's hot outside, otherwise it'll succumb to sudden cephalotus rot syndrome, a term that I just made up but is all too common when you grow these plants.  Speaking of which, I lost a little more 25% of my entire cephalotus  collection in the past 3 months despite having lots of experience with these plants, so don't feel bad if you lose a few, it comes with the territory.  I have them outside under shade cloth, and almost an entire tray of plants slowly died over the past 3 months after that heat event.  I know it was heat because I had added an extra layer of shade to the entire collection else except that one little patch of plants (ran out of extra shade cloth!).  Of course, a few pots of UC Davis perished...and of course the really big mother plant bit the dust!     
  
Anyways, I actually already wrote about the history of the UC Davis clone that you can find here: [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/6033/cephalotus-follicularis-phil-mann-pm1](https://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/6033/cephalotus-follicularis-phil-mann-pm1)  
  
Okay, all of that aside, this clone, with the right conditions, can form MASSIVE clumps with large, colorful traps!  It's quite vigorous and propagates like crazy, which is probably why this clone is becoming more and more available as time goes on.  I didn't realize it, but this clone can also produce black-ish lids!  WOW!

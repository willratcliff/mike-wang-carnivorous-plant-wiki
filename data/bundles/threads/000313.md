#### **Mike Wang** (`@meizzwang`)
`post #4` · `id=9765` · `2014-08-11 20:09:15 UTC`

I was informed the site was visited a few years back, and is overgrown and no longer cared for, since the original owners passed away.  The plants were reported to be overgrown and struggling.  This site used to be regularly burned ever year.   A pond was installed on the property, which effectively wiped out a large portion of the population.  However, this may have happened decades ago.  
  
  
I've seen sites that haven't been burned in decades and while things are definitely overgrown, the plants are still thriving.  I've also seen sites which were reported to be in bad shape back in 1994, and in 2013, the site looked amazing: [sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/931/leucophylla-santa-rosa-different-site](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/931/leucophylla-santa-rosa-different-site)   
  
On the other hand, I've seen sites that were reported to be in decline, and then when we visited the site, it was completely gone.  In these cases, the soil was disturbed, and the drainage was altered.  At sites where the land isn't altered, there's still a a chance that plants can remain for a long time without fire or management.  If the land is altered, even with great site management, the plants may not persist for long.

This site can definitely recover, so long as the water table isn't altered.  Sarracenias can last a long time in the wild without fire, so long as shrubs and trees don't overgrow the site and turn it into a forest.  I've seen a few sites that haven't been burned for at least 2 decades and the plants are still okay.

The Franklin Co, FL leucophyllas are from the eastern most population of their range, and just like most populations, these plants have some unique characteristics.  Some of the best red variants I've ever seen come from this area, and their colors become the most intense at the end of the grow season when  night temperatures become rather cool and the light spectrum changes.  
  
Growth-wise, these Franklin County leucophyllas typically send up decent spring/summer traps, and then produce their most  colorful pitchers  during the fall.  The new traps open up with a moderate amount of red pigments, but as they are exposed to the elements during late fall/early winter, the entire pitcher can turn solid red.  This may not happen under everyone's conditions, it does commonly occur under cool sunny days and very cold nights.  
  
Most of the leucophylla clones in cultivation from Franklin County look very similar and follow the same growth patterns, but just like people, each individual has slightly different characteristics that make them unique.

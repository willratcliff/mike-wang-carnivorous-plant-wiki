I didn't even know ancestral rubras existed in Tattnall Co, GA until one of my contacts hooked me up with some plants!  This must be super rare because I've been to the tattnall Co, GA site and didn't see them despite having plant X-ray vision....okay, okay, I don't have that, but I certainly didn't see them in situ!  reportedly, these come from the same site where the rare Tattnall Co, GA purpurea ssp. venosa originates.  The great news about those extremely rare plants is that they're now VERY common in cultivation and their numbers ex situ easily exceed the size of the original population in situ!  This means it makes no sense for people to go out there and rip out plants, so the threat of collectors destroying what's left in the wild has been reduced drastically.   Next step is to propagate the clone F out of the ancestrals from this site, although admittedly, I doubt there's much poaching pressure on the wild plants since demand isn't very high for them.

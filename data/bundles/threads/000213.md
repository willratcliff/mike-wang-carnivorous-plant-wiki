The county has designated the site as a location that needs to be protected in the future, but so far it isn't.  The lot is for sale and it is several acres in size, with the designated building site up well away from the lake shore.  So it all depends on how future owners treat this stretch of lakefront that is about 100 feet long or so. Those colors you're getting out there mbfmark are remarkable!   
  
Such a sad story about S. montana in the wild-sounds like the same thing is going on for most privately owned Sarracenia sites.  It's just a matter of time when someone else buys it...and when they put that much money down on property, if they aren't plant or land conservationists, that habitat is inevitably destroyed.  
  
Rest assured, plants in cultivation from transylvania Co, NC are being reproduced at a very rapid rate, but those plants are only an iota of the genetic diversity out there.  For future re-introductions, aside from proper land management,  the survival of the species in situ relies heavily on diversity to adapt to new environmental stresses.

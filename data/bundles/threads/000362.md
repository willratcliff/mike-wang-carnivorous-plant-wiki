Here's a S. flava var. atropurpurea from the Carolinas.  The entire trap can get solid red, including the interior of the pitcher.  A lot of the times, S. flava var. atropurpurea won't look solid red unless it's under optimal conditions.     
  
Most S. flava var. atropurpureas you see in cultivation today are from Florida, where they are sometimes found mixed with S. flava var. rubricorpora populations.  With today's current naming convention, some flava var. rubricorpora plants can produce S. flava var. atropurpurea plants-here's a post on that subject: [sarracenia.proboards.com/index.cgi?board=flava&action=display&thread=273](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/index.cgi?board=flava&action=display&thread=273)   In the wild, I have seen all red plants mixed with the rubricorporas in Bay county and in Liberty Co, FL.  There are also reportings of S. flava var. atropurpurea from Santa Rosa Co and possibly Okaloosa Co, but these populations are different in the sense that there typically aren't rubricorporas found in those colonies.   
  
 The Carolina atropurpurea is uncommon and extremely hard to find in cultivation these days because they're slow growing and don't divide very often.  There are also very few sitings of this  Carolina variety in the wild, which adds to their overall rarity.  
  
Anyhow, I find these Carolina plants to be some of the most amazing individuals out there:

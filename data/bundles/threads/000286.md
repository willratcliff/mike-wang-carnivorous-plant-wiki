I could see some alata being in Deep Throat, but I think the suggestions on here might be wrong about where it's coming from.  
  
The errant pollen hypothesis doesn't hold up for me, because Damon has made a ton of plants from that grex, even recently, and a lot of them look like Deep Throat siblings.  
  
Looking at the parents, Mr. Mike Wang tried to talk me out of buying a S. Leucophylla 'Red' aka 'Martha Stewart' (link below). He said the plant struck him as notably inbred, that this would make it useful as a breeder, but otherwise there were a lot better options out there for just growing. I bought one anyway, because of its history.  I can attest this plant is pretty, but it's also the antithesis of vigorous. It also hesitates to put up pitchers. Mine is currently putting up its second pitcher of the year. I doubt a plant this genetically weak is a mutt.  
  
[Sarracenia leucophylla RED (CC's clone)](https://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/3405/sarracenia-leucophylla-red-ccs-clone)  
  
But let's go take a look at dad. S. minor 'Giant' looks like few other S. minor I've seen and it has a reputation for being "shapely". S. minors typically have a round hood, sometimes with a cleft.   S. minor 'Giant's' hood isn't round nor does it point down.  Instead, it flows forward and comes to a point, like an alata.  This is the most likely plant to have alata genes out of the two parents.  So that would make it, "S. miniata 'Giant'".  (If true, then is it even necessarily an okefenokeensis?  Its height could also come from the alata genes.)  
  
[S. minor var. okefenokeensis Cali Carnivores clone](https://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/1394/minor-okefenokeensis-cali-carnivores-clone)  
One could question the liklehood of a S. minor x alata cross, but keep in mind these are likely select plants Bob Hanrahan sent Peter D'Amato back in the 90's.  Hanrahan's property would be the exact kind of place where a minor x alata cross could naturally occur.

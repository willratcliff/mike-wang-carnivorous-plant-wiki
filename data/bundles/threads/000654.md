When the pitchers first started to develop on this clone, I thought it was a S. flava var. ornata, but over time, the red color started to become more and more solid.  The traps are not solid red (yet) but it most likely will be as the trap ages.  As many of you know, there is quite a bit of controversy as to whether the "real" atropurpurea grows in Florida.  Many suspect it's just some variant of S. flava var. rubricorpora or so genetically mixed that a few individuals turn really red.

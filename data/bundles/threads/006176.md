This is probably one of the more interesting sites that I've visited, and not due to diversity but rather due to the strange ways that this plant has adapted to really unfavorable substrates!  Everyone is always after figuring out what the perfect substrate to grow these plants in, and the more I see these in the wild, the more I'm realizing that the answer is dynamic: the best substrate depends on your environment and how you care for the plant!  
  
   
  This site is in the middle of a forested area, so light isn't optimal: normally, Darlingtonia are growing in areas that get decent light, but unlike Sarracenia, they are also found THRIVING in relatively dark environments where you wouldn't expect them to be able to make it because the light level is so low!  The downside to that is powdery mildew: luke cold conditions and low light leads to the infection, especially in the wild!  
  
Anways, back on topic, let's take a tour of this site!

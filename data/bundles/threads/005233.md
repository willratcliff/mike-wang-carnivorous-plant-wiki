I always wondered, why aren't excellens crosses as prominent as mooreis?   Now I understand!  Even in a collection of hundreds of minors blooming and thousands of leucophyllas blooming,  it was still tough to time the flowers right unless you stored pollen or have the ability to manipulate flower timing.  That said, sometimes, there's luck and you get flowers to open at the right time, just like in the case of this cross!    
  
Also known as Sarracenia x Excellens, I think at one time someone said they wanted to rename the excellens cross that contains minor giant "most excellens."  Well, what would you name this cross if you stepped it up a notch further and used a primo dope alba as one of the parents?  Supersonically most excellens?!  
  
I don't think this exact cross has been made before using the first "man-made" alba to create a hybrid that's super white!  Not all the seedlings turned out this white, but they all look very interesting and have great genetics for further breeding projects.    
  
Will these turn even whiter during the fall and as the traps get bigger?  I have no clue, but what is known is that I've never seen an excellens like this before!  These traps as they are right now likely don't represent the color potential of this cross, so I'll have to take more pics as these seedlings grow up.  That said, the preliminary results are very exciting!

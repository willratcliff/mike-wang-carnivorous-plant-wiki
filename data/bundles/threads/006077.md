Here's a clone that's nearly impossible to acquire in the US, but far more abundant in Europe.  It reportedly originates from a botanical garden/zoo in Germany named Wilhelma. I had to get it imported in the US (the few who have this clone in the US didn't ever have divisions available) and found a source that was deemed reliable: so many "cuts" of this clone appear to be not the real deal: lots of growers have selfed seedlings that are labeled "Wilhelma SG."  As far as I can tell, this clone that I acquired appears to be the the original clone: one key feature is that the traps have a slightly bulbous, wide shape to them.  Just because your clone isn't showing this trait right now doesn't mean it's not the real deal since there's such a wide range of phenotypic variance even within a single clone, but if it never eventually does that over the years, I'd suspect something is off.  Also, anytime you see a plant with the name "Wilhelma SG," that means it's a selfed seedling from the mother plant, which is different from the original clone.  
  
Generally speaking, I've had a tough time with Cephalotus: it hasn't been easy keeping them alive in the long run, but this season has been the most successful to date!  What's the secret?  Kill 150+ plants and figure out why they all died!  I'm sure many out there don't need to kill that many plants to succeed, but that's what it took me.  Not everyone is going to run into issues like I did and they may succeed for many years, but if they ever move or have new challenges due to climate change, they won't be so prepared for it.  It's counter intuitive, but IMO,  killing plants is the single most effective way to understand how to keep them alive!     
  
  
Under my care, the plants kept getting too hot, which caused sudden cephalotus death syndrome, so as soon as I kept the temperatures cooler during heatwaves, they no longer started randomly dying off on me weeks to a month+ later!  Don't ever let the soil temperature go above 85F (29C), that's a recipe for sudden cephalotus death syndrome!  This clone ALMOST bit the dust earlier this year, but after losing all its traps after a heatwave, it came back with some new pitchers, and I was able to keep it alive!  YESSSSSS!

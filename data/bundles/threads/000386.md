Here's some purp. purps. from Tucker Co, West Virginia.  Rumor has it S. purpurea ssp. purpurea isn't found growing naturally in West Virginia.  In the early 1900's, there was a construction project in Pennsylvania, and they relocated a huge amount of purple pitcher plants to West Virginia, so technically, these originated from a naturalized population.    
  
One characteristic of plants from this population is the traps are nice and bulbous.  There are other populations with this same characteristic, but many of the other purp. purps. I grow are more elongated and less "tubby."  
  
  
In the pictures below, notice the pretty neat red lining on the outside of the trap.  I'm beginning to suspect this is environmentally induced because I have another clone from Connecticut that did the same thing.  However, Since Pennsylvania and Connecticut are relatively close, perhaps these are genetically similar:

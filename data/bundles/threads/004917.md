I've had this clone for over a decade now and never photographed it until now!  The pot had been completely shaded by surrounding plants year after year, so it never really did anything....especially since the soil was almost completely decomposed and half missing from the pot!  
  
Last winter, whatever rhizome that could be salvaged was cleaned up and placed new peat.    
  
On interesting feature that I'm starting to notice with this clone and many from Washington Co, AL is how early in the season fall traps are produced!  I think these have been greatly overlooked and should be considered for those who live in climates where the fall weather is unpredictable (ie. it abruptly becomes cold in early September).  Maybe these clones can sneak in their fall traps right before that cold hits, so you won't miss the fall show!  
  
S. leucophylla 'vigorous,outstanding' highlights the "standard" look for regular leucophyllas within its region of origin.  This clone can produce an abundance of pitchers once fully established with multiple growth points, and I think in a year or two, I'll be able to get some pics at its best.

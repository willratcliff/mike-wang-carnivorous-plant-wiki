It seems to be quite a priviledge to walk into your backyard and stumble upon some amazing wildlife.  I've been at this new location in Northern California for almost 4 years, and every year I start stumbling upon new animals that I didn't know existed here.  In the middle of the summer, I started hearing these very bizzare, low pitched noises and it was really scary.  Was it a mountain lion?  Yeah, that's how ignorant I was to it.  For the longest time, I couldn't figure out what the noise was, but I knew it was some sort of bird.  One night, I heard the noise maybe 5 feet away from me, so I got a flashlight and BAM!  It was the cutest little screech owl!  It was probably mating season, and I heard maybe 6 or 7 of them every night for a good 5 weeks.  Not sure you can see it, I couldn't get close enough to get a good shot:  
[https://postimg.cc/gxtcTtPL](https://postimg.cc/gxtcTtPL)  
  
  
  
Anyhow, OMG!  Owls live here?  What else?  Coyotes are old news, they howl every time an ambulance, police car or fire truck rings their sirens.  Something I wasn't expecting showed up a few nights ago when I was weeding my garlic patch (yes, Mike Wang weeds at night with a headlamp).  I got tired of pulling weeds, so I took a break and checked out my new Hachiya persimmon tree.  Without an intentional search, I saw a small tarantula hanging out!  I immediately grabbed my daughter Ellie and then she asked if we could go into the field above the house to look for more.  Ironically, she was scared of the dark and the noises in the woods, but she's not scared of these horrifying TARANTULAS!  Well, they're rather beautiful to me, but I can see how others are afraid of them.  
  
  We searched for maybe 20 minutes and found another one!  Holy crap, they've been here this whole time and I didn't notice them in 4 years?!  You have to be out a the right time, these are probably the last of the males on the search to reproduce before they die.  Pics taken 11/4/18:

I lived in a rustic, 12x12 cabin for 2 1/2 years, after graduating from the Univ. No indoor plumbing, wood stove heat, Coleman stove to cook, y'know, camping for 2 1/2 years. During the warm months, I'd sleep in the loft with a powerful slingshot and a bowl of wet, compressed balls of newspaper, in a nearby bowl. Every now and then, I'd turn on the battery lantern and there'd be one or more huge Wolf Spiders on the ceiling or walls; that's where the balls of wet newspaper and slingshot came into play… thwack!!  
  
It'll freak you out when you're in bed and one of those things runs across your face

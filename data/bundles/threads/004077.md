Totally thought I already photo-documented this clone, but guess not!  Here's a fascinating S. flava var. rugelii clone that produces slightly coppery colors on the traps!  It reminds me of Mike King's S. flava var. rugelii 'copper tinge' Santa Rosa Co, FL except this is from a different County (Okaloosa Co, FL), has much more vigor, gets taller, and has different characteristics to the shape.   For one, I'm noticing some slightly angular lips!  However, I don't know if that's genetic or environmental.  Should it be genetic, that means there's great potential in creating really strange, angled lipped hybrids, much like what you see with Reptilian Rose!  That would be pretty neat if this clone is another "pathway" to creating such interesting hybrids.  
  
 From reviewing the pictures, it appears the coppery color fades away as the traps age, but I can't remember for sure, will pay closer attention this year.    The interesting coppery blush characteristic is most likely due to somewhat distant hybridization with either atropurpurea or some other species/hybrid.

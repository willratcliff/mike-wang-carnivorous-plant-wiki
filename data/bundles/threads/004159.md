Here's an interesting cross between a S. flava var. atropurpurea x leucophylla, both parents originating from the same population in Okaloosa Co, FL.  Out of the many seedlings from this cross, there were a lot of mutts...in fact, the vast majority weren't keepers whatsoever!   After they started producing adult pitchers, it was clear the dark pink clone below was a winner, so I kept that one and 2 others...although 1 of the other 2 isn't all that spectacular, so it'll probably get canned as well.  
  
  
It's really difficult to capture the color on this clone, but it's DARK, Maaaaan!  It's not black widow dark, but darker than your typical pink or red Sarracenia.  I think the first shot gives the best impression of how dark it is, but it looks much different in person.   Photos taken 5/29/18:

Okay this plant is officially really cool guys!  When it comes to breeding, when you use the right parents, the colors in the offspring can become even darker than either of the parents.  However, in this particular case, the dark color was an exception to the rule, as only one individual displayed this intense, dark color on the body of the plant.    
  
Without a doubt, this is the sort of primary hybrid that has incredible potential for crosses, and only time will tell what will happen.  Photos taken 10/29/18, it appears a little just a bit darker in person than what you see captured in these photos:

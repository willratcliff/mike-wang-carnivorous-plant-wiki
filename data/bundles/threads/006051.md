Sarracenia leucophylla 'WAF' Covington Co, AL. Yes, yes, you are all wondering WTH does WAF stand for?  Well, it's not "white Anthocyanin Free" I can tell you that much!    My homie Rob  Chiggity Chocolately Cocomo aka Rob Co of  the pitcher plant project figured out the name on his first guess.    
   
Notice how white the traps are, I'm pretty confident that this clone can turn solid white without any veins under ideal conditions, but that's just my gut feeling.     It is technically an alba (interior of the trap is solid white), but I'm starting to get tired of typing/voice to texting such long names that get "auto-jacked up"  aka auto-corrected to something incorrect every time because Siri don't speak plant geek so the heck with it!   Ahem, where was I?  Oh ya, with the light veining, it's very unusual for clones to have this much white, which is why this one stands out from the rest and will be propagated so that you can one day get a chance to give it a shot!  This was just "discovered" a week or two ago so I'll need at least a season or two to get divisions.  Come to think of it, I do remember seeing this clone last season: it was very white but not as impressive as this time around!  
  
Anyways, albas are incredibly rare from Covington Co, AL, so to find a rare gem like this is pretty significant!  Who knows what it can do with some breeding work!

It's not easy to select out "good clones" from a batch of plants because what MW might consider nice could be construed as composting material for others!  What is good and what is bad is very subjective, and online, people's opinions are strongly linked to how good the picture represents what you see in person.  So many times, countless growers have told me that the plants look way more impressive in person than in the photos, and that's because MW ain't no professional photographer!  
  
Anyways, here's a "new" ish clone from a large pheno hunt that is a regular leuco, but it's on the whiter side!  This clone grows pretty fast and has a very standard look/shape for the population in Washington Co, AL.  That said, it's not common to find regular clones like this producing very white yet not alba-esque traps!  When small plants produce unusually white traps, that white coloration  always (so far!) becomes augmented as the traps reach vegetative maturity.  Of course, the right conditions must be met for the plant to reach its fullest color potential: size alone won't bring it there!  
  
There is also a little bit of red below the white, which helps the colors pop out a bit more, but I'm not too sure that trait is stable.  We'll see what happens once the plant reaches maturity.  These are small divisions and TBH, I don't know where the mother plant is, it's somewhere in the leucomania abyss!

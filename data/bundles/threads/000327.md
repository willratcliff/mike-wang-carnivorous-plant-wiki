Here's some unique S. alatas from Covington County, although to be honest, I'm not sure if it's Alabama or Mississippi.  If I had to guess, it would be MS, since there's more alatas in that area, and the alabama area is more known for leucos.  Maybe someone out there knows?  
  
Anyhow, these alatas are extremely fast growing, and while the smaller pitchers are not very interesting, the bigger traps are stout and bulbous.   Typically, the  "bulbousness" is usually found only at the head of your average alata, but this population of plants seems to be bulbous on the head and part of the "neck" if you will.

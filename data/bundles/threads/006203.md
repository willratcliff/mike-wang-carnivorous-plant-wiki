Many years ago, I made this cross, grew out a seed batch, and even distributed several seedings out there!  Over the years,  was left was a community tray off plants that sat in the neglected section with overgrown weeds, plants packed in there like sardines with no space to spread their roots, and decomposing peat that was way past its prime...I mean it had been over 5 years with no transplanting!  There were maybe 40 seedlings left in there, many of which were still small to average sized.  However, one single plant in the tray shot out a huge trap, and I didn't even notice it at all until this winter!  Yes ladies and gentlemen, that's what happens when your collection gets so big, you don't notice everything because there's just too much to see....also an indication of overdoing it....maybe.....  
  
So, anyways, I dug this baby up, tossed the rest of the plants and thought to myself  (with explicatives, this is a milder version of what went through my head),  "GAH-sh Dangit, SON, this is unquestionably a giant!"  Now how would you know it's a giant?  Well, there are a few tell tale signs:  
1) it has a relatively small rhizome yet it still produced a big trap!  Pretty much all giant clones can do this under the right circumstances.  
2) Both mother plants are what I would call "genetic giants," and to recap the meaning, this is a giant clone that has the genetic potential to shoot out massive traps but it only does so once the rhizomes size up a bit  
3) the rest of the seed batch produced regular sized traps.  A giant clone is typically one of a kind, it's not uncommon to only get one or only a few select giants even in a good sized batch of seedlings.  
  
Since this plant went through extreme neglect, I don't really know much about it, but in the coming years, it'll be propagated and observed to see if it grows with hybrid vigor giant (a plant that sizes up year after year and has vigor from being outcrossed) or is yet another slow genetic giant.

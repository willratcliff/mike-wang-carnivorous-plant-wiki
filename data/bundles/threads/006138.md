To recap, there are only a few dark clones of rubricorpora Liberty Co, FL in cultivation, all of which as far as I know originated from the collection (there is another dark rubricorpora clone from BAy Co FL that came from Mike King's collection).  If you're after breeding the darkest plants possible, this is one of the building blocks for it!     This proven breeder isn't yet in circulation and it has finally been propagated for distribution!  In person, it appears dark purple: definitely not black but for those of you with experience breeding, you know that with the right combination, these sort of genetics can get augmented and become even darker with the offspring.    
  
Just to give a good analogy on the importance of these significant and rare wild type color forms, let's take modern day, cultivated watermelons as an example.  They originated in Northeast Africa and the wild types were light yellow and white fleshed, there were no red watermelons a long time ago!  From centuries of human intervention, someone down the line found a red mutant, and that one or maybe even two events is the reason we have red watermelons today!  All red watermelons trace back to that rare event.  
  
   
  
This particular clone is called back row dark #2 because this plant was "discovered" growing in a community tray of genetically diverse rubricoproras all from Liberty Co FL and it happened to be in the second to last row.  I'm no professional photographer but the way to confirm that this is indeed a really dark clone compared to the rest is to look at the other regular rubricorpora clones growing under identical everything (soil, light, environment, etc) and see how dark this clone is compared to them!

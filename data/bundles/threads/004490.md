"Hey Yo Ellie Boo-Boo, can you hold this trap for me so I can snap a pic of it?"  This was the conversation we had before leaving for preschool this morning and describes why you see my daughter's little paws holding the traps in place in the photos below.  
  
As some growers found out the hard way, a lot of flava rugeliis in cultivation....hmmm, not sure how to put this nicely...they suck!  Even amongst the select giant plants like S. flava var. rugelii 'Bob Hanrahan', you really need the right environment (ie. probably requires greenhouse conditions) to get gigantic traps.  However, there are many clones out there that are easy to grow and almost anyone can get large traps on them (ie. S. flava var. rugelii clone A).  In the next 2-3 years, all of these issues will likely disappear because dozens of select clones are being bred and selected for vigor, ease of growth, and large trap size.  We aren't there yet, but the backbone genetics to create such plants like the clone pictured below, are being worked on.  
  
S. flava var. rugelii 'multi' Liberty Co, FL was given such a name because as a small plant, it had multiple traps on it and stood out of the crowd for the size of its traps relative to the size of its small rhizome.    This season, it produced an enormous trap, so it's now officially a giant in my book.  Much like S. flava var. rugelii Giant thomas Co, GA, I think S. flava var. rugelii 'multi' Liberty Co, FL will alternate between producing gigantic traps one year and regular sized traps the other year.  In other words, this clone appears to be a moderately vigorous genetic giant.

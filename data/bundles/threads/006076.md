I've known about this site for decades but never tried exploring it because the dang shrubs were always way too thick to get up in there.  After a fire raged through it, this was the first chance to explore it!  The site probably  has several hundred plants but isn't super extensive or densely populated like other sites.  As you go up the creek, the population eventually stops: the ravine becomes extremely steep with some extreme slopes on both sides.  I guess it was just too steep for the plants, there wasn't really any suitable spots for the plants to anchor in and grow long term.    
  
Here are some photos of the site.  Notice the burnt out shrubs on both sides, all you see left are sticks!  This used to be so thick and dark up on there, you couldn't walk more than a few feet into the site before getting stuck and not being able to go any further.  It wasn't super easy to navigate this site even after the fire: wading through the creek was probably the only way to go pretty deep up in there, and the rocks were quite slippery:  
  
The scene was absolutely tranquil, this picture is something you'd probably want to hang up on the wall in a cozy cabin.  Notice the darlingtonia in the background:

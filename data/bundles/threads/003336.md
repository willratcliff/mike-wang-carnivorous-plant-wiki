Hi Bobby,  
  
These can get to a meter tall under optimal conditions, but usually reach around 2-2.5' for me outdoors here in California.  Other growers who baby these for Jesus can consistently produce 3' tall plants.    I sometimes get very tall ones, but that's an exception to the rule.  These are genetically different from the hurricane creek white cultivar: while hurricane creek whites do the chameleon thing, their "regular" looking traps are still whiter than non-albas .  With some of these other clones that I have, the phenotypic variance is so extreme, you sometimes can't even tell it's an alba until the late summer/early fall pitchers show up.

Nat: I heard that gangsta rap keeps these plants strong and on their feet, especially when they listen to NWA (north west alatas)....  
  
With regards to color, I think the factors that affect it are dynamic, but I don't truly know yet.  Rob S. informed me he cuts all the traps off down to the ground on his  rubricorpora plants during the winter and they're solid red with yellow tops when new growth resumes the next season.  When I do the same thing, all of my plants turn mostly green throughout the grow season.    
  
Leucophyllas are different beasts compared to S. flava var. rubricorpora because they have all season long to do their thing before fall traps are produced. Last winter (2015-2016) I cut off all the traps to the ground on my hurricane creek white bed and in the fall of 2016, it was the best, whitest, and most dense  trap production that I've ever seen:  

![](../../images/e6/e6d4b2050182e6207e1ea087cb4aab257bf0b9d5d03602a58cfa9e461313007c.jpg)

  
  
Perhaps light and temperature play a pretty darn big role in pigment coloration, but one thing I also want to investigate is how does the type of prey being consumed affect trap coloration?  I think what they catch in the spring and summer might have a big effect on coloration since this is the primary source of nutrition stored into the rhizome and then consumed for the fall pitcher show.    
  
Another thing we don't tend to pay attention to is rate of growth of the fall traps.  I wonder if slower growth rates produce more intense colors?  No idea....

In cultivation, most flava rugellii's will produce decent sized traps at vegetative maturity if kept happy, but there are a few individuals that are super vigorous and consistently produce large traps year after year.  In contrast, there are also somewhat average to slow growing clones that will sometimes produce dinky traps one year, but then the following year, a gigantic trap will emerge.  In some cases, entire populations of plants are giants, whereas in other cases, only a few individuals will produce gigantic traps.  The diversity in giant rugeliis is as extreme as the size of their pitchers!  
  
As an aside, S. flava var. rugelii 'Bob  Hanrahan,' a giant flava clone from Moultrie GA so far is one of the most painfully slow growing clones I've had in a while.  This might be because I received a greenhouse grown plant with a large rhizome last year, and it usually takes a while to get such plants to acclimate to outdoor conditions.  It only produced one trap last year that was close to 2' tall and has since produced pitchers no more than 4" tall this year.  
  
Anyhow, S. flava var. rugelii 'Giant' From Okaloosa Co, FL can produce abnormally large traps on relatively small rhizomes, and I'd say it's not vigorous, but not slow neitha!    The rhizome is barely 1.5" long, yet the trap it produced is about a foot and a half tall already!  Yes, some of you lucky ducky members out there have this plant already before I could photograph it, been working hard to get divisions in circulation.

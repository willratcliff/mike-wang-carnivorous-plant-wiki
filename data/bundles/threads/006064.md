So here's an interesting clone that colors up as the traps age!  I found out about it late last season a little bit late as the traps had already started turning brown, but for sure, this clone stood out of the crowd!  No other clone from the same population, growing under identical soil and environment, produced traps like this!  Well actually, there was one other, but it wasn't as yellowish, this one is for sure the best of the two clones!    
  
S. alata 'Golden Yellow' St. Tammany Parish, LA, here's when I first found the plant last year:

Notice the trap has some green near the bottom: only portions of the trap exposed to full sun will color up:  

![](../../images/b0/b08fb02354b7cb7a799bd2288ec9ccb2d8eb0b18d1bf0a5a26cd6996bed686fc.jpg)

  
  
I divided the mother plant and propagated some divisions.  Here's what those look like a year later!  The fact that it colored up even after being divided and has colored up 2 years in a row now (and probably many years earlier, I just didn't notice the plant) suggests there's a strong genetic component to it's color!  Pics taken 12/2/24:  

![](../../images/13/13160f8305d39f19f54d1148d60d215f10a81bf39551faabff93fd85a62c207f.jpg)

S. x Hummer's hammerhead is cross between S. (psittacina x alabamensis) x alabamenesis.  The name seems rather appropriate because it was bred by John Hummer and the shape of the pitcher heads does remind one of a hammerhead shark.     
  
When I first got this plant, it didn't seem all that interesting.  At the time, it had tiny pitchers with an uneventful dull green color.  It was just an addition to the collection, and in time, if it didn't look all that great, it was going to be tossed.  
  
Time has shown this plant forms beautiful specimens with an abundance of short and sturdy pitchers.  This plant is a weed and grows extremely fast.  Once given full sun, the pitchers develop a yellow tinge to them:

I do like me a monkshe ad hammerhead a wee bit better (aka Monk's Head Hammerhead spelled the same way but spaced differently) but the clumping habit of this clone is pretty darn nice!  Pics taken 10/3/23:  

![](../../images/89/890671b2c7bc9c78a5b4f7805efb3b8dc1b4706a9312bbcf4c20413f0bb0536a.jpg)

  
  
Look at how dense this clump forms when grown in full sun!  The colors are also pretty darn stunning, thanks to the alabamensis parent.  Speaking of alabamensis, that species is way under-rated, it has incredible color potential with breeding, so if you can get your hands on some nice yellow clones (mainly from Autuga Co, AL), don't miss the chance to breed with them!  

![](../../images/0b/0bf55480a001514384e9eb6cc23809c88a5e3792ccf02dc13d85ea9db6f7a665.jpg)

Back in the day when I had access to a lab, I did a bunch of in vitro propagating/cloning of orchids.  There was also a greenhouse in my backyard (pictured below) which we spent a year renovating.  This brings back sweet memories of being able to grow all sorts of warm, tropical orchids (Encyclias, Cattleyas, Mormodes, Catasetums, Cychnoches, Gongoras (we used to call them Al Gongoras), Stanhopeas....pretty much all the weird tropical ones that had amazing fragrances were growing in this greenhouse:  
  

![](../../images/12/1222c395223a2bc0ee4c13abbbca0db6cf428e74a123f126d8d43ea0e566e702.jpg)

  
  
Notice the nepenthes on the right: every time you walked into this greenhouse, the air was thick with the fragrance of exotic orchids, and it was always fun to escape from the dry California air outside and hang out in a humid, tropical paradise after a challenging day (NOT!) of ditching labs and going to my friend's labs to tissue culture....For those of you who are not yet adults, cherish every last moment of not having responsibilities, and take advantage of what you can.  This is exactly what I did, and it proved to be one of the best educational experiences I had during college:

found more old photos of my greenhouse, figured it would be nice to document them here in this thread!  this is way back from maybe 2006 or so?  Maybe some of you weren't even born yet, LOL  
  
Cattleya amethystoglossa 'pink powder puff', such an amazing cultivar with a large number of blooms per spike:  

![](../../images/88/888af5a8b68977d048fb459e155671be3ae65912f3d4c04b76187eee0b455ceb.jpg)

  
  
Another clone of C. amethystoglossa:  

![](../../images/2d/2d3e5bfcb11b566c7ccd29c09e2cf4f1c4e67603e4bf17ce6fdc68109bdd190a.jpg)

  
  
Coryanthes misasii, the bucket orchid!  insects fall into the "trap" and the only way out is a small hole that is the exact diameter of the pollinators.  If you're not a pollinator, you end up dying in the trap, but if you are a pollinator, you escape through the little tunnel.  the pollinator probably has pollinia on its back, and this gets stuck to the stigma on the way out, and before flying off, another set of pollinia sticks to the insect!     

![](../../images/fb/fbec406eb9caef4f1bf687fca25a92e274bd898a5904c976f156768e38f82853.jpg)

  
  
Here's a view of the exit:  

![](../../images/16/166f35a3abe6b475246392768dfc681b98b98d50fa8cc9d8557ccb53125a7138.jpg)

  
  
A view inside the "bucket" filled with this viscous liquid.  It's a lot like Nepenthes, except there's no digestive enzymes or cells to absorb nutrients in there!  BTW, this orchid smelled sweet and it filled up the greenhouse with its fragrance!  

![](../../images/26/26c8190400ae1f745be4e0af270f07a7e41c91204e54e83c5acdd1ffe339a3b2.jpg)

These came from the OLDEST Sarracenia seeds I've ever tried sprouting!  
  
  
Parrot pitcher plants from this region are extremely rare in cultivation.  I had acquired seeds and totally forgot they were in my refrigerator until 11 YEARS later!  For perspective, I've had 5 year old seed where 0% germinated, but in this case, the seeds were kept dry the whole time and kept cool the whole time.  I had moved 3 times in that 11 year period, so the only time the seeds came out of the refrigerator was when I had to move!   I figured what the hay, let's give it a try!  Seeds were stratified and after that they were placed under lights.  The typical 2 weeks flew by and NOTHING! Dangit, or so I thought!  But I left them there because normally, if the seeds are a dud, they'll start rotting after several weeks, especially if kept warm and humid.    
  
Well, about a month later, some of the seeds FINALLY cracked open, but they never sent out those hypocotyls (stem and roots), they just stood there half cracked open like a clam.  I thought "huh."  The peat already started growing moss on it and I knew there was no way these seedlings would survive being smothered by moss because they're just SOOOO slow to sprout!  Fresh seeds are the complete opposite: after 2 weeks, they're jumping out of their shells, eager to grow.  Anyways, I had to transplant them into fresh peat at this stage, which isn't easy...any slight damage in the process means the end of that seedling!  Since these were very old, only about 40% of them germinated, maybe less, but there were still enough that did sprout to have a good amount of diversity.  
  
After being placed into fresh peat, the cracked open seedlings just sat there until about 2 months later, they FINALLY resembled a seedling that would make it....many still had their seed coats on top of their "heads" and I had to carefully pop them off!  Long story short, these were the most difficult seeds the germinate EVER!   
  
4 months later, they were alive and well but still slow growing. It wasn't until about 8 months from germination that they finally started to grow at a normal pace!  Old seeds may take forever to germinate, but if they have good genetics and good conditions, they'll snap out of it and grow at normal rates eventually....since you were probably wondering or now you were wondering since it was just explained LOL  
  
Super long story short, from the day I got the seeds until the day these were photographed, it's been 17 long years! While this is an exception to the average, I hope everyone can now understand the trouble as well as the time that is invested in getting these plants into circulation!

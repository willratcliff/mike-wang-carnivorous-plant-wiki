Here are some pictures of seedlings that were started late fall of 2010.  They were grown under two 40 watt fluorescent tubes for most of the winter, spring, and summer.  
  
Around late september, about a month before it started getting cold, I placed them outdoors to harden off.  At this stage, it's important to slowly acclimate them to direct sunlight. After about a month of being outdoors, they were gradually placed in full sun.  By the time they were in full sun, most of these seedlings had already started to go dormant.  
  
  Seedlings tend to grow really fast the following season if they are the right size and given a dormancy.  The key is taking them outside at the right size.  If you take them out too early and force them to go dormant, they will be stuck in the "seedling" stage "forever."  J/K  Take home message is that to maximize the benefits of growing your seedlings under lights, take them out when they're about the size you see below and give them a dormancy.  The next season, the first leaves are usually double to triple the size!

Thanks guys!  looks like you found it Fred.  
  
After looking at the pictures above, I want to mention that these plants are a bit over-crowded.  When you transplant the ones that are way over-crowded from last year, you'll notice a lot of the plants are small with undeveloped rhizomes.  Seedlings that are given ample space tend to have decent rhizome formation.  
  
Another thing to keep an eye on  is "wildfire rot."  When you have dense plantings, if one weak seedling dies, it can spread the disease really fast within the community pot, and you may only end up with 25% surviving.  The upside to that is you probably selected for disease resistance.

Really interesting stuff from meizzwang.  
  
My approach is somewhat different. I utilise a light box to get seedlings away to a 'head start' prior to the actual outdoor growth season and then allow the plants to experience that season in full. This ends up being in addition to the 2-3 months they have had under lights. The outdoor growing season in my location starts mid October so seed is germinated in a light box at the start of August & the seedlings get the light 24 hours a day. The growth rate of the seedlings is rapid. Come October, the seedlings are gradually introduced to the outdoor sun (semi shade > small periods in early morning of direct sun etc). The seedlings do not go dormant but harden off & continue to grow. By the end of the season they have many times the development of traditionally germinated & raised seedlings. They happily transit into dormancy at the end of the season, by then sporting large rhizomes.  
  
I should point out in reinforcement of Mike's advice about crowding of seedlings inhibiting growth, I germinate and raise all seedlings in their own pot to gain maximum size. In my experience, Sarracenia seedlings thrive best when they have room that allows their roots to spread. During the first season as described above, I will carefully re-pot them at least once, often twice, to achieve this. The rhizome development is profound. After all, this is where it's at for healthy Sarracenia growth.   
  
Backing Mike up again, having had this start to life the plants have what you might call a blueprinted vigour over & above plants raised traditionally that never leaves them. Maturation and flowering can be achieved in as little as 3 years. Many growers who have never tried this technique will tell you that eventually such "forced" plants will "collapse". This is simply not true. I have raised all my seedlings this way over 20 years. Give it a go, it may work for you.   
  
Mike's way, my way, any which way, starting plants under lights is a great way to go. One thing is for sure, Mike and I share the same enthusiasm for growing outdoors - only way to do it if you can swing in your location

The misconception that plants will collapse if not given a dormancy is only true if the plant has gone dormant, and you immediately try to force it to grow again.  However, if the plant is acclimated to 24/7 lights and is actively growing, it can theoretically grow indefinitely this way.  
  
I guess the difference in Kiwi Earl's method is that plants are grown under lights for an entire season, and then essentially taken outdoors at the beginning of the outdoor  grow season? I think genetics played a huge role in its growth rate-only a few individuals flowered in year 3, whereas most flowered in year 4.

As to my procedure? Not quite. Apologies if things weren't clear.  
  
I get the seed germinated and the seedlings underway a couple of months *early* before they then go outdoors for the *real* growing season. They only have 2-3 months under the 24/7 light regime and continue growing under the sun until the end of the season proper. So, rather than Oct - March being their first season, it is July/August - March with the artificial light period really kicking the newly germinated seedlings into top gear for their introduction into outdoor living where they really put on the size. They don't grow for two seasons without dormancy, just the one *long* one.  
  
Should've mentioned also that well stratified seed will germinate in 5-6 days under my light-box conditions; way quicker than natural germination times.  
  
Here is an small article I wrote a good few years back now for *Stenopetala*, the journal for our New Zealand Carnivorous Plant Society, that gives a brief description of my light-box set up. It was very simple. The reflective foil is a good component to consider as it really bounces the light around.

There's a lot of imposter clones out there!  To name a few, I've seen imposter clones of S. flava var rubricorpora 'claret,' S. flava var. rubricorpora 'Burgundy,' Adrian Slack imposter, countless leah wilkerson imposter clones...the list goes on!  Rarely are the imposter clones as nice if not better than the original named cultivar, but in a few circumstances, they actually are better!  
  
So why is MW rambling on about imposter clones?  Well, to start, the tag to this leucophylla plant said PLI, and for the longest time, I was scratching my head as to why the hell did I name it that?   What could that possible stand for?  Pneumonic Litigating Instigator?  **P**seudo**l**itho**i**nstagammer?  AHA!  not even close...now I remember, **PURPLE LIPS IMPOSTER**!  
  
Should we really be perpetuating these "imposter" names?  I dunno, probably not, but it really does describe this clone quite well and I'm not Phil F. who can pull some amazing, creative names out of a hat.  
  
Well, in any case, this clone has pinkish red lips for now, and my guess is the lips were darker when the plant was named, which is plausible since colors do change as the traps age (the trap below just opened).  What I do remember is this clone did produce much more white pigments last year than this year, so perhaps the pics below aren't the best representation of what the plant does look like.  Nevertheless, this is one of those rare cases where the imposter clone looks really good....whether it's better or not is in the eye of the beholder.

A few years back, this clone produced 7-8 gigantic traps even when grown in a 6" pot, so I have changed the name to S. leucophylla GIANT 'PLI' Franklin Co, FL.  
  
This year, I didn't get giant traps, but I got one decent sized one worth photographing.  What's very peculiar is the color of the traps, even from the same exact plant, in the same environment, same soil, same clump, etc. is different from one trap to the next! At first, I second guessed myself and thought the plant was mislabeled until a few of the traps turned our purple lipped!  Yuuuup, phenotypic variation is a biiiiii....t perplexing.

The best flava rugelii site I've ever seen is also the scariest site I've ever been to.  Damon & Axel of California Carnivores accompanied me on a trip to the deep south, and we're talkin' DEEP South!  The site is located deep in the forest on dirt roads that don't show up on the maps, and judging by the lack of foot traffic, this spot isn't visited very often, or so we thought!  Being in a very isolated forest far from civilization has both it's charm and danger: one minute, you're in paradise, standing in front of hundreds of thousands of wild plants that are giant and thriving.  In the snap of a finger, you're in survival mode making decisions while adrenaline rushes through your body.  
  
This site was recently burned, and there was a massive amount of seedlings everywhere, which indicates a very healthy and thriving population.   While there are flava sites in the Carolinas that are larger than this site, this is by far the largest population of flavas I've ever seen in the wild.  Literally, as far as the eye can see, there's a "river" of flavas that follow a gentle slope, much like how Darlingtonias are found in seeps:

![](../../images/16/16d17e73e54c4e32cda26fe4a7a47559a11a84a8dfccfdf418a4cd83d61d09f6.jpg)

  
  
  
I hear Damon and Axel yelling at me to go back and we had only been there for a good 15 minutes.  Granted, I'm always the one who wants to stay, but in this case I was like "Really?  We literally just got here, C'mon man!"  I stubbornly said I'll be there in a minute-for the love of Jesus, I had barely taken any pictures yet!   But then I saw them running at me frantically, and I wasn't sure of what to make of that, so I took a picture:  

![](../../images/3b/3b299bee8d5ad09f2770a1bd71205b6af2e96dff981ccc2567c39e6970a2c569.jpg)

  
  
Axel: "Mike, did you just hear that?"  
Mike: "hear what?"  
  
We paused for a second and heard a creepy whistle that started out in  monotone and then ended with an upglide, followed by a gunshot in our direction!  It continued 2 more times, and worst of all, the sound was coming from the direction of our car, which was not close but not too far away.  I've done a lot of shooting, and it sounded like either a 12 gauge or 20 gauge shotgun, so I knew unless he had sluggers in there, we're safe if we're far away from him.    We did our due diligence prior to "boggin" and knew hunting this time of year is with bow and arrow only, and who goes out to the middle of the forest on a TUESDAY in the middle of nowhere?!       
  
Okay, to give Damon and Axel credit, they weren't being wussies because it was 100F out there and it felt like 115F, we were definitely in some serious danger.  Damon and Axel were about a 1000 yards from me and because of the hilly nature of the site, a lot of the sound gets absorbed  so it's hard to hear even a loud cry for help or gunshot nearby.  We were in trouble.  
  
 I was pumped with adrenaline and in survival mode, and rationalized that there were two options: creep back to the car carefully while hiding behind trees and scouting the situation, or find another road and follow it back to the highway.  I have a 6th sense when it comes to directions and wasn't worried about getting lost, but we decided it made more sense to see if we can scout out the situation because without water, you're in even more danger.  IT's tough finding drinking water in an area that is as warm and hostile as NW Florida (it hasn't rained much recently).     
  
We all decided to slowly check out the car after a little bit of time had passed and the gunshots ceased.  As we carefully approached the car, it was still there in perfect condition and nobody was in sight.  Needless to say, we got into the car as quickly as possible and left at BALLER speed on the sketchy dirt road.    
  
Take home message is that even with a lot of experience and a good size group, people don't realize just how dangerous it is out there.  One second you're in paradise and in the blink of an eye, you're in fight or flight mode.  More to come, this wasn't the only gunshots we heard on the trip!

Thanks guys!  
  
KE: We barely had any time to explore the site, but I didn't see any signs of S. rosea in the area, and as a matter of fact, it's really hard to find them in general.  In Liberty Co, FL, they're everywhere in certain bogs, but out in Okaloosa Co, they're either scattered or rarely seen.  You'll find S. x nacziis in some bogs but no roseas whatsoever, which indicates a pollinator flew from another bog to introduce new genes.  I suspect most bogs out there are very genetically diverse for this reason.     
  
  The biggest and beefiest flavas were in the lower, wetter areas of the bog or seep, but needless to say, we barely had any time to inspect them.  What I do remember is seeing some giants, but for the most part, the plants were merely large and not gigantic.  This site looks like it was burned in early to mid spring of this year, so perhaps it might be even more impressive next year.  I'm still not sure if the site looks at its best the first year or second year after burning...perhaps it depends on a lot of other factors.  
  
  
The S. leucophylla in general looked not so good, only a few plants were beginning to produce traps or had traps on them.  I inspected some growth points that were already dormant despite not producing any traps this year.  I wish we had more time to check it out, as I did see some more bright white plants in the distance.    
  
Interestingly, the only place I've ever felt the need to carry a firearm was in Alaska (because of the bears of course), but this trip definitely has changed my perspective.

Typically, people learn their lesson and try to avoid places where you've been historically shot at.  However, if that spot happened to be the most amazing flava rugelii site you've ever seen in your life, I guess some people like myself are crazy enough to go back for a risky visit!    It's ironic how some of the best places in the world for plants is where people want to kill you the most (ie. afghanistan+Pakistan(origin of apricots, peaches, nectarines, garlic, apples, etc), Panama, Colombia, South America (orchids), and this "shooter site" in Okaloosa Co, FL).  
  
Luckily, nobody shot at me, but I did pre-empt my wife by giving her instructions on what to do if I got aced out on this trip!  She wasn't very happy when I told her I'm going back to this site, but she hesitantly supported me once she knew homies was definitely rolling there!  
  
Instead of driving in this time around, I drove deep in the forest and parked on a remote dirt road.  from that point, I did a 1.5 mile run into the site ( if someone was trying to protect their weed garden or whatever it was out there, they're less likely to hear me on foot).  Notes to those crazy enough to repeat this: if you don't like spiders or spider webs getting into your face, don't run out there!    Anyways, while I was jogging on the dirt road, it was apparent that nobody had been there in recent times.  There were no signs of recent  tire tracks, no foot prints, no foot trails,  no signs of anyone attempting to remove several fallen trees blocking the crappy road, and no signs of the tall grass (which was overtaking the road) being driven over by a car. The road was overgrown and wild!   
  
   
  
  The high that day was 96F and 81% humidity, and that run definitely gave me a headbuzz afterwards (not in a good way).  Despite training for this trip, I  thought I was going to pass out afterwards, us Californians aren't used to that kind of heat and humidity, let alone intensely exercising in it.   
  
In retrospect, running in wasn't the smartest move, but the only option at the time unless you have equipment to remove trees from the road.  It wasn't the smartest because there are no trails leading to the site.  It also wasn't the smartest because unlike last time, the site hasn't been burned in years, so everything is overgrown!  the previous way we entered the site years ago (see first post of this thread) was un-navigable because it was now thick, tall shrubs with that FROWNAX (aka smilax) from hell mixed in!  
  
To make things a bit more memorable, on the way out, I got totally lost!  For a good 20 seconds, I admit, I got a bit freaked out, but then remembered my brother lent me a device that tracks your position and sends it out to a bunker in Arizona in case you get lost (you just have to press a button).   You can text them messages and if you seriously get lost, they'll send the local police your GPS coordinates and you just stay put.  I had plenty of water, food, and it wasn't cold, so I'd survive for a while out there.    Being prepared and ready for this was quite calming and then I started thinking:  why not use the bog  as reference point and never lose track of where you are  relative to the site?  As long as you can always get back to the site, you'll be fine.  I couldn't find the "road" that I used to get into the site because it was overgrown and clearly not used for probably years-it was erroneous to think that it's easy to back track using the "trail"  you used to get in!  On the way back, everything looks different, and this site is freakin enormous.  
  
  Anways, my caveman directions logic worked as I eventually figured out that I was heading Southeast instead of due east where the road was located.  Just go due east and eventually the road would show up!  That eventually worked, but after 30 minutes of walking.  Right when I hit the dirt road, as an unexpected reward, I found a persimmon tree loaded with delicious, native persimmons and man, that really can lift up one's spirits!       
  
  
Was all of the shenanigans worth it?  Yes!  Why?  
1) Because I found the absolute biggest S. flava var. rugelii trap I've ever seen in my life!  
2) this site appears to be the biggest, most genetically diverse flava site I've ever seen in my life.  
  
  Mostly, that one giant plant made up for all the trouble, it was unbelievable.  Yes, I have about a gallon of sweat dripping from my face, that was after the run (although even at rest, with that heat and humidity, my California acclimated body is pouring in sweat).  Look at how big that thing is relative to my dome aka head:  

![](../../images/e4/e44d6215d3f3f654f6723582228606d044b4b531261d936d0a2a73c683f7ef7a.jpg)

  
  
It's strange how a lot of those giants don't have well defined red throats, isn't it?  Very interesting that the biggest one I've ever seen has a poorly defined red throat.  This site had many giant flavas with amazing red throats, but none compared in size to this one.  
  
Well, that's just a preview for many more in situ posts to come.  I have a ton of pictures and stories to share, so stay tuned!

Xanthoparmelia-you need permits or  permission from the landowner to collect any material on any land, unless you are the landowner yourself.  To answer your other question, I think you'll want to read a dozen or so threads in the "Sarracenia in the wild" section, that should really give you an idea of why we go out there.    
  
daniella3d-This is public land, so anyone can go there.  No permission is needed.  However, on private property, if you are caught tresspassing in Florida, the landowner can only use lethal force against you if you are trying to break into their dwelling or if they fear bodily harm or danger.      Trouble is, when you go deep into a national forest, it's not uncommon to run  into illegal operations that can be very dangerous since they are willing to use lethal force to protect their product.  This site is so remote and so confusing to find, very few people make it out there, which is perfect for a marijuana farmer or illicit drug producer.  
  
The closest phone signal to this site is about a 40 minute drive away.  By the time you call the police, they'll be long gone, and for obvious reasons, we didn't go back to find out what they were trying to protect.  Could have just been someone crazy on an afternoon during the middle of the week who thought it was fun to shoot at people, maybe they didn't have anything to protect out there.  Sure, I could have called the police and maybe they would have gone out there, but when you only have a few days of vacation, you have to choose between spending your time doing police work or spending every last second you have checking out plants!  I remember Damon and Axel were extra cautious after that incident and were reluctant to visit a few other sites with me, they just stayed at the hotel.

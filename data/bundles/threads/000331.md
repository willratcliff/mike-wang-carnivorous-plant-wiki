Here's a plant I got from the famous Brooks Garcia (who named S. x Leah Wilkerson), and it originally came from one of the Wilkerson's properties.  Interestingly enough, this plant looks like something between a rosea and a venosa, and reminds me of some of the S. roseas in Baldwin Co, AL that look "venosa-like."    
  
S. purpurea venosa is found as far as NE Georgia, and the wilkerson's property is probably one of the northernmost locations for S. rosea.  Perhaps the two variants "mingled" sometime in the distant past and parted their own ways.  
  
The flowers are short, and much smaller than the other S. roseas flowers I've seen.  They also have a very unique scent, which I can describe as sweet yet with a hint of some chemicals-it's weird I tell you!  
  
  
S. rosea N. Walton Co, FL-this thing is a real beauty:

Been a very long time since I've updated this thread probably because it's been a very long time since I've repotted the plant!  Last winter, I went through my "old" rosea collection and divided/repotted everything that was salvagable.  It had been many years since the plants were repotted, but between work and kids being born, some of the material had been put to the side lines for longer than they should have.  
  
After being repotted, weeded, and watched over carefully for a season, this plant has bounced back into a ridiculously fat clump!  I think this is the only semi-dwarf S. rosea that I've ever grown and heard about.  Doesn't matter how well you care for it, those traps stay small yet compact.    S. rosea N.

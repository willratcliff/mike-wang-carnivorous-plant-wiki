I had heard of this plant many years ago and it's already in the US, but I've never seen pics of this plant in cultivation, let alone flowering.  Here's something  new to the collection: seeds were imported from Africa, so these are unique genetics and not divisions from clones circulating in the US!  When I first received them, the seeds were so tiny, I had my doubts that anything would work out, but almost a month after sowing, several tiny plants emerged!  They took forever to start forming decent sized colonies but once they gained a certain size, the plants started taking off!    I kept the seedlings warm and under grow lights to get the seeds sprouting , but once it started taking off, I made divisions and they very easily acclimated outdoors.  They get bright filtered light all day long and love it!  The soil consistently stays pretty warm during the summer months.  
  
As soon as the plant completely colonizes all of the soil, they go into flowering mode! Many different seeds germinated and I have no idea if it was from one plant or several, but many of them started to flower about a year after germinating, it's incredibly vigorous!  It also can send up an abundance of flowers and probably fill the pot with blooms if you get good growth momentum on some plugs.  As small, just starting colony, they formed a pretty impressive show!    
  
So far, they've survive near freezing temperatures outdoors and probably had light frost on them recently, but I haven't been paying close enough attention.  They are kept outdoors under shade cloth right by the side of the house and they're sitting on cement, which gets very warm during the day.  That said, some of the plants formed flower spikes in the fall and now they're just starting to open, and the colors were an absolute surprise!  
  
This plant doesn't produce seeds on its own and it probably requires cross pollination.  This is good news for cultivators as it won't invade your collection!  
  
Expert grower Noah Juve of Insectiside Nursery mentioned to me that a lot of Utricularias have variable flower colors based on temperature, and this clone is no exception!  I don't know with certainty if this is genetics or environment, but with some more testing we'll soon be able to find out.

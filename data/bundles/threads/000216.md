A lot of discussion has arisen about marl bog purpureas.  The first question is, are they genetically a different form, or is the fact that marl fens, which have a Basic pH (greater than 7) cause them to be more colorful?  
  
In my opinion, while the pH may put pressure on individuals to adapt to the conditions, I can't say that all marl bog plants look the same and should therefore be a variety...  If you look carefully at S. purpurea ssp. purpurea from one population to the next, there is certainly some difference regardless of the pH of the soil.  What I can say is that the marl bog plants that I've grown so far are definitely  unique, and they certainly can get very dark red.  
  
In any case, this particular plant below from Dorcus Bay, Bruce Co, ON is certainly a great looking plant!

Eric-Typically, under acidic conditions, nitrogen and other salts are "locked up" and aren't as reactive.  This is why many chemists, when experimenting with certain compounds, store them under acidic conditions.  When you add a base like Lime to the acid, it "frees" up those otherwise "locked" salts, which can become toxic to the roots because essentially, it'll be raising the ppm.  This is assuming you raise the pH to near neutral (pH=7)  I think if you raise the pH high enough, you'll get the same effect as peat: it'll be so basic that the salts are all "locked up"  I'm not exactly sure what pH value would give you the desirable results, but I'm sure someone out there knows the pH of these marl bogs.

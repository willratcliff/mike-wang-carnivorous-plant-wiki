I had been searching for this species for many years and it seemed nearly impossible to acquire!  The occasional offering that I found here and there looked very questionable (was I going to recieve some weeds in a pot instead of the desirable plant?)  A few growers in the US have this but it seemed most were hobbists and not willing to go through the hassle of selling a clump.  No matter what, acquiring this plant was going to be hard, so I decided to get a division from a reputable source in Europe and then import it over here.    
  
Growing this plant so far has been relatively easy, but it was a bit challenging to get the plant started: it has been in a box for I'm guessing over a month!     Based on other growers' comments, I always thought of it as a warm, tropical plant, but after doing a little bit of research, it turns out these are actually highland plants!  I actually walked right by some U. pubescens unknowingly when I visited Panama in 2005, and I found them after climbing up a steep Mountain near  El Valle.  At the parking lot, it was hot, tropical, and humid, but by the time we got towhere the utricularia grew high up the mountain, it was cold, cloudy, and windy!  I remember shivering in my shorts and T-shirt!  
  
Upon receiving the plant, it was potted up in pure peat moss.  The first winter, we had temperatures in the mid to low 20's outside, but the plant was kept under frost cloth by the house, I don't think it ever froze over.  Can it tolerate light frosts?  Probably, but my guess is only if infrequent and not for very long periods of time.  They can definitely tolerate cold temperatures just above freezing!    Anyways,  The next spring, it barely grew, most of the growth happens during late summer early fall.  This was also the best time to divide the plant and make more, although you can probably take divisions any time of the year from healthy plants.  
  
Here are some simplified growth parameters:  
1) bright indirect light is best.  They produce bigger leaves with lower light levels, so long as there is also high humidity and warm temperatures.  Keep the soil warm during the grow season, that's one factor that helps increase growth rates!   
2) pure peat moss is a great substrate, although you can probably use sphagnum moss or a more airy mix (not needed though)  
3) sit the pot in water, and once the reservoir dries up, fill it up.  
4) if you want profuse blooming, let the plant colonize all of the pot up, it can then flower for months with proper care  
5) keep the humidity high during the grow season, they like boggy, waterlogged, humid conditions.  That said, I don't like to allow the soil to go anaerobic, so I allow the water in the reservoir to dry up before watering again.    
6) You can probably feed with 1/4 to 1/5 strength maxsea, but I haven't experimented with this species yet.  Last time I tried feeding my utrics, I killed a few off, they're for sure very sensitive to nutrients.  There should generally be sufficient nutrients in peat for the plants to grow well, they don't need much!  Newly transplanted plugs into new soil generally take off pretty quickly.  
7) The majority of growers cultivate these indoors, very few to almost none (unless they're in the tropics) grow these outdoors.  I'm in a mild, Mediterranean climate cold wet winters and warm, dry summers,  and with a few modifications (eg. shade cloth, keeping them in cracked open ziplock bags) I have been able to get these to grow well.  However, they only seem to do well from about late spring until end of fall.  The rest of the time when it's cold, they do pretty much nothing.

You're welcome Paul, and thank you all for the kind words!  
  
The black veined flava is very unique, and there's a pretty amazing story behind it:  
  
Several decades ago, there was a plot of private property owned by the Kennedy family (former US president's family) located in Bulloch Co, GA.  A professor at the University of Georgia was given permission to do experiments on the property and take any plants he wanted.    
  
When Carl Mazur, former VP of the ICPS and the person who inspired me to grow Sarracenias, was younger, he got in contact with the professor, who took him to the site.  At the time (several decades ago), there were so many fields of Sarracenias everywhere, and the  negative effects of development and wild collecting wasn't well understood. In fact, when the CP hobby became popularized, the main source of plants made available by companies were dug up from the wild, and it was legal and acceptable (except for endangered species, of course).  In fact, to this day, it's still legal to dig up CP's from private property, although this activity is very controversial to say the least.  
  
Anyhow, Carl was given permisson to dig up some plants, and he took them home and kept them in his collection for several decades.  These plants were heavily veined, and had cut throats, but didn't posess the black veins.  
  
  In the late 1990's, when Carl started selling seeds online, he offered selfed seeds from this particular clone, and I grew some out.  Since they were selfed, the seeds were notoriously very slow growing, and took extremely long to produce flowering sized plants.  In fact, this plant is a semi-dwarf and doesn't get much taller than a foot.  To this day, I rarely get these clones to flower consistently.  
  
All of the selfed seeds turned out to be the black veined varieties, and since I haven't exported any to the UK, it's only available here in the US.    
  
I have been working to produce more vigorous black veined plants, and this year, I created seeds from S. flava var. ornata  (bulloch co, GA x Prince george Co, Va) x bulloch co, GA.  The two bulloch co, GA plants in the above cross are siblings, but are genetically different.   The hope is that by mixing up the genes, I'll likely get a few vigorous black veined flavas that I can further develop.    
  
Once that is in place, I will distribute them world wide-the original clone is so painfully slow growing, rarely divides, and rarely flowers.  What's the point of distributing a plant nobody can get?

After fifteen years work in breeding roses, I have come to feel that plant vigor is one of the essential attributes that a selection **must** possess in order to be considered "viable for commerce".  I have bred numerous plants that have the capacity of producing some of the most beautiful blooms, but the plant itself is very poor in terms of vigor. Ultimately, I think most growers would be frustrated by such serious underperformers. I realize its a bit different with Sarracenia breeding, but I would personally think twice before sending out divisions of plants that are hesitant to build themselves into specimen quality plants. Just my personal opinion, of course.

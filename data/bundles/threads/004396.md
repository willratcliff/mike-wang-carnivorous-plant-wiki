2013 was the last time I randomly drove into the forest of MS hoping to find an alata bog.  After 3 hours of driving to some areas that looked like they had potential, we gave up.  
  
Years later, I thought, hey, why not try randomly driving in the forest again, at least I know where they aren't located from the last trip!  If you think about the effort put into finding a new site several years ago, repeating the same method and expecting a different outcome....isnt' that the definition of insanity?    Well, maybe this time around, probability will be on my side....and it was!  
  
After driving down many different roads in the forest for several hours and then turning back with nothing to show,  I finally stumbled upon a few straggler S. alatas in what appeared to be a site that was overgrown and probably disturbed by human activity.  Essentially, at this point, our random driving in 2013 was about as successful as this attempt, it was really disappointing!    I turned back to the main road and reasoned that it was raining and there's lots of lightening, so might as well keep driving.    
  
20 minutes or so down a random road in the middle of the forest, I began wondering if I was wasting even more time, and the deeper you go, the sketchier the roads were getting.  A storm had eaten away most of the road, so you had to drive off the road to get past an enorous ditch.  At this point, I had made 2 random turns off the main side road (I placed shiny objects at each turn), these roads are more like trails and are not well mapped out (plus I don't have GPS or anything fancy, still sportin my flip phone!).   5 more minutes of this BS and I'm turning around, I hope there's no sketchy people out here!    30 seconds after successfully going past the really big ditch in the road, in the corner of my eye was a tiny little bog!  Very first thing that went through my mind was literally "**Like** **OH....My GOD!!!!**"   
  
Getting into this bog wasn't easy, it was surrounded by thick shrubs and you had to walk through some  "unknowns" that could have nasty critters in it.  Fortunately, nothing bad was in it, no mites, ticks, chiggers, spiders, snakes, wasps, or boo-hoos for me!

Back in the day, my Russian grandfather was a geological surveyor for the railroad company in Sao Paulo, Brazil, so he would basically go deep into the forest and map out where the railroad would go.  I don't know if you've ever gone miles into the rainforest by yourself (not following trails or roads), but very quickly, everything looks the same, so you better have a good instinctual sense of direction or tools to navigate the area.  At the time, my grandfather didn't use tools to navigate, he just used his natural sense of direction that he was born with, it's pretty much why he was able to do that job.  Long story short, a very good sense of direction is in my genes, but at this alata site, I definitely got lost.  The very first sensation of being in the middle of the forest and not knowing your way is typically a few seconds of freaking out, which admittedly, I did.  **Bride of Chucky** isn't anything compared to a feeling of being truly lost in the woods by yourself.    How did this happen?  
  
This habitat is unlike any I've ever been to because it's literally a network of bogs in the middle of a deep forest, and they are vast!  It's unbelievable how extensive these chains of bogs are.   Everything looks the same, and nothing is distinguishable.  Most CP sites  have a very crystal clear entrance, signs of human activity or paint on the trees to tell you you're close to the road, or it's just one single big bog, so as long as you follow the bog, you'll know how to get out.    Not this site!  First off, the side road that I drove to the site was an overgrown dirt trail, so there were minimal man- made disturbances or signs to help you navigate.   This bog complex is a mind bending maze!  There's no clear entrance or exits to each of the mini-bogs.  Look into the forest and head towards the open area?  Could be another small bog, but not the way back to your car!  Once you get to that small bog, you think you've back-tracked, but turns out you landed into another small bog that you previous hadn't explored.  Cool!  But you can't focus on the plants until you figure your way out.     Hold on a second, why not just follow your footsteps back?  What footsteps?  There's tons of animal trails too, and walking back looks totally different than walking in!  In retrospect, as I entered each new bog, I should have looked back to see what it looked like so I could memorize the entrance.  
  
   
  
  If you recall from the previous post, I entered the "second" bog by walking through ~40ft of thick brush.  I figured hey, should be pretty obvious on the way back, just look into the brush and look for the first small bog by the trail/road, but I couldn't have been more wrong.  People with a sense of direction always either consciously or subconsciously know where they are relative to a starting point, no matter how far they stray from the starting point.   I always generally knew where I was relative to the car, I always knew N,S,W,E relative to the car. But I wish I had kept track of the path of least resistance for the way back.  I ended up walking through about a quarter mile of thick brush, never doubting my bearings, and it worked, got back to the car safely!  Take home message is if you attempt this, you definitey need tools or a damn good sense of direction.

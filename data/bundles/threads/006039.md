This clone may not look like much at this stage, but it's probably the most genetically unique individual in the collection!  Sure, that's just speculation, but my gut feeling on these sort of things has historically been accurate.  So what's so special about this clone anyways?  
1) the most unique characteristic of this clone is that it's purple red from head to toe, all season long!  We're not just talking about traps that turn purple red as they age: even developing traps are fully purple red!  In my lifetime, I can't say that I've ever heard of or seen any leucophylla clone with such a trait!   
2) this clone originated from a leucophylla var. alba!  It was literally the only seedling out of thousands (I did a huge grow out) that had this trait.  Even as a seedling, it stood out of the crowd with its dark, unusual leaves.  Sure, it could have been the result of cross contamination with a red leucophylla clone, but given it's super unique abilit to color up the way it does, I believe this is a mutation that occurred from sexual reproduction.    
3) This clone is a genetic goldmine and in my opinion, can possibly drastically enhance leucophylla breeding.  Albas with dark purple bodies?  Enhanced red clones with solid purple-red bodies?   Dark purple to near black "pure"  leucophyllas?    I have no clue how it will "behave" in crosses but I do know that it's something that will be extensively hybridized with once we get the chance.  To date, it hasn't yet flowered and is not yet in circulation, so it hasn't ever been bred with!  
4) this clone is moderateto slow growing, suggesting that it's not the result of hybridization but rather mutation.  
  
Will this clone look even more impressive once it reaches vegetative maturity?  I have no clue, but we shall see!  S. leucophylla purple red mutant Baldwin Co, AL, pics taken 9/10/24:

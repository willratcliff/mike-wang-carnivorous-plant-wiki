For those of you into pedantic details that are probably better debated in an english class rather than in a community of plant enthusiasts, yes, there's an apostrophe in Cook's, although google maps at one time showed an apostrophe and also didn't show one.    In case you haven't heard this, I was at a gas station in Baldwin Co, AL one time and a dude said the most impressive double negative I've ever heard...well, it was more like a quradruple quintuple negative: he said "I ain't sayin that it ain't, but I ain't sayin that it is neither!"    
  
What the heck does this have to do with the topic?  Almost nothing, just like the significance of that apostrophe.  I rest my case, LOL  
  
Anyways, sadly, Cook's bayou is another location that is absolutely JACKED, homies!  It is developed, plowed, plundered....just absolutely VIOLATED from a ecological standpoint.  I surmise there are still a few stragglers out there, but back in the day it used to be fields upon fields of rugelii's, atropurpureas, ornatas, extreme red throats,some impressive roseas, psittacinas, and probably a few others.  
  
Here's a flava rugelii from an extirpated site, it can get extremely tall and produce a very large lid!  This is very likely a giant: it's crammed in a small community tray and hasn't been transplanted for over 12 years, but is still cranking out a decent sized trap!  Pics taken 5/26/21:

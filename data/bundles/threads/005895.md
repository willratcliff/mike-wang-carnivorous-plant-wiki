Quite frankly, at times, it's almost impossible to tell the difference between A28 and A58.  This is a proven black clone that originates from Mike King's collection, I acquired it a little more than a decade ago and it has done quite well outdoors here in Northern California.  The only catch, like most alatas, is that it's hard to get this clone to turn almost near black like you see them in situ or under LED's/greenhouse conditions.  It seems these might need a little bit of a "fake suntan" to color up all the way.  The same is true with A58, although both clones have come pretty close to coloring up all the way outdoors in the past.  
  
That said, after pheno hunting through thousands of alata clones, these two clones, as well as maybe 2 or 3 that I was able to find, are perhaps the very best ones in cultivation.  Black alatas are a bit challenging to breed because the coloration appears to be recessive, so your parent plants have to have the right genetic combo to get dark offspring.    Cross two dark clones together and you often times get plants that aren't superior to either parents.  Long story short, it might be a multi-generational project to breed dark clones that color up relatively easily OR it may be that we need to find the right 2  parents that can produce black plants on the first attempt!  I think there are some candidates that could work, we'll see!   
  
On the other hand, breed these darkalata  clones with other species and there's a good chance, with the right parents, that you'll get very dark offspring.  Think of black widow, it's an F1 hybrid between a dark alata and a rubricorpora.

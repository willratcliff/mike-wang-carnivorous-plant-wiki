Here's another example of a cross that's pretty out of this world that I haven't done a great job showcasing because....drum roll....the plants were overly crowded and never given the proper amount of space to reach their fullest potential.  So what happens when you do give these quality crosses ample room to size up?  I guess you could just look at the pictures below and see the answer for yourself!  
  
Clone #4 and clone N are both unusually tall rubricorporas, so the goal of this cross was to make really tall plants!  It was also quite nice to see that the selective pressure for good coloration paid off: so many in this batch had poor red coloration on the body, so I got ruthless and only kept maybe 25% of the entire seed batch.  Every last plant propagated was selected for good coloration, and knowing that both parents were tall, the hope is that a high percentage of them would produce tall traps!    
  
This was grown by one of my customers who preferred to be anonymous.    Here are some photos of S. flava var. rubricorpora (clone #4(tall) xclone N) Liberty Co, FL, this is what they can look like with proper care!  
  
  
At first I asked him what rubricorpora clone was showing up in the back right hand corner of the photo, that thing looks so good!

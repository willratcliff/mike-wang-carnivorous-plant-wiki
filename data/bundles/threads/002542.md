Another rare gem, S. leucophylla from Calhoun Co, FL is something that's both rare in cultivation and near extinction in the wild (at least within that county).  Much like Bay County, most of Calhoun County has been either developed or farmed.  A little more than a decade ago, some of the last remaining sites were roadside ditches where the Sarracenia luckily missed the herbicide and plow.  Today, those well-known roadside populations are no longer in existence.  In fact, you couldn't tell Sarracenia were there in the past!  Regretfully, some outstanding roseas were there as well, and South of this main site on the same road were giant psittacinas, also now extinct in the wild.  
  
This leucophylla clone may in fact be significant due to it's ability to produce an early crop of fall pitchers.  Is that an environmental characteristic, or is it due to genetics?  I have no idea, all I know is that this clone is very nice!  Here are some pictures of S. leucophylla Calhoun Co, FL, taken 8/12/15.  These traps formed about 2 weeks ago, which is WAY early for us here in Northern California.  Normally, we don't see fall leucophylla pitchers until early September:

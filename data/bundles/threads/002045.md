I just wrote this long story in an email to a friend (it has been edited), so I thought this will become an important timeless thread as the forum evolves. Perhaps this will be an ongoing thread and others can add to it as time passes by.  Many of you may wonder how did this whole Sarracenia forum thing get started?  Well, here's the story through the eyes of Mike Wang:  
  
Back in 2010, I tried to submit a Darlingtonia article to the ICPS and I didn't hear back from them even after a few follow up emails.  They NEVER replied!  Fortunately, my friend investigated for me and found out what was going on.  He informed me the article wasn't appropriate because they wanted to see more hard science with graphs and charts (data) that you can enjoy reading "over a hard cup of coffee."  I had merely written a field trip report (like what you see on this forum).  Also, they had too many amateur field trip reports about other CP's in the past, so they wanted something new and credible.  To their credit, they have an international reputation to stand  by, but perhaps they were a bit hyper-vigilant.  I suppose my observations, photos and stories weren't deemed worthy at the time unless there was hard data to back it up.   I gave up on them and this rejection is what motivated me to become  more active online.    
  
The Italians caught wind of the trip reports after I started becoming more active on other forums (ie. CPUK), and Maurizio of the AIPC asked me to write something for them, so it was a slam dunk.       They did an outstanding job on the article and took my amateur work and made it look professional.     
  
CP Politics are  strange: when nobody knows your name, your information isn't valid or valued.  When everyone has heard of you, people come to you out of nowhere non-stop and treat you like  a celebrity.  All of the sudden, the same stuff you wrote about years ago that nobody wanted or believed is now valid and in high demand.    
  
After the Darlingtonia article was published, people started to recognize me, and I was showered with emails from all over the world (it wasn't that great of an article, but the Italians made me look like a pro with their amazing editing skills).   With all the momentum picking up, I decided it was the right time to centralize a place online to educate the world on Sarracenias (this is all I know in life, LOL).   
  
 I stumbled upon the Sarracenia Forum, and it had been there for maybe 8 months  with 4 or 5 members.  It was totally dead and didn't seem to have a future.  There were about 6-7 other new CP forums, but all of them were pretty much dead.  I think 1 of those have survived.   
  
I told Jonathan Mejia (creator of the Sarracenia Forums) that I will help turn his empty home into a live, active site that will be the world's foremost resource for Sarracenia.  Of course, Jonathan seemed skeptical yet enthusiastic.  He was the right person for that job: he's such an easy going dude, and followed through with every last request that is agreed upon.  He listens to your suggestions but will also give you other solutions if he doesn't believe you have the best idea.   His work ethic is unstoppable!    
  
After getting a good feeling that Jonathan was the man to work with, I devoted countless hours on the forum.  I worked on it for years, uploading pictures, writing descriptions, clone by clone, and built the Sarracenia photo library from scratch (today, we have multiple authors, thanks to our members!).    
  
Others caught on and made it stronger.  Jonathan continued to improve the site and solicited feedback from the members on how to make it better. As we gained members, we recruited others to advertise on other forums, got friends to advertise on facebook, twitter, tumblr, instagram,  blogs, etc. and 4 years later, I think Jonathan is now starting to believe that the vision is coming to fruition.  Today,  people organically find the Forum via google searches and social media.    
  
  
As it stands, our members who visit every day and contribute their love and passion for Sarracenia are the ones who are holding the fort(you know who you are) and they are the reason we are so strong today.  I am grateful for you all!  It's not just me, my close Sarracenia friends, and Jonathan anymore.  
  
  
This is where we are today, March 11, 2015.   
  
To be continued......  
  
  
-Mike

I guess I should add to the story of how the forum was created, and how it has grown.  
  
**BOMBING THE SOUTH POLE OF THE MOON**  
  
The truth of the story is this:  
The forum would have never been created, had a US government organization filled with scientists and engineers, called NASA, not bombed the nearest celestial body, our moon.  
  
On October 9, 2009, NASA's low budget mission LCROSS, sent a small explosive to a crater on the South Pole of the lunar surface, and examined its plume to detect the state of hydrogen in that region. This was the first time NASA ever detected water on the moon.   
  
I had graduated college earlier that year, and with my B.S. in Biological Sciences at hand, I was ready to take on the world! I kept in touch with my science roots, and kept up with the latest news, and so the LCROSS mission was one I watched very closely.   
  
I was also very lonely, and seeking a community of people with which to fit in. I joined the Manhattan Orchid Society, as orchids were near the top of the list of plants I loved to grow. However, I was in need of a bit more than that, and so I went online to look for like minded people. Specifically, I went to youtube, and one of my favorite youtubers: pogobat (a young man by the name of Dan Brown), was starting a tribe of people, the pogotribe!  
Here is the video where Dan announced the POGOTRIBE:  

  
  
The pogotribe were just fans of Dan Brown, and I was part of it, but even better, Dan Brown declared that the presence of water on the moon means that human life could potentially live there, and because it was not claimed by any person, he was going to claim the South Pole of the moon as the outpost of the pogotribe. And so he created the first forum I ever joined, in late 2009, SPOTM (South Pole Of The Moon).  
  
The moon grew and proliferated due to its ideals. Its values, of which it had 9. These were the things that linked us together as a community, and they were:  
  
1. Use our awesomeness for good  
2. Embrace our inner weird  
3. Have fun  
4. Think impossibly big   
5. Grow out  
6. Grow strong  
7. Stand by what we know to be true  
8. Accept that we don't know everything  
9. Welcome all who stand by these values

  
  
And so we became an online community, a tribe, and we attempted to reach level 5 status!  
  
We did community projects in our local areas, and as a group online. We tried to create youtube videos, and give to charity, and use our intelligence to decrease "world suck". We were a subset of a larger community called the Nerd Fighters. There is a VERY LONG story about this community but here is a summary:  

  
World Suck was an idea that the world does suck, but we as individuals can do small things to decrease the amount of suck in the world. The idea was started by John Green, the writer, and his brother Hank, the musician/scientist. They started a successful charity organization called the foundation to decrease world suck. How successful? In 2013 alone the foundation to decrease worldsuck has given over 350 thousand dollars in grant money to many charities that decrease the amount of suck in the world.  
[fightworldsuck.org/documents/](http://fightworldsuck.org/documents/)  
  
But lets get back to the nerdfighter subcommunity I was a part of, SPOTM. I was on SPOTM for a bit over a year, and at first the community was great, but then it got huge, and the trolls came in, and, unfortunately, it wasn't a pleasant place to be. I was mocked for the manner in which I chose to present myself on the boards. I left SPOTM in 2010.   
  
**PROBOARDS**  
I did love the SPOTM format though. I thought, how can I make that better? They were hosted on proboards, like our forum is. In early 2011, I was looking for another place to call home, another community. I wandered many other carnivorous plant communities, like terraforums, but they all lacked the sense of community and purpose that SPOTM had. I decided to create my own forum based on something very specific. What I considered to be the "orchid" of the carnivorous plant world, the genus Sarracenia.  
  
I thought long and hard about how to handle people who might be unpleasant on the forum. I wanted to have ideals like SPOTM, but the embrace everyones inner weird just wasn't going to work. I decided to have a zero tolerance policy on trolls. Trolls don't come on often, but when they do, their username, and IP address are banned. If ever I hear there was dishonesty in the sale or trade section, I decided this would also lead to a banning. These things were going through my mind when I posted the very first post on this forum which you can read here:  
[sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/1/forum-rules](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/1/forum-rules)  
  
The second post I ever made was rules for posting pictures:  
[sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/4/board-rules](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/4/board-rules)  
  
And the third post was rules for trading on the forum:  
[sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/7/rules](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/7/rules)  
  
Looking back now, I can see why people may have been hesitant to join The Sarracenia Forums.   
  
Funny story about the name. I actually made a conscious decision to call it THE Sarracenia Forum, instead of "A Sarracenia Forum" or just "Sarracenia Forum".  
  
The decision came about because:  
1. There was no other forum dedicated to ONLY Sarracenia online  
2. Since this was the only one, it was THE one  
3. I live in The Bronx. People never say they are going to The Manhattan, or The Queens, but they always say they are going to The Bronx. Even the Wikipedia page for The Bronx says its THE BRONX:  
[en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx](http://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx)  
  
Funny story about that, a family owned much of the land here, and their name was Broncks. So when people would visit them, they'd say they were going to visit The Broncks. The name stuck.  
  
So I decided to call the forum I was building on proboards, THE SARRACENIA FORUM.  
  
It wasn't until two days after I had laid down the rules, that I decided to give a welcome:  
[sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/11/welcome-sarracenia-forums](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/11/welcome-sarracenia-forums)

Mike asked for a section to post pictures of entire collections, of many sarracenia types. I thought about it for 10 seconds and knew I was going to call it Sarracenia Panmixia. In my bio classes in college I learned that panmixia was when every individual in a population had an equal opportunity to mate and breed with any other individual within the population. Many calculations to simulate evolution (by natural selection, sexual selection, bottlenecking) assume a panmixic group. I thought, what a better way to describe an assortment of many plants than the same word that describes the random mating of many individuals. Yeah, my mind is weird.  
panmixia- random mating within a breeding population  
  
On top of splitting the pics section into species, I have often gotten requests to split the species into subspecies. Due to many hardships in coding sub sub forums, and I also didn't want there to be too many folders - this has not, and most likely will not occur. Imagine I split rubra into all of its subspecies, and you had a plant that didn't fit any of the subspecies descriptions. Where would you put it? Same with var burkii/rosea? If I split purpurea into purp ven/purp purp/rosea, then there would be pictures of the same entity in two locations. Same problems arose for flava forms which look one way during certain parts of the year, but a different way a different part of the year.  So this is why the Pics section stays at the species level.

**  
PART 3- FORUM PROJECTS PROLIFERATE 2014**  
When last we left, the Sarracenia forum had gained many members due to the work of [meizzwang](/user/11) and others who helped create the posts and pictures necessary to use the forum as a resource for learning. This started in 2011. It took 2 years before the Sarracenia Forum took on any type of organizational project.  
  
**  
GLOBAL MODERATORS**  
  
The first project was getting the forum organized with mods. I created the role of Global moderator in late 2013/early 2014 and [greenthumb](/user/127) and [goodkoalie](/user/112) both rised to the occasion. The original posting was in May of 2013, and in January 2014 the positions were filled.  
[sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/695/looking-new-admin-mod](http://sarracenia.proboards.com/thread/695/looking-new-admin-mod)  
  
The responsabilities of the global moderators are as follows:  
As for responsibilities, they are simple:  
  
1. Moderate the forum by deleting spam posts to thread, or shoutbox. This also includes banning members if need be.  
  
2. Help Members if they need it (Everyone will see you as a Global Mod, so just answer questions, or help if anyone approaches you).   
  
3. **** Update the Calendar ******  
This is probably the biggest hassle and most annoying part of the position. If ICPS or other plant societies have events, they should be added. This also includes events from Cali Carnivores, and other vendors, or any events where the vendors may be selling plants (even if the event isn't exclusive to carnivores!).  
  
4. This is less of a hassle, and I usually do it, but if you find a thread is very interesting, or that it's discussion is growing fast. Or if a new member is adding a lot of awesome content, update it on the news feed! I usually update it once a month, but sometimes I am late (update news after it is "news"). If you can keep on top of it, it would be cool, but you don't need to. Actually, there are many times when nothing at all is happening.   
  
5. Come up with ideas to update and improve the forum! I am open to all ideas, and this will take place in the form of discussion between us.   
  
  
The mods took this last one to heart. I received a message from David soon after, sending me this in early 2014:  
  
I had some Ideas for the forums:  
•Plant Identification section  
•I was thinking about if we would want to a 'Forum Auction' to raise money to help benefit the forums? ( if there are any upgrades that cost money that would be useful.)  
•I think it would be a great idea to have a CP art section in the forums too  
tell me if you have any opinions on these Ideas  
Thanks!  
-David

  
The Plant Identification section now lives on in the Plant Pictures section.  
  
The first forum auction took place in 2015,

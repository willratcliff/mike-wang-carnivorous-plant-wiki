Thanks for the suggestion, Paul!  For individuals that stick out and are on the level of cultivar status, it makes sense to give them a unique name.    The challenge is,  what do you do if you have more than 100 individuals from one population (this is the case with some populations in the collection!).  Perhaps the best thing is to do is have a code and then a spreadsheet to decode it.  for example, say you have 5 different populations of S. flava rugelii, and more than 100 individuals.  you could name them  
SFRUG1, SFRUG2, SFRUG3...SRUG100.  For flava ornatas, you can do the same thing, SFO1, SFO2, SF03...SF0100.  For flava atropurps, SFA1, SFA2, etc.  S. leuophylla, SL1, SL2.     
  
Moving forward, for the un-named plants in the collection, I'm going to do this.  Only issue is, it's probably best to re-name EVERYTHING in the collection to be consistent, which is a crazy task.  Can you imagine changing the tags on 1000's of plants?.  The name change can confuse people too because of all the material currently in circulation. I've been hesitant to make any changes because of the confusion that may arise, but I think it has to happen.

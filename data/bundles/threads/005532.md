Here's an extremely rare genotype of flava rugelii from a relic population of S. flava var. rugelii that is just barely hanging in there in situ!  Well,   I have been told that while there aren't many plants in situ, they are relatively in good shape and being decently managed, so they're not expected to disappear anytime soon.  There are also apparently some really cool looking catesbaeis there too!  
  
  
I've had these in the collection forever and they have been crammed in a community tray for as long as I can remember, so they haven't really ever looked all that great.  This past winter, I divided the whole darn thing and made some divisions as well, so now that they have fresh soil and space to spread their roots, they're at least photograph-able!

So those previous pics were the divisions that are being distributed, here are the mother plants!  Pics taken 6/14/22:  

![](../../images/fd/fd92ab5f113610097970c979ed0aa6d713bc925ec82a66f27c51822912cbdb01.jpg)

  
  
From a genetics standpoint, these are very closely related to the Bulloch Co, GA population.  If they weren't labeled, I probably couldn't tell a rugelii from bulloch apart from a rugelii from Evans Co, GA.   Given their similarity, I may cross the two populations together to increase heterozygosity and increase chances of future survival:

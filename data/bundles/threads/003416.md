With regards to the name of this clone, most seasoned Sarracenia nerds would say (with or without the California accent/diction), "uh dude Mike, isn't that just a S. leucophylla var. alba maaaaan?"   Well, this plant has veins in the interior of the trap and it is yet to be proven whether or not the interior can become solid white, so for now we'll stay away from using the fancy terminology and just call it a regular leucophylla.      However, the  interior of the trap plus the lips looks whiter than the exterior of the trap, and that helps distinguish this clone from others...for now, LOL  Who knows if this is just another chameleon leucophylla.  Last year it produced much brighter white pigments in the interior; this year it's less obvious but still noticable in a couple of the photos below.  
  
Another key characteristic of this clone is that the traps are relatively GIGANTIC and pretty tall considering how small the rhizome is!  It's currently around 2" long.  Is this a giant clone?  No idea, we'll have to see later on.

It is well known and fully documented that Sarracenias, both in cultivation and in the wild can produce anomalous flowers during the fall due to heat stress.  However, here's a new ***HYPOTHESIS:*** they can also produce anomalous flowers during late summer/early fall from COLD STRESS!  In my microclimate, the hottest it has been all summer long is 83F, far too cold to be the cause of heat stress.  In contrast, it's been so cold at night all summer long that you can see steam from your breath! My day temperatures average in the low 70's, while nights dip down to the low to mid 50's.  Long term exposure to these conditions are, what I believe, causing some clones to flower prematurely.  It  makes sense: extreme heat can overcome  the inhibiting compounds that prevent flowering till spring, so why can't sufficient exposure to cool summer temperatures do the same thing?  Afterall, that's what the winter dormancy does!  
  
I had two anomalous flowers this year, one was S. minor var. okefenokeensis clone C Ware Co, GA, and S. flava anthocyanin free Brunswick Co, NC.  Pics taken 8/15/21:

All of the species can  display this anomalous fall flowering, which further supports the hypothesis  (or maybe even theory at this point) that fall flowering is induced by either long periods of excessive cold or long periods of excessive heat during the grow season.   For sure, flava is the absolute most susceptible to fall flowering, at least under the conditions here in Northern California.  While some clones are more susceptible than others, there are different clones flowering year after year.  
  
Rubras have been the most reluctant to produce anomalous flowers, so far!  I have a pic of S. jonesii flowering in mid July but can't find it.  We'll eventually have all of the species documented in this thread, just need more time.     Pics taken 8/30/22:

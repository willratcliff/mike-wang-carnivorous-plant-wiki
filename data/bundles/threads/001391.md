Here's an interesting clone of S. leucophylla var. alba that has more white than your average, every-day S. leucophylla.  With the younger summer traps, there are veins on the interior of the trap, but during the fall, it can become completely white.  Another interesting facet of this clone is the amount of white on the trap.  Normally, especially during the spring and summer, the white pigment doesn't go below the mouth too much.  The amount of white seen on the summer traps on this clone is about the same as the amount of white you'd normally see on a fall pitcher of a "regular" clone.  Can't wait to see if the fall traps on this plant produce some extreme white pitchers!    
  
Last year, this plant dried up (I didn't water the plant enough and the next day, there was a heat wave) and also fried in a heat wave.  As if there wasn't enough damage already,  the wind threw a couple of punches at it and kicked it a couple of times, causing the amazing yet beat up trap to toppled over.  Long story short: even though the trap showed some outstanding characteristics, I didn't end up photographing it.  In fact, many of my outstanding S. leucophylla clones were burnt last year, but hopefully, I'll be able to photograph and share them with you all this year.  
  
All the baby'ing afterwards paid off, as this one is producing some neat looking traps.  photos were taken 5/31/14.

Leucophyllas always make me nervous about this time of the year...will they produce fall traps, or are they already going dormant?  It seems when we consistently have warmer weather, all the plants produce a strong set of fall traps, but if we get cold snaps and lots of cloudy days in the late summer/early fall, it triggers the plants to go dormant early.  So far, it seems like  one or two of the hurricane creek white divisions produced fall traps about 2 months early, but the rest of the plants are only producing either nominal summer traps or phyllodia.  S. leucophylla var. alba MKL042, one of my favorite bright white clones, seems like it has already gone dormant!  fingers crossed it's still active because it didn't produce dit-a-lee squat this year...  
  
So what's S. leucophylla var. alba Baldwin Co, AL clone A doing?  It recently produced 2  impressive pitchers (although it looks more amazing in person than in the photo).  Are these the fall pitchers, and that's it, or will there be an even more impressive fall show?  Well, the next set of leaves seems to be phyllodia, but that doesn't mean there's a chance for an even more impressive show.

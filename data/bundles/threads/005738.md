You don't see many giant purpurea ssp. purpureas in cultivation.  Some may have never seen a giant one before!  well, if you're reading this thread and haven't seen one before, let's change that!  Here's S. purpurea ssp. purpurea GIANT Caroline Co, VA (left) compared to a large, mature marl bog form on the right:  

![](../../images/26/2679e38bd05a39f5a58cb581b8e058f726acfdf985a750798534b90f524f0bb6.jpg)

  
  
The caroline Co, VA clone is super rot prone and while I technically still have one division left in the collection, it is hanging on by a thread and could rot at any moment!  So what do you get when you cross a giant, but hard to grow purpurea with the most resilient purpurea ssp. purpurea in the collection?  Hopefully, they're some gigantic purp. purps that are grower friendly!  
  
So far, these seedlings are growing at BALLER speed and are overall very easy going.  Most of the plants look exactly like the Caroline Co, VA plant, shape-wise!  This is a super interesting cross and I'll keep you all posted as things progress.  
  
Pics taken 9/15/22;  

![](../../images/c8/c8dc913d8a3ec5e87dcf8ce5b998d66f5988fdff45367612fedbb77a86511e99.jpg)

  
  
This tray has already gone through one round of selective pressure, but I'm still going to cull the smaller ones to make sure that everything that gets distributed is in fact a giant and not some regular, boring sized form

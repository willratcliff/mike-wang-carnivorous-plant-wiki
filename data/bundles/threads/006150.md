A long time ago, I acquired some wild collected seeds that originated from the NASC, these are genetics that otherwise would have never entered cultivation and I'm really grateful for the opportunity as it now is a chance for the general public to acquire these sort of plants.  While that was seemingly the easy part, growing them out, keeping them alive for many years and then finding the winners is actually a lot harder than it sounds.  Damping off at the seedling stage, raccoons destroying hundreds of plants in a single night, pests and diseases taking over the seedlings is just a glimmer of what an uphill battle it is to get to the stage of actually landing a winner.   I hope new growers start to get an idea of the serious amount of time it takes to "find" these plants via pheno hunts after years of evaluations.   What most growers omit is that in many cases, you spend years growing out the seeds and you don't find anything worthy...that comes with the territory.   
  
This past winter, the leucophylla population from Escambia Co, FL had been neglected for many years as I had so many other projects going on.  There are hundreds of populations in the collection that are just waiting to get spaced out and given room for the plants to grow to their fullest potential!  I'm currently maxed out on space, so anything that is given excellent treatment has to be special.  I guessed there were some special ones in this batch so they got divided and given space to thrive.  Prior to this, the plants  were way overgrown and nearly choking themselves out!   they got divided, placed into fresh new soil and BAM!  A few days ago I noticed this very special looking one!  Dang it's heavily veined, have you ever seen a leucophylla with so many veins like this one?  I haven't.     
  
Anyways, long story short, here's S. leucophylla 'fiber optic' Escambia Co, FL, pics taken 9/18/25:

I tried google searching for this cultivar and I couldn't find any pictures of it except from the cultivar description:http://legacy.carnivorousplants.org/cpn/Species/v29n2p46_49.html  What's even more interesting is the photo from the cultivar description doesn't show the plant at its fullest color potential!  
  
This is a cultivar from way back in the day, long before many here knew what a pitcher plant was!  I remember seeing it in person in 1996 when I first visited California Carnivores for the very first time, and it was one of those "impossible to acquire" cultivars.  Fast forward the clock 22 years later, and it's still not very common and quite a rarity in collections.  Only difference is it's now easily obtainable.  
  
So what's going on, why is this hidden gem cultivar kept such a tight lipped secret?  I have no idea, although you can blame me for the past few years that I've had it but not taken a single picture of it.  But what about the other 15-30 years or so that it's been around, why hasn't anyone caught on that this clone is pretty dang amazing?  Did California Carnivores only release it to one person in the last several decades and it never caught momentum in the Sarracenia world?  For sure, I think people were skepticle about this plant if they hadn't seen it in person: there's crickets chirping online when it comes to this plant.  
  
What  I do know is that the color on this plant is outstanding!  Another great feature is the long lasting pitchers: most Sarracenia in the collection are brown this time of year, but S. Lamentations is still in really nice shape!  I see this plant as the stepping stone for more amazing cultivars to come in the future!

These sort of chameleon clones that are difficult to color up are some outstanding genetics to use for breeding.  They do require the right environment to coax out that super all white trap, and I haven't gotten there yet with this clone.  However, these chameleon clones hold the key to super white, jaw dropping albas, but it will take a lot of trial and error as we as large numbers to find that gem that colors up right away under various conditions.  The genes are there, but you need the right combo for them to show up easily.  
  
Utilizing unique genetics for breeding is the way  to create unique and never seen before hybrids!  You can continue to breed and recombine Adrian slack, Waccamaw, leah wilkerson, etc. with various different clones, but if you want to get something that's different or you want to take breeding to a different level, this clone, as well as many other chameleon clones, is the route to take!  
  
  
Now that I talked your ear off about breeding, what characteristics about this clone in particular make it stand out?  The obvious one is the solid white throat (alba) characteristics, but more importantly is what you don't see.  This clone can get very very white with the right environmental conditions.  Another unique characteristic is that the greenish petiole below the white turns yellowish as the trap ages.  As these traps get older, I hope to snap some pics of it once they change color.

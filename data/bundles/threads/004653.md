Generally speaking, S. leucophylla var. alba (leucophyllas with solid white mouths) is notorious for being difficult to color up in cultivation. S. leucophylla var. alba 'cotton Mouth' Baldwin Co, AL  is what I call a classic chameleon clone: Normally, it looks like a regular leucophylla and doesn't have that solid white look to it. Under optimal conditions, this clone has become ridiculously white(not shown), arguably whiter than hurricane creek white, but what you see below is how it typically performs for me during a "good year": it doesn't color up very easily. It has great vigor, and the white interior is very dominant, but the exterior just doesn't put on a solid white show very easily.  
  
If you're looking to breed the next generation of really white leucophyllas, this is the type of clone you want to use! It's very counter intuitive, but when you breed these plants, you have to think about recessive genes (hidden) as much as what you see with your eyes!  Pics mostly taken 9/28/19:

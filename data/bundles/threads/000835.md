Very little history is known about the origins of S. leucophylla Helmut's Delight.  The plant was imported into Europe from Australia perhaps 30 years ago.  It is named after  Helmut Kibellis, a German guy who lives in Australia.  It was purchased by Mr. Kibellis at an Australian supermarket (keep in mind, things were different 30+ years ago, and it wasn't uncommon to find wild collected Sarracenia for sale in supermarkets).  It looks rather unique, and likely originates from a population in the wild that no longer exists today.   
  
  
FINALLY!  This famous and very popular clone  is practically a weed in Europe, but remains a rare gem here in the states.  I'm not aware of other growers outside of California  and within the US who have it.  After years and years of lusting over this clone, this plant has finally been imported, and is now here to stay.  So far, it seems like a decently fast grower, and the white pigments travel further down the pitcher than a lot of the other "regular" leucos out there.  White pigment production is highly environmentally induced, but there is also a strong genetic component to it.    
  
  
  
Another neat characteristic of this plant is the intense netting in the interior and exterior of the pitcher.  I can't say I've experienced this plant to it's fullest potential yet, but so far, it's definitely living up to my expectations.  Perhaps it may produce some fall pitchers in the next month or so.

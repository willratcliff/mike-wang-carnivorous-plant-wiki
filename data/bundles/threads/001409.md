Perhaps I'm guilty of creating the hype around these extreme red throated flavas, but hey, aren't they something else?  Slightly off topic, but this solid red throat characteristic isn't limited to species; I've seen some new hybrids in the pipeline grown by Rob Co and bred by Dr. Travis Wyman that have this exact characteristic, and let me tell you, they're stunning!  Dr. Wyman is probably 2-3 steps ahead of humanity when it comes to breeding....     
  
Anyhow, many of the S. flava var. rubricorpora clones in circulation, especially from Liberty County, FL, have a standard "red blotch" or "cut throat" but only a tiny amount of them have this solid red throat.  I know in Europe, there's an ornata from Liberty County that has a really neat solid red throat, but I'm not sure there's any other rubricorpora out there that has both the solid red throat and red body/yellow top.  Anyone else seen one?  
  
Well, this plant is still in the works, but it's already starting to show off its unique traits.  I think I have maybe one or two more rubricorpora plants that are similar, but they're not worth photographing yet.

Year after year, S. flava var. rubricorpora 'solid red throat' Liberty Co, FL easily colors up, even after transplanting or even if we have somewhat crappy weather.  HOWEVER, chop off all the winter leaves, and the red colors go away!  
  
I can't remember the thread, but I'm going to look for it-we were discussing the effects of chopping off leaves on flava var. rubricorpora.  Bogman, who grows his plants on the east coast, cuts off all the leaves to the ground during the dormancy period and in the spring, his plants come up solid red bodied!  This I don't doubt!  So why do my rubricorporas consistently turn greenish whenever the winter leaves are chopped off?  
  
I don't know, but the only difference I can think of is that on the east coast, the leaves during the winter dormancy might die to the ground after a deep freeze...the dormant leaves seem to senesce quicker than the winter leaves here, which remain even after new spring traps are formed.

Looks like a very nice clone.  I do wonder, however, about the "Red" designation with Jonesii.  I have two different clones, one from Meadowview, and the other from a private property location in Henderson County, North Carolina.  Both plants if shaded some, or we have a rainy summer, will be pure green.  Give them lots of sun, however, and they are nice and red.  As fall approaches they both turn deep maroon as the weather cools.  We definitely have much sunnier summers on the West Coast (especially this year!) than the Southern Appalachian mountains of that area.

Quite a valid suspicion, and I'm glad you brought that up Jeff!  Many of the solid black alatas do the same thing:they're completely green, even in full sun, until the fall when they color up.  
  
With this particular variant of jonesii, on the other hand, it's red when grown in full sun throughout the entire grow season.    Most other jonesiis may color up in the fall, but I still consider those "regular" jonesii's.  All of the plants here are grown in full blasting sunlight from sun up till sundown  
  
  
S. jonesii 'RED' Greenville Co, SC on the left, a "regular" S. jonesii to the right.  Both are in as much sunlight as they can possibly get.  The regular one produces greenish pitchers with a little bit of red pigment during the summer and may color up in the fall.  I don't call those "red" variants:

It was the late 1990's, and I was chillin in my brother's room with some other friends, we were hanging out.  He had his door open and you could see my crops in the distance from his room.  Out of nowhere, this caucasian dude with a hat worn sideways (he looked very gangsta) waltzed on in seemingly uninvited and greeted my brother and others.  He then inquired, "Hey, who's JONES is this?"    
  
I was immediately shocked and confused.  How does this guy know anything about Sarracenia jonesii, let alone, when did the word "jonesii" get remixed into "jones?"  After hearing the response from another dude, it quickly dawned upon me that Jones is slang for home (sigh)....yes, this dude just wanted to know who's house he was visiting.  He was a friend of my brother's friend, and none of us knew him.  
  
Okay, now that I have that very random historical factoid covered, here's some recent photos of S. jonesii 'RED' Greenville Co, SC.  Photos taken 4/23/18.  Yes, the flowers smell just as good as they look, like sweet raspberries:

Here's a S. minor var. okefenokeensis  that's flowering in August and displaying an extra whorl of sepals.  Technically, this extra set would be called tepals.  
  
Is this trait stable?  Most likely not-this exact clone has flowered several times, and I haven't seen this happen before.  However, it's still possible that this exact piece of rhizome had a point mutation (meaning, the mutation was asexually induced) and it's possible that the mutation is genetically permanent-I will keep a close eye on this plant.

couple more pics.  I think what's very important to explain here is phenotypic plasticity: clones that produce very bulbous traps at maturity can look quite boring when they are juveniles.  Case and point: check out the most bulbous rosea clone I have in the collection, I didn't even know it was special for many years because it looked like this:  

![](../../images/2f/2fe6ad580f9da834f7214b49be210a1916e1ef0c1498f6f920c7b0ce454c88df.jpg)

   
  
Pretty amazing, right?  Nope!  BUT.....here's the EXACT same clone at vegetative maturity, straight up holy grail level of bulbousness!  

![](../../images/4a/4ac7a972bcb71fcff1208e5e1de8427bb840ca6e7e318b7a81ef04dc194e6bd9.jpg)

  
  
Same thing happens with montana ladies and gentlemen!  These montana (best clone x henderson Co, NC) seedlings aren't yet bulbous, but both parents are and most of these are expected to have some incredible, round shapes as they mature.  That said, they're still pretty darn beautiful even as seedlings:

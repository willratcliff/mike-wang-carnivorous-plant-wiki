Here's some of the rare and endangered Sarracenia species you don't see very often.  As we gain more knowledge and years of experience growing these plants, it turns out S. alabamensis isn't as difficult as once thought.  Grow them like S. leucophylla or S. oreophila: keep the soil moist at all times, but don't let them sit in water.  If you have to sit them in water, make sure it's very shallow, otherwise the roots will suffocate and rot quickly.  
  
Unlike its very close cousin, S. rubra ssp. wherryi, S. alabamensis can produce some enormous-sized traps.  Unfortunately, I didn't take my own advice above (dropped the ball when it started raining) and the plant that produced the biggest trap last year rotted out.  It doesn't help that space is limited and these plants are all crunched up together, but they're still thriving despite all the challenges.  
  
  
S. alabamensis Autauga Co, AL, photos taken 9/7/15.  Check out how yellow they get!  As stated above, these plants can produce huge traps (2+ feet tall), but they're not in optimal conditions right now:

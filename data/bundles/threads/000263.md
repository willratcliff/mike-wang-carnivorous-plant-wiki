So here's the mysterious S. flava var. ornata 'black veins' from Bulloch Co, GA.  Finally, the veins are turning black on the lid, and it's taken a while!  It seems as though the pitchers really had to mature for a good 2 months before the dark colors set in.  The veins on the body can turn black as well, but since I have them all packed close together, the body is not getting as much direct sunlight and is therefore, not turning black.   
  
The veins under the lid are also quite detailed, extensive and noteable.  Other ornata clones do this as well, and are equally interesting in the same way.   
  
This Bulloch Co, GA plant is picky and a pain to grow, but even with red veins, it's a  unique plant in the sense that the veins are very crisp and clear.  Other clones tend to have red pigments between the veins, which lowers the contrast.  The overall yellow color is also noteworthy.    
  
Anyhow, enjoy the photos-there are a couple of different clones, but it takes a very trained eye to see differences:

That particular clone originally came from Art Junier, and he was a computer/math nerd.  Seriously, there was more to the tag than that: it said something like "XP00014560."  Maybe that's the Expiration date for the plant?!    
  
I can't see how this additional numerical code has any value to the plant, so I don't include it.  Since Art is not likely on this earth anymore (we think he might have passed away), there isn't anyone who can decode it, so I only kept his original clonal information.  Long story short, CH05Y means it came from Art Junier, and that's the clonal name for that unique individual.

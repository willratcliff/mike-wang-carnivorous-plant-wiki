Some really bright white S. leucophylla plants grow in Eastern Alabama, but for the most part, the population there consists of "regular" leucophyllas.  This site is rather extensive, and is maintained by regular burning.  There are also a lot of moorei hybrids and back-crosses as well, which will probably be part of another post.    
  
When we visited, I think we got there too early because we saw that many of the S. leucophylla plants had just started pitchering up.  Earlier in the year, there was an extreme cold snap and perhaps the plants this year were delayed a little bit, but who knows.    
  
On a slight tangent, this site is home to the western most location of S. flavas that I know of, but there could very well be other populations further west than here.  Historically, S. flava occurred as far west as Mobile Co, AL, but those populations are now extinct.  In fact, Mobile Co used to be LOADED with Sarracenias, but today, I'll give you a dollar if you can find a plant there!  Almost everything there is completely destroyed.

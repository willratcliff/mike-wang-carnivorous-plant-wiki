Here's the famous S. leucophylla 'Schnell's ghost.'  While I'm not sure exactly where it's from, I think it's either from Mobile Co, or Washington Co, AL, just based on the looks of it.  This may be one of those S. (lecuophylla x alata) x leucophylla backcrosses: the key feature to this plant is that it has yellow flowers but is not anthocyanin free.  It's also a very white clone, arguably an S. leucophylla var. alba.

HI Steve,  
  
Thanks for the compliment, and you bring up an absolutely great question!  
  
If the description for S. leucophylla var. alba does not allow for plants that sometimes exhibit green veins, then I think recent horticultural findings were not taken into consideration.  S. leucophylla 'Hurricane Creek White' is undoubtedly a S. leucophylla var. alba, yet it can have green veination in the interior of the traps depending on environmental conditions.  On the other hand, it can also have a solid white interior as well, again, depending on environmental conditions.  
  
Case and point: the two pictures below are of the same exact clone, S. leucophylla Hurricane Creek White clone E.  First picture shows a trap with green veins in the interior:

When growing S. leucophylla in general, it seems you have to sift through piles and piles of junk before you find that one or two gems in the haystack.    Once you get that gem, then it's a matter of getting it to perform and then propagating it so that others can enjoy it as well.   This is sometimes  easy because the weather, genetics and conditions cooperate. Othertimes, it's not so easy.   
  
S. leucophylla 'tall, white petiole dominant' Okaloosa Co, FL has been moderately easy to propagate, but what I'm having a hard time doing is getting it to color up like it did when it was first identified as a winner!  The traps are ridiculously white but not ever solid white like you'd see in an alba.   Out of countless S. leucophylla plants that took years to grow to a decent size, this individual was one that stood out of the crowd for having an unusually white petiole.  Thing is, that was only when the traps were bigger.  
  
Ironically, I'm starting to like its "chameleon look" better than when it's very white!  It's much easier to get it to look the way you see the plants below than it is to get it very white! This clone gives the cultivator both pleasure to grow even if you can't grow it for Jesus, yet a challenge for experienced growers who want to get it to color up fully.  
  
It's still too early to say that it won't get very white, the fall traps haven't yet formed!  These are late summer traps on divisions.  In fact, the divisions look better than my mother plant!  Photos taken 8/18/18:

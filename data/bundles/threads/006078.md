The first time I saw pictures of Utricularia blanchetii (different species), there was an immediate impulse to acquire it...you know that feeling when you know you're hooked, and fortunately, it's not on drugs, gambling, or (insert vice) but instead something that heals the mind and soul!  So what does this have to do with Utricularia parthenopip-squeek?  Yeah, I'm pretty sure nobody else calls it that....Ahem, well, the first acquisition of blanchetii looked super dubious upon arrival: the plant barely looked alive the pot was overtaken by thick moss.  Even looking under the soil on the side of the pot, there wasn't any of the desired plant "running" to the sides of the pot as you'd see when they're extremely happy.  More on blanchetii when I write a thread on it.    Long story short, it was super disappointing, so I ended up ordering another blanchetii from a different vendor.  The second pot arrived looking Okay: it wasn't super healthy, but it wasn't overtaken by moss and unlike the first purchase, there was more than one or two visible leaves (they're pretty microscopic on this species).    
  
Almost 2 years goes by after acquiring the second pot, I finally somehow figured out how to salvage the first one and it was thriving!  The second pot had also started to look well and put up flower spikes!  YESSS!!!  Maybe I have two different clones of blanchetii, right?  WRONG!  I ended up getting parthenopipes, and this was actually a pleasant surprise because I was also after that species!  
  
Here's U. parthenopipes about 2 years after I acquired it.   Check out those flowers, they bloom for quite some time and can form so many flowers even ina  small 2" pot!  

![](../../images/ae/aed091c8c92da8e96acac716bf85901ab5905240680c894c9ae559a65b819957.jpg)

  
  

![](../../images/7c/7c05e944680cce38786e30d51a435d5c1955b30e6512ab176f19e2d805b2256c.jpg)

  
  
  
The hardest part about growing this species is getting the Utricularia to outgrow the moss.  It took nearly two long years of babying and selecting out vigorous plugs to finally get it clean and growing strong like this!  IF you start with weak plants, it's a huge uphill battle to get these things going:

I think I finally found a thread that covers some of the issue: [www.cpukforum.com/forum/index.php?/topic/5629-ut-parthenopipes-vs-ut-blanchetii-white-flower/](https://www.cpukforum.com/forum/index.php?/topic/5629-ut-parthenopipes-vs-ut-blanchetii-white-flower/)  
End conclusion seems to be that the white plants do represent what was described as parthenopipes...but that it may be a synonym with blanchetii due to high variation in both and many intermediate forms in the wild.  
Also means that this white plant is probably what I lost last year. I just managed to get purple blanchetii to reestablish at least.

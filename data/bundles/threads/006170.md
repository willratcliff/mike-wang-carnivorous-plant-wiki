For the longest time, I've seen these flowers in bloom just minutes away from where I grew up in the San Francisco Bay Area, California.  In fact, my family used to own property where this plant grew, but that property unfortunately was destroyed and turned into a home.  Fortunately, I collected seeds from the entire population and have secured these genetics in cultivation!  I've also planted them throughout my property in Northern California so hopefully these genetics will persist in the long haul.  
  
Here are a few things that I learned about how to grow this species:  
1) Seeds need light to germinate, don't bury them!  
2) Plants are photo-sensitive, they need long days (short nights) to grow vegetatively, otherwise they will go dormant, even if they're tiny seedlings!  In other words, no forcing them under lights with long photoperiods (you probably want to do 9-11 hour photoperiods indoors).  
3) about 40% sand and 60% potting soil is a great mix for this species  
4) full sun outdoors is ideal, but do not let the soil get too hot.  IF it's an unusually warm day, place the plants in shade until the weather cools down again.  
5) The first year, you can crowd the plants, but if you don't separate them the second year, they will struggle.  
6) for me, the hardest part of keeping these alive is during the summer dormancy period!  Too dry and the fleshy storage roots will die.  Too wet and they'll rot.  You want the pot to dry out all the way after they are done growing, keep the pot in a cool location that has zero direct sunlight, if possible.  If the soil gets too dry, add water but only enough to get the top layer of soil wet, the rest underneath should remain dry if possible.  Repeat every once in a while (it's art, can't give you specifics because that depends on your conditions).  
7) Feed regularly with a fertilizer low in nitrogen and high in P and K during the winter growing season.  Use maybe half strength.    
8) these can probably tolerate light frost, haven't experimented with that just yet.  
9) I sowed the seeds 11/4/24.  You can probably sow them earlier if it gets cooler sooner, depends on the weather.  They need cold but above freezing temps to germinate.

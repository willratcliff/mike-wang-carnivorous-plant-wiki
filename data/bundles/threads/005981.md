Many, many years ago, back when there were still quite a few fields of leucophyllas everywhere, old school Sarracenia enthusiasts stumbled upon what was likely either the only or one of the very few anthocyanin free individuals of leucophylla in the wild!  What makes this variant special is the fact that it has zero anthocyanin production and it produces yellow flowers!  What's so special about an anthocyanin free plant when there are alba clones that also produce yellow flowers?  Well, those albas aren't anthocyanin free: they can produce red coloration!  Those yellow flowers are likely the result of introgression with S. alata and subsequent back crossing with leucophylla to the point that many generations later, the plants look pure but still retain the yellow genes for flower petals from their alata ancestor(s).  
  
In case you're having a hard time following this in depth, nerd talk, let's dumb it down a bit cuz even I'm having a hard time following my own thoughts!  Anthocyanin free plants simply do not produce red coloration ever anywhere on their "body."    
  
These almost look like hurricane creek white, but they're definitely not and they don't get quite as white.  are they considered albas?  I don't know, but wouldn't it be ridiculous to call this S. leucophylla f. viridescens var. alba Baldwin Co, AL?  My mind almost ran out of breath thinking that one through!

Several years ago, I was talking to my buddy Matt Miller of Flytrapcare.com and we were discussing Cephalotus.  He told me that if I wanted to grow this species, without a doubt, I had to get Cephalotus follicularis 'western venom.'  Why?  He said it was the absolute fastest growing clone he had ever tried! At this point, the rabbit hole had been dug, now it was time to go down it!  I had previously never heard of this clone and was determined to find it somewhere!  
  
As the typical cephalotus story goes, I looked and looked and looked....nobody had one for sale!  I went to the local corner store, tapped on everyone's shoulder (metaphorically of course), seeing if they had even heard of the clone....nothing but crickets and deer staring into headlights.  Was this another "dangit!!!" situation bruh?    
  
Lots of time had passed and seemingly out of nowhere, I eventually found it for sale.  It was offered by the original source who named the clone (Ting K, a talented grower in Northern California).  I think I ended up getting some of the very last ones that he offered because I haven't seen him sell this clone for a few years.  Anyways, when they arrived, I immediately called up Matt and told him  the grower had sent me 3 plants, two of which had several growth points!!!!  They were clearly freshly dug up and potted, the tubers were a nice size but the growth on top didn't look too well.    He responded "yuuup, that's exactly how mine were sent!"   I promised Matt I'd hook him up if I ever got divisions off of these plants (he regretfully lost his clone).  
  
About 3 months after receiving them they started growing and this is what they looked like:  

![](../../images/22/22dcd5fea1bb03d91f89fdb533884fa2a382aea3c3d89d47b08f42eadccc32ae.jpg)

  
  
The first season I had them, they produced adult pitchers and filled the pots!  Matt wasn't kidding, they do grow fast as fudge!  I ended up dividing one of them and sending Matt a copy for him to give it a shot in his new greenhouse in Missouri.  
  
Fast forward today, I have 3 mother plants, all of which have multiple growth points!  Yes, of course, these will be divided and if all goes well, I will offer some probably spring 2025!  One really intriguing characteristic of this clone, at least in my climate, is that the traps are darker than most other regular forms.    
  
Here's the picture of the original mother plant, grown by and photographed by Ting K, the original source of Western Venom.  Almost unbelievable until you grow it yourself and see that it does have the potential to size up crazy!  Notice the coloration on his plant, the traps are relatively dark!

The name on the tag is slightly different, but I couldn't remember what it was, and it probably wouldn't fit in the subject description for this thread.  So what do we got here?!  This is the "stretchiest" leucophylla var. alba that I have in the collection: the white goes down the petiole pretty darn far, I measured 2" last year!  I believe this clone alone is the future of S. leucophylla breeding, and holds the genetics to unlock some incredible hybrids with way more white pigments that what we've seen so far.  Typically, when you cross these sort of plants with the "right" individuals, the white pigments in the offspring can stretch to more extremities than what's seen in either of the parents.  More on this topic later, I'm in the process of developing proof to back up this hypothesis, so it might be a while.  All of the evidence I've observed so far isn't provable yet.  I'm just here staking a claim in this discovery before it happens

Vigor is decent on this clone!  Definitely not a slow-poke!  Typical of the washington Co, AL genetics, this leucophylla is relatively easy to color up, it takes very unoptimal conditions to bring out the Chameleon effect (ie. look like a regular leucophylla under some environmental conditions).  However, those chameleon clones, once they perform, are really jaw dropping, so I wouldn't discount their value in breeding projects...especially since hybridizing with them can potentially unlock the alba in them!

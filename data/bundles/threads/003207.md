First off, the parents to this plant is S. leucophylla var. alba Hurricane creek white clone F Baldwin Co, AL crossed with S. leucophylla var. alba 'yellow flower' Washington Co, AL.    
  
What do you get when you cross the best original hurricane creek white clone with a difficult to grow but highly  desirable, yellow flowered super white alba variant from Washington Co, AL?  I dunnooooooo, something neat and bright white!  This "hybrid" cross between two genetically different S. leucophylla var. albas from 2 different populations looks like a mix between both parents, although it seems to be leaning more on the leucophylla var. alba MKL042 clone: it's tall and skinny.  The "head" has a slight hurricane creek white shape to it.    
  
In general, when you take 2 pure bred species lines (for example,  let's say a flava rugelii and a rosea) and cross them together, the hybrid offspring tend to look like a mix between both parents, and there's usually increased vigor compared to the parent plants.  HCW clone F is very vigorous, although MKL042 is rather slow growing.    Since the S. leucophylla var alba (HCW clone F x MKL042) offspring appear to be a mix between both parents and also appear to have decent vigor, this suggest both parents are pure inbred lines (ATTN breeders:this gives you serious insight in future breeding projects should you decide to work with either of these parents).    
  
  
I only had about 7 seedlings make it from this cross, and so far it seems there are 2 keepers.      The others might be nice, but I can't tell yet.   Anyhow, on to the photos!

Pokie22-done!  
  
Eric-yes, but it's waitlist only at the moment.  It'll be divided as soon as the plant goes dormant, which is in about 2 months or so.  
  
Bristol-with regards to the red pigments fading away and white going farther down the petiole, it doesn't seem to be related as much to the size of the trap as it does to the environmental conditions.  Plants that aren't transplanted for years on end, have good quality peat and receive good quality water, and haven't been stressed out for a long time tend to get solid white and have the white travel down the petiole the most.  With this clone in particular, I wouldn't be surprised if the red veins goes away or isn't produced on subsequent pitchers, but it's too early to tell because this is the first well grown trap from this clone.    
  
However, there also seems to be a strong genetic component with regards to far the white goes down the petiole.  Some clones  under less than ideal conditions have significantly more white on the petiole than the average leucophylla clone under optimal conditions.

Let's not forget, Bristol made this selection and the plant is named after him!  As far as I know, this clone is of great historic significance, as it's the very first successful "hybrid" leucophylla var. alba in cultivation (ie. very genetically different albas crossed together).  So far, it appears this alba can color up relatively easily under multiple different grow environments, has higher disease resistance, has incredible vigor, is highly sought after by both NERDZ© and newbies, and produces an abundance of traps during the fall once it forms a nice sized clump.  
  
This clone is significant because it's the very, very beginning of what I believe will be an ALBA CRAZE!  Once the alba form  colors up easily and it doesn't take an expert to keep it alive long term, I beleive there will be breeders that only focus on albas, and they'll strive to produce the whitest of whites.   I predict S. leucophylla var. alba 'Rod's Favorite' is  the "seed" by which such movement will take place.

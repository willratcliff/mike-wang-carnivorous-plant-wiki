Before you get a tad bit scared by the name (murder written backwards, from the movie The Shining), let me remind you all that while the name is catchy for this clone, it's not at all accurate, since murdering is, by definition, unlawful.  Last I checked, it is still legal for plants to catch insects in our backyards, although I'll have to double check what the rules are on the endangered insects LOL      
  
Nonsense aside, holy Ef a moley!  Check this baby out!  It looks very similar to a wilkerson's red rocket, except it originates from a land far, far away from the wilkerson's property!  This clone has quite a bit of white at the top of the trap, and the brilliant red color pops out even to the untrained eye (eg. my wife).    This is the first time it has produced amazing fall pitchers, probably because it was transplanted two winters ago and finally given some space after a good decade of being overcrowded in a community tray.  I had NO clue this gem was hiding in the community tray until now!      Pics taken 9/1/22, unless otherwise indicated,  these traps all just opened a few days ago:

My leucophyllas in general never look this great during the spring because it's normally too cold to form nice looking traps.  In other climates, spring traps can look quite impressive if the temperatures are warm while the first set of traps are developing.  This year, we had the hottest March on record, it got up to 92F and felt like summer for close to a week!  
  
I've never seen good looking spring traps like this on this clone before, but with the different weather, this plant can show up with some surprises!

So what's so special about S. montana from Jackson Co, NC?  Well, to start, these plants don't seem to color up as much compared to the other montanas I've seen in cultivation...instead, they tend to form this yellowish-green hue to them, much like S. flava!  However, some individuals from this population turn solid purple in full sun.  The shape is also quite unique: some traps are long and symmetrical (normally, montanas are squat and dense).  The diversity is huge, so it's hard to put a generalization to these plants.  
  
One last thing: I'm noticing some very large traps coming from small rhizomes, so these might be able to produce some pretty big pitchers once they're fully grown up.
